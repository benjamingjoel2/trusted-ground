## A membership is not a licence

Trade associations are membership bodies. Each sets its own entry rules. Some check members' finances or bind them to a code of conduct; others mainly connect businesses. A membership claim tells you something only when you know what that body requires, and when you have checked the claim with the body itself.

## The bodies you are most likely to see

| Body | Who it is for | What membership tells you |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **ATTA**, Adventure Travel Trade Association | Adventure travel businesses worldwide: operators, agents, tourism boards | The company is part of an international adventure travel trade network. It is a trade association, not a regulator or a financial protection scheme. |
| **IAATO**, International Association of Antarctica Tour Operators | Companies operating tourism in Antarctica | The operator has joined the body that promotes safe and environmentally responsible private-sector travel to Antarctica, and agreed to follow its procedures for operating there. |
| **ASTA**, American Society of Travel Advisors | Travel agencies and advisors, mainly in the United States | The company belongs to the US travel advisors' trade association and has agreed to its code of ethics. |
| **ABTA**, the UK travel association | Travel agents and tour operators selling in the UK | ABTA members must follow its Code of Conduct, and ABTA provides financial protection for many package holidays booked with members. Check what applies to your booking. |
| **ATOL** (a licence, not a membership) | Companies selling flight-inclusive trips in the UK | Issued by the UK Civil Aviation Authority. Protects flight-inclusive packages sold by ATOL holders if the company fails. |
| **National tourism boards and authorities** | Varies by country | Some only promote tourism; others license or register operators. See [licences](/guide/licences/). |

## How to check a claim

1. Find the body's own member directory or contact page. Use the body's website, not a link sent by the operator.
2. Search for the operator's exact legal name and trading name.
3. For ABTA and ATOL, note the membership or licence number and check it is valid for the company that takes your money.
4. If you cannot find the company, ask the body directly before you pay.
