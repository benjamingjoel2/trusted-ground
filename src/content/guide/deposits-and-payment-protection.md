## Deposits

There is no single legal standard for the size of a deposit on a tailor-made trip. Operators set their own terms, often because they must pay lodges, permits or flights in advance. What matters is that you know, in writing and before you pay:

- how much the deposit is, and what it secures (for example, specific lodges or permits);
- when the balance is due;
- what you get back if you cancel, and on which dates the cancellation charges change;
- what you get back if **the operator** cancels;
- whether your money is protected if the operator fails, and by which scheme.

## Protection by market

**European Union.** Under the Package Travel Directive (EU) 2015/2302, organisers established in a member state must provide security for the refund of all payments if they become insolvent (Article 17). If the organiser terminates the package, it must refund all payments without undue delay and in any event within 14 days (Article 12).

**United Kingdom.** The Package Travel and Linked Travel Arrangements Regulations 2018 give similar protection for packages sold in the UK. Flight-inclusive packages sold by UK companies are generally protected by **ATOL**. Paying by **credit card** for an item costing more than £100 and not more than £30,000 can give you a claim against the card issuer under section 75 of the Consumer Credit Act 1974.

**United States.** There is no federal scheme that protects package travel payments. Paying by credit card lets you dispute a charge with the card issuer under federal rules on billing errors; the FTC explains the process and its time limits. Some states, such as California, regulate sellers of travel.

**Brazil.** The Consumer Defence Code (Lei nº 8.078/1990) gives consumers rights against suppliers of services, including travel companies that sell in Brazil. Tourism businesses must also register with Cadastur (see [licences](/guide/licences/)).

**Booking direct with an operator abroad.** If you pay a company outside your own market, these home-market rules may not apply. Your protection then rests on how you pay, on any scheme in the operator's own country, and on your travel insurance.

## How to pay

- Prefer a **credit card** over a bank transfer. Transfers are very hard to recover.
- Pay the company named on your contract. Be wary of requests to pay a different company or a personal account.
- Keep the contract, the invoice, the terms and every payment confirmation.

These rules are summarised for orientation, not legal advice. Check the current text with the sources below, or ask a consumer adviser in your country.
