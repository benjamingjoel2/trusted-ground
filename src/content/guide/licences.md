## Start with the destination's rules

Whether a tour operator needs a licence depends on the country it operates in. Many countries run a register or licensing scheme through a national tourism authority or ministry. Examples:

- **Brazil.** Tourism service providers, including tour operators and travel agencies, must register with **Cadastur**, the official register kept by the Ministry of Tourism under the General Tourism Law (Lei nº 11.771/2008). The register can be searched online.
- **Kenya.** The **Tourism Regulatory Authority** regulates and licenses tourism enterprises, including tour operators.

These are examples, not a complete list. For any other country, look for the national tourism authority or ministry and check whether it publishes a register.

## Then check the selling market

Some markets regulate the company that *sells* the trip, wherever the trip takes place:

- **California** requires sellers of travel to register with the state Attorney General's Seller of Travel program. Some other US states have similar laws.
- **United Kingdom.** A company selling a package that includes a flight from the UK must generally hold an **ATOL** licence from the Civil Aviation Authority.

## How to check

1. Ask the operator for its licence or registration number and the name of the authority that issued it.
2. Look the number up yourself on the authority's own website. Do not rely on a logo or a screenshot sent by the company.
3. Check that the name on the register matches the company name on your contract and on the payment details.
4. If there is no public register, ask the authority directly, or ask the operator for a copy of the licence and its expiry date.

A licence shows the company is allowed to operate. It does not by itself show the company is financially sound or that your payment is protected. See [deposits and payment protection](/guide/deposits-and-payment-protection/).
