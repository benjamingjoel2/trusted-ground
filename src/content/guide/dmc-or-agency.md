## The short version

A **destination management company (DMC)** is a company based in the destination that designs and runs trips there: it books the lodges and hotels, employs or contracts the guides and drivers, and handles problems on the ground. ADMEI, the trade association for destination management, describes a DMC as a professional services company with local knowledge and resources that designs and delivers activities, tours, transport and logistics in its destination.

A **travel agency** sells and arranges travel. Many agencies are in the traveller's home country and buy the ground arrangements from a DMC in the destination. Some agencies are based in the destination themselves.

## Why the difference matters to you

- **Who is on the ground.** A DMC usually controls the guides, vehicles and suppliers directly. If something goes wrong mid-trip, it is often the company that fixes it.
- **Who your contract is with.** If you book through an agency, your contract may be with the agency, with the DMC, or with both. Ask which company is the organiser of your trip.
- **What legal protection applies.** In the EU and the UK, a company that puts together a package (for example, accommodation plus transport or tours) and sells it is an *organiser* under package travel law, with duties to the traveller. Those duties apply to organisers established in, or selling into, those markets. A DMC outside the EU or UK that sells to you directly may not be covered by those rules. See [deposits and payment protection](/guide/deposits-and-payment-protection/).

## Booking direct with a DMC

Booking direct means one fewer company between you and the people running your trip. It can also mean you lose protections that a home-market agency would give you. Neither is better in every case. Decide with the facts in front of you: who is the organiser, how your money is protected, and who answers the phone at 2 a.m. in the destination.
