## The operator's insurance

A professional operator should hold **public liability** (or general liability) insurance, and often **professional indemnity** insurance. These cover the operator's legal liability if it causes injury, loss or damage. They are not travel insurance for you.

Ask:

- Which insurer, what kind of policy, and until what date?
- Does it cover the activities on your trip (for example, trekking at altitude, diving, self-drive, boat transfers)?
- Do the guides, drivers and boat or vehicle providers carry their own required insurance?

A serious operator will answer in writing. Some will show a certificate of insurance.

## Your own travel insurance

Government travel advice in the UK and the US says to take out travel insurance before you travel. Read the policy, not only the summary:

- **Medical treatment and evacuation**, which can be very expensive in remote areas.
- **Cancellation and curtailment**, including what happens if the operator cancels or fails.
- **Activities**: many policies exclude high altitude, diving below a set depth, or other activities unless you add them.
- **Supplier failure**: some policies cover the insolvency of a travel company; many do not.

Buy cover when you pay your deposit, not just before you fly, so that cancellation cover starts early. See also the question [Do I need travel insurance if I book locally?](/questions/do-i-need-travel-insurance/).
