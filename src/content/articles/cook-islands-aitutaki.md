Most trips to the Cook Islands combine two very different islands: Rarotonga, a mountainous, jungle-covered island ringed by a lagoon and a single coast road, and Aitutaki, a short domestic flight north, where a reef encloses a wide turquoise lagoon. Both are small and easy to get around. What needs planning is the season, your cash and the fact that serious medical care means New Zealand.

## Rarotonga: the main island

Cook Islands Tourism describes Rarotonga as the "mainland" and hub of the country, about a three-hour direct flight from Auckland. The road around the island is 32 kilometres, and the tourism board says it takes around 45 minutes to drive the whole loop. Two buses circle the island, one clockwise and one anticlockwise, and will stop anywhere on the main road if you wave them down.

Highlights named by the tourism board include:

- **The Needle (Te Rua Manga):** a half-day hike from the north side through the island's jungle interior. You can continue on the Cross Island Trek to the south coast and Papua (Wigmore's) Waterfall. A guided walk is recommended.
- **Punanga Nui Market** in Avarua, every Saturday morning, for local food, crafts and music.
- **Snorkelling** in the lagoon, with the board pointing to the western and southern sides.
- **Island Night** shows, combining a buffet with traditional dance and drumming.

Sunday is considered a day of rest and worship, so expect quieter roads and some closures.

## Aitutaki: the lagoon

Aitutaki is about a 40-minute flight from Rarotonga, with multiple flights daily. Cook Islands Tourism describes a triangular reef enclosing a turquoise lagoon dotted with motu (islets) around a main island of roughly 16 square kilometres, home to 1,780 people at the 2021 census.

On the water, the options range from full-day lagoon tours with snorkelling and lunch to private charters, sunset cruises, kayaking, stand-up paddleboarding, diving and kitesurfing on the steady easterly trade winds. One Foot Island is a common stop: the tourism board describes its white sandbanks and a small post office where you can have your passport stamped. The tourism board says humpback whales migrate north into these South Pacific waters between July and October.

On land, the 30-minute climb up Maunga Pu, Aitutaki's highest point at 124 metres, gives views over the whole atoll. The tourism board also points visitors to the Cook Islands Christian Church in Arutanga, which it describes as the oldest church in the country.

## Entry and money

According to the UK Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office (FCDO), visitors can stay in the Cook Islands without a visa for up to 31 days. You need a ticket for onward or return travel, proof of accommodation, and a passport valid for at least six months after the date you plan to leave, with two blank pages. Entry rules are set by the Cook Islands authorities, so confirm them with the government's visitor information before you fly.

The currency is the New Zealand dollar, which Cook Islands Tourism says is supplemented by local notes and coins. The FCDO notes that the only ATMs in the country are on Rarotonga and Aitutaki, and that you must use cash on the other islands. Cards are widely accepted on Rarotonga and at some places on Aitutaki. Any currency worth NZ$10,000 or more must be declared.

## Getting around safely

Driving is on the left. Cook Islands Tourism says visitors aged 16 and over can drive for up to six months on a full overseas licence, but anyone not licensed to ride a scooter or motorbike at home needs a Cook Islands licence from Police Headquarters in Avarua. Helmets are compulsory.

The FCDO warns that roads and street lighting across the South Pacific islands are poor, with few footpaths, and that driving at night is especially challenging. It also advises not to leave valuables unattended on the beach or in scooter storage, and to follow local safety advice when swimming, snorkelling or diving.

## Seasons, cyclones and health

Cook Islands Tourism says the drier months run from April to November, with an average temperature of 26°C; November to March is warmer and more humid, with occasional rain between December and March. The FCDO gives the South Pacific cyclone season as 1 November to 30 April, with around nine tropical cyclones across the region each season on average. If you travel then, flexible bookings and good insurance matter.

Medical facilities are limited. The FCDO says evacuation to New Zealand is likely the only option in a serious emergency, that you may have to pay before treatment, and that divers should check where the nearest working decompression chamber is. It advises drinking only boiled or bottled water and seeking travel-health advice at least eight weeks before you go. In an emergency in the Cook Islands, call 999.

## Before you go

- Check your passport has six months' validity beyond departure and two blank pages.
- Book accommodation before you fly: you may be asked for proof of it on arrival.
- Carry enough cash for any trip beyond Rarotonga and Aitutaki.
- Check whether your scooter licence is valid at home; if not, plan for a local licence.
- Buy insurance that covers medical evacuation to New Zealand and any diving.
- If travelling between November and April, watch cyclone forecasts and keep plans flexible.
