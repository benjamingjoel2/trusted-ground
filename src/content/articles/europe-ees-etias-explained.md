Two EU systems are changing how non-EU visitors enter most of Europe. The Entry/Exit System (EES) is already running: it records your fingerprints and photo at the border in place of a passport stamp, and you do not need to do anything in advance. ETIAS, an online travel authorisation for visa-exempt travellers, is not yet in operation, and no applications are being accepted.

## What the EES is, and where it applies

The EES is an automated system that registers non-EU nationals each time they cross the external border of one of the 29 European countries using it for a short stay. According to the European Commission, it records your name, travel document details, fingerprints and facial image, and the date and place of each entry and exit. It also records any refusal of entry.

The Commission says the EES started in stages on 12 October 2025 and became fully operational on 10 April 2026. Since then it has replaced passport stamping and detects overstayers automatically.

In practice these are the 29 Schengen area countries, which the UK government lists in full. They include Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway and Switzerland. Cyprus and Ireland are EU members, but the EU says passports there are still stamped by hand.

## Who the EES applies to

The EU defines a "non-EU national" as anyone without the nationality of an EU country, Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway or Switzerland. The EES applies whether you need a short-stay visa or not. A "short stay" means up to 90 days in any 180-day period, counted across all the participating countries together.

Exemptions include holders of residence permits and long-stay visas, and nationals of Andorra, Monaco and San Marino. The full list is on the EU's Travel to Europe website. The UK government advises exempt travellers to show their exempting documents to the border guard rather than register.

## What happens at the border

On your first crossing since the EES started, a border officer will photograph your face and/or scan your fingerprints, and your passport will not be stamped. According to the EU:

- If you do not need a visa, the system stores four fingerprints and your facial image. Visa holders' fingerprints are already held in the visa system, so only the facial image is added.
- Children under 12 do not currently have their fingerprints scanned.
- If you refuse to give biometric data, you will be refused entry.
- On later trips, officers only check your existing record. With a biometric passport you may be able to use self-service kiosks where they are installed.

The UK government says there is no cost for EES registration and no action is needed before you reach the border. It warns that waits may be longer than usual. If you travel through the Port of Dover, Eurotunnel Le Shuttle at Folkestone or Eurostar at St Pancras International, the checks take place in the UK before you leave. The UK government also says your digital EES record is valid for three years.

Cruises have their own rules. According to the UK government, sailings that start and end outside the Schengen area are usually exempt from EES checks for day trips ashore. If you board or leave a ship inside the Schengen area, you may need to register.

## ETIAS: the travel authorisation still to come

ETIAS will be an entry requirement for nationals of 59 visa-exempt countries and territories, the UK among them, visiting 30 European countries for a short stay. According to the UK government, you will not need it if you already hold a visa or residence permit.

**Current status.** At the time of writing (November 2026), the EU's official ETIAS pages state that ETIAS is not in operation and that no applications are being collected. The EU says it will announce the start date several months before launch. The UK government says ETIAS is expected to start operating from autumn 2026, that no action is required from travellers yet, and that any website selling ETIAS now is doing so fraudulently.

According to the EU's official ETIAS site, once ETIAS launches:

- **Fee:** EUR 20. Applicants under 18 or over 70 are exempt, as are qualifying family members of EU nationals.
- **Validity:** up to three years, or until the passport you applied with expires, whichever comes first. A new passport needs a new authorisation.
- **Processing:** most applications are decided within minutes. Others can take up to four days, or longer (up to 14 extra days if you are asked for more information, or up to 30 if you are invited to an interview).
- **How to apply:** only through the official ETIAS website or mobile app. You will need your passport, a payment card and details about your education, occupation and travel plans.
- **No guarantee of entry:** border guards still check that you meet the entry conditions.

The EU says the launch will be followed by transitional and grace periods. During these, travellers without an authorisation can in some cases still be let in, as long as they meet the other entry conditions.

## What to do before you travel

- **Check your passport.** The EU says your passport should be valid for at least three months after you plan to leave (exceptions apply). If you will need ETIAS, apply with the passport you will travel on.
- **Count your days.** The 90-in-180-day limit covers all Schengen countries together, and the EES now tracks it automatically. The EU's Travel to Europe site has an online tool for checking how long you can stay.
- **Ignore ETIAS sellers for now.** When ETIAS opens, apply only on the official EU site, travel-europe.europa.eu/etias, or the official app.

## Questions to ask your operator

- Will your itinerary keep me within 90 days in any 180-day period across all the Schengen countries we visit?
- Does the schedule allow extra time for EES checks at each border crossing, especially for land or ferry crossings?
- If the trip includes a cruise, where do we board and leave the ship, and will we need to register in the EES there?
- Does the trip include a non-Schengen country such as Cyprus or Ireland, and how does that affect the border checks?
- If ETIAS launches before I travel, will you remind me, and will you point me only to the official EU site?
- Who covers the cost if a missed connection is caused by border delays?
