To see mountain gorillas in Rwanda or Uganda you need a permit for a specific day. Each permit gives you about an hour with a habituated gorilla family in a group of no more than eight visitors. Numbers are tightly limited, so permits need booking well ahead. Rwanda sells them through its official online platform, while Uganda's wildlife authority says foreign visitors must book through a licensed tour operator.

## How the permit system works

Mountain gorillas live in the volcanic Virunga range shared by Rwanda, Uganda and the Democratic Republic of the Congo, and in Uganda's Bwindi Impenetrable National Park. Visits are limited to protect the gorillas, which can catch human illnesses such as coughs and colds.

The two countries' rules are very similar:

- **Group size:** a maximum of eight visitors per gorilla family per day.
- **Time with the gorillas:** a strict maximum of one hour per day.
- **Minimum age:** 15 years in both countries.

A permit is for one trek on one date, so your itinerary has to be built around it.

## Rwanda: Volcanoes National Park

In Rwanda, gorilla trekking takes place in Volcanoes National Park in the north-west. According to Visit Rwanda, run by the Rwanda Development Board (RDB), the park has twelve fully habituated gorilla families open to visitors. Eight permits are issued per family per day, which makes 96 permits a day.

Key points from Visit Rwanda, at the time of writing (November 2026):

- **Price:** US$1,500 per person. Special offers are available for Rwandan and East African Community residents.
- **Booking:** RDB recommends booking in advance. Its gorilla page points to the Irembo platform, and its booking page lists the RDB online booking service (visitrwandabookings.rdb.rw) for gorilla and primate permits, where the full price list is published. If you book through a private company, RDB advises using licensed operators and tour guides.
- **On the day:** visitors gather at the park headquarters in Kinigi at 7am. They are assigned to a gorilla family according to fitness and briefed on the rules.
- **The hike:** it can take anything from 30 minutes to four hours or more and reaches altitudes of 2,500 m to 4,000 m. Porters can be hired to carry bags and help on the trail.
- **Where the money goes:** RDB says 10% of permit revenue goes to local communities.

## Uganda: Bwindi and Mgahinga

In Uganda, gorilla permits are issued by the Uganda Wildlife Authority (UWA) for Bwindi Impenetrable National Park and Mgahinga Gorilla National Park. UWA's published conservation tariff for January 2027 to June 2028 lists the following rates for foreign non-residents:

- **Gorilla tracking permit:** US$800, which includes the guide fee, park entrance for the day and a community development contribution.
- **Low-season rate:** US$600 for gorilla tracking, applying only in April, May and November.
- **Gorilla Habituation Experience:** US$1,800 (Bwindi). This is a separate, longer product.

The tariff lists lower rates for foreign residents, visitors from elsewhere in Africa and East African citizens. The most important rule for international visitors is this: UWA states that all gorilla and chimpanzee permits for foreign non-residents and visitors from the rest of Africa can only be booked through licensed tour operators. It refers travellers to the Uganda Tourism Board's list of tour operators. Rates change between tariff periods, so confirm which tariff applies to your trekking date.

## Rules on the trek

UWA's gorilla tracking rules match the briefing you can expect in both countries:

- Carry your permit and payment receipts.
- Do not trek if you have a cold or other infectious illness.
- Keep at least 10 metres from the gorillas.
- Do not smoke, eat or drink while with the gorillas.
- If you must cough or sneeze, cover your face and turn away.
- Do not use flash photography, and leave no litter in the forest.

Visit Rwanda recommends solid walking shoes, gaiters, sturdy clothing against stinging nettles, gloves and a lightweight waterproof.

## Safety near the borders

Both gorilla areas lie close to the border with the Democratic Republic of the Congo. The UK Foreign, Commonwealth & Development Office (FCDO) advises anyone trekking near the DRC border in Rwanda to check its advice for that region, and warns that short-notice border closures can affect plans. For Uganda, the FCDO reports terrorist attacks in western areas near the DRC border, including in national parks, and advises particular caution. Read the current advice for both countries before you book, and talk your itinerary through with your operator.

## Questions to ask your operator

- Is my permit already secured for a specific date and park, and can I see the permit or booking confirmation?
- Which tariff and rate applies to my trekking date, and is the permit cost shown separately from your fees?
- Are you licensed in the country where I am trekking? (See our [guide to licences](/guide/licences/).)
- What happens to my permit and payment if I am ill on the day, or if the park or a border closes?
- How will you assess my fitness, and can you arrange a porter?
- What are your deposit and refund terms for the permit and the rest of the trip? (See [deposits and payment protection](/guide/deposits-and-payment-protection/).)
