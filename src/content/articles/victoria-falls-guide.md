Victoria Falls, also known as Mosi-oa-Tunya, sits on the Zambezi River on the border between Zambia and Zimbabwe. Both countries protect it as a shared UNESCO World Heritage Site. How it looks depends on when you go. After the rains the falls are a roaring wall of water and spray, but late in the dry season the flow drops sharply and more rock shows. With the right visa you can see both sides in a single trip.

## The falls in numbers

The Zimbabwe Parks and Wildlife Management Authority (ZimParks) gives the falls a height of 108 metres and a width of 1,708 metres. It notes they were added to the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1989. The IUCN World Heritage Outlook, which assesses the site's conservation, describes it at peak flow as the world's largest curtain of falling water. Up to 500 million litres a minute go over the edge, creating a plume of spray that rises 500 metres and can be seen 20 kilometres away.

Below the falls, the river has carved a zig-zag of gorges. The IUCN explains that they mark the positions of seven earlier waterfalls. The Devil's Cataract, at the western end of today's falls, is where the river has begun cutting back to an eighth.

## Seasons: high water and low water

The Zambezi River Authority (ZRA), the joint Zambian and Zimbabwean body that monitors the river, describes three seasons in the catchment:

- **November to April: hot and wet.** This is the rainy season that feeds the river.
- **April to July: cool and dry.** Rain tails off.
- **August to October: hot and dry.** This is the driest stretch, when flows fall furthest.

The ZRA's records show how big the swing can be. The long-term mean annual flow at Victoria Falls is 1,100 cubic metres per second. The highest flow ever recorded was 10,000 cubic metres per second, in March 1958. In the 1995/96 season, the annual mean was just 390. At the time of writing (November 2026), the ZRA reported a flow of 272 cubic metres per second on 10 November, with flows still steadily decreasing.

What this means in practice is a trade-off. High water brings the thunder and the spray, but the spray can hide the view and soak you. Low water shows the rock face and the shape of the gorge, but with far less water falling. The IUCN notes that droughts, including 2019/20, one of the worst in a century, have sharply reduced dry-season flows. Check recent flow reports from the ZRA if the spectacle matters to you.

## The Zimbabwean side

Victoria Falls National Park in Zimbabwe covers about 23.4 square kilometres. According to ZimParks, it has a dense rainforest watered by the spray from the falls, with ferns, palms, liana vines and mahogany. Baboons, warthogs and monkeys are common, and a leopard is an occasional sighting. ZimParks' cautions are simple: keep away from the cliff edge and watch for slippery rocks.

The IUCN describes this rainforest in the splash zone as a fragile ecosystem that depends on the spray, and it notes concern about how the relatively small rainforest will cope with growing visitor numbers. Stay on paths.

The town of Victoria Falls has an international airport. ZimParks says the falls can be reached by road along the A8 from Bulawayo.

## The Zambian side and the bridge

The Zambian side is protected as Mosi-oa-Tunya National Park, next to the growing town of Livingstone. The two sides are linked by the Victoria Falls Bridge, built in 1904 to 1905, which carries road and rail across the gorge just below the falls. The IUCN notes that the Zambian and Zimbabwean customs and immigration posts sit along this corridor, and that the bridge has become an attraction in its own right.

## Crossing the border: visas

Rules depend on your nationality. The following is the UK FCDO's advice for British passport holders at the time of writing (November 2026).

- **Zambia.** British passport holders do not need a visa and can stay up to 90 days in any 12-month period. You must show a return or onward ticket and enough money for your stay. Your passport needs at least six months' validity and two blank pages.
- **Zimbabwe.** You need a visa. Most visitors get one on arrival, usually for 30 days, paid in US dollars, so bring small notes. You can also apply for an e-visa in advance. Your passport needs six months' validity and three blank pages.
- **KAZA Univisa.** This visa allows unlimited travel between Zambia and Zimbabwe, plus day trips into Botswana. The FCDO says it costs US$50 and is valid for 30 days. You can get it online in advance, or on arrival at Livingstone airport, Victoria Falls airport (Zimbabwe) and the Victoria Falls land border.

Get your passport stamped. On leaving Zambia by air there is a departure tax, normally included in the ticket price.

## Before you go

- **Decide what you want to see.** Pick roaring spray after the rains or a clearer view of the rock face late in the dry season, and check recent ZRA flow reports.
- **Bring waterproofs** for your camera and yourself if you visit at high water.
- **Check your visa plan.** If you will cross more than once, the KAZA Univisa may be simpler.
- **Check your insurance for adventure activities.** The FCDO warns that adventure sports in the Victoria Falls area have caused serious accidents and deaths. It says bungee jumping is not usually included in standard insurance policies.
- **Keep back from edges.** Stay on marked paths, especially in the rainforest.
