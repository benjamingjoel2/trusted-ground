Few journeys feel as Greek as standing on a ferry's upper deck while a whitewashed harbour slides into view. Island-hopping is less complicated than it looks once you understand two things: islands come in groups, and from Athens you can sail from three different ports. Plan your route within a group, pick the right port, and leave slack in the schedule for strikes and weather.

## Think in island groups

Visit Greece, the national tourism organisation's site, divides the islands into seven groups: the Argosaronic, Crete, the Cyclades, the Dodecanese, the Ionian Islands, the North Aegean and the Sporades. Hopping is easiest within a group, where islands sit close together and share routes.

The Cyclades are the classic choice. Visit Greece explains that their name comes from the circle they form around the sacred island of Delos. The group includes:

- **Andros**, the northernmost, and **Tinos**, a major pilgrimage centre.
- **Syros**, with its elegant capital.
- **Kea** and **Kythnos**, close to Attica.
- **Mykonos**, **Paros**, **Naxos** (the largest and highest, with Mount Zas at 1,004 metres) and **Santorini**, whose caldera was formed by volcanic activity.
- **Milos**, **Sifnos**, **Folegandros**, **Sikinos**, **Amorgos** and the Small Cyclades, including Koufonisia.

## Choosing your Athens port

This is Athens, the city's official visitor site, lists three ports for the islands:

- **Piraeus** is the main port and serves most islands. You can reach it by bus or on metro line 1 from the city centre.
- **Rafina** has ferries to Andros, Tinos, Mykonos, Paros, Naxos, Amorgos, Ios, Syros, Sikinos, Folegandros, Koufonissia and Crete. By car or taxi it takes about 50 to 60 minutes; by public transport, take metro line 3 to Doukissis Plakentias then bus 314, or a KTEL bus from Pedion Areos.
- **Lavrio** serves Kythnos, Kea, Kimolos, Milos, Paros, Naxos, Tinos, Syros, Sikinos, Folegandros, and further afield Limnos and Kavala. KTEL buses run from the city centre and from Athens International Airport.

Check which port your sailing leaves from before you book a hotel. Rafina is about an hour from central Athens by road and Lavrio about 75 minutes by taxi, so arriving at the wrong port can cost you the boat.

## Seasons, strikes and weather

Timetables vary through the year, so build your route from the schedules for your actual dates rather than from a summer route map.

Two things can upend a plan at short notice:

- **Strikes.** The FCDO warns that there are regular strikes, sometimes called at short notice, that can disrupt buses, trains, ferries and air travel.
- **Weather.** Strong winds can delay or cancel sailings. EU passenger rules treat severe weather as a special case (see below), so you may get a refund or rerouting but not accommodation or compensation.

On land, summer heat matters too. The FCDO advises avoiding hiking at peak sun hours, carrying water, and never lighting fires because of the high wildfire risk. It also notes that Greece prohibits tent camping and parking motorhomes on beaches, seashores, archaeological sites and the edges of public forests; use licensed campsites.

## Your rights if a ferry is delayed or cancelled

Most ferries leaving EU ports are covered by EU passenger rights at sea. According to Your Europe, the EU's official guidance:

- If your sailing is **cancelled** or **delayed at departure by more than 90 minutes**, you can choose between a refund within 7 days or rerouting to your destination at the earliest opportunity at no extra cost.
- You are entitled to snacks, meals or refreshments in proportion to the wait and, where needed, accommodation ashore up to €80 a night for up to three nights. Accommodation is not owed when the cause is severe weather.
- If you **arrive late**, compensation is 25% or 50% of the ticket price depending on the journey length and delay. For a journey of up to four hours, that is 25% for a one-hour delay and 50% for two hours. No compensation is due if severe weather or extraordinary circumstances caused the delay.

The rules exclude very small vessels and some short routes, so ask if you are on a small boat.

## Before you go

- Pick one or two island groups rather than zig-zagging across the Aegean.
- Confirm your departure port (Piraeus, Rafina or Lavrio) and how you will get there.
- Leave a spare day before any flight home in case of strikes or weather.
- Keep your ticket and booking confirmation: you will need them to claim a refund or compensation.
- Check FCDO advice for strike notices close to your travel dates.
