For a few weeks each summer the Valensole plateau turns purple, row after row of lavender running towards the horizon, while to the west the villages of the Luberon sit on their hilltops. The short answer for lavender is mid-June to mid-July, but flowering shifts with the weather and harvest can start sooner than you expect. Plan Luberon walks around the daily fire-risk map, which can close the hills at a day's notice.

## When the lavender flowers

According to the regional tourism board for Provence-Alpes-Côte d'Azur, flowering depends on the weather and usually falls between 15 June and 15 July, with the fields at their most striking from mid-June to mid-July. Timing really does vary: in 2026 flowering began early, around 10 June, and cutting was scheduled to start on 29 June and continue gradually over about 15 days.

That means a trip timed for mid-July can arrive to find many fields already harvested. If lavender is the main reason for your visit:

- Aim for late June and early July, and keep your dates flexible if you can.
- Check with the Valensole or Manosque tourist offices shortly before you travel.
- Visit early or late in the day for softer light and cooler temperatures.

## Valensole and the lavender plateaux

The Valensole plateau is one of the most important areas in France for growing lavender and lavandin, according to the Alpes de Haute-Provence tourism board. Cultivation also extends to the Entrevennes plateau and around Puimichel, Le Castellet, Oraison and Manosque.

Not everything you see is true lavender. The tourism boards explain:

- **Lavender** was introduced to France by the Phocaeans and found its ideal conditions in Provence.
- **Lavandin** is a hybrid of true lavender and aspic (wild lavender) that appeared in the 1920s. It forms a large clump with long, compact flower spikes, and its essential oil yield can be up to ten times that of lavender. It is widely used in soaps, detergents and home fragrances.

Access to the Valensole site is free and open every day, but the tourism boards ask visitors not to walk into the fields, to help preserve them. These are working farms: photograph from the edges and field tracks, and do not pick the flowers. Dogs must be kept on a lead in the Verdon natural park.

## The Luberon and its hill villages

The Parc naturel régional du Luberon covers 1,867 square kilometres and 77 communes, 51 in Vaucluse and 26 in Alpes-de-Haute-Provence. It joined UNESCO's biosphere reserve network in 1997 and the global geoparks network in 2004.

The park explains that its perched villages were founded from around the year 1000 on hilltops, around fortified castles, so that inhabitants could see an enemy coming and make an attack difficult. Over about a millennium they went through crises and revivals; the later hamlets, by contrast, grew up on the plains.

A few places to build into a Luberon itinerary:

- **Roussillon** has the Sentier des Ocres, a paid walking trail through the ochre landscape run by the town, with no booking required. At the time of writing (November 2026), the town lists individual entry at €3.50. It can close by prefectoral order for fire risk, heavy rain or storms, announced the evening before.
- **Sénanque Abbey**, in a valley near Gordes, is a 12th-century foundation where monks still live under the Rule of Saint Benedict. Visitors are asked to keep silence, as the monks withdraw into enclosure during visits; the abbey strongly recommends a guided visit, and photography is not allowed during services.

## Summer fire risk and forest access

High summer in Provence brings real wildfire risk. The UK Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office (FCDO) notes that risk is highest in the south and along the Mediterranean, that access to forests and tourist sites may be restricted at short notice, and that causing a wildfire, even accidentally, is illegal.

In Vaucluse, the prefecture publishes a daily map of access to forest massifs, updated at 18:00 for the following day, with a phone line on +33 4 28 31 77 11. The levels are:

- **Green**: access possible.
- **Yellow**: access possible, mornings recommended.
- **Red**: access forbidden all day.
- **Black**: access and work forbidden all day.

At all levels, smoking, lighting fires, camping and driving or parking on emergency forest tracks are banned. In a fire, call 18 or 112.

## Before you go

- Book lavender-focused trips for late June to early July, and confirm flowering with local tourist offices.
- Stay at the edges of lavender fields and never pick the flowers.
- Check the Vaucluse fire-risk map after 18:00 the evening before any hike in the Luberon hills.
- Carry water and start walks early; summer heat builds quickly.
- Keep silence and follow visiting hours at Sénanque Abbey.
- Read the latest FCDO advice, including entry requirements, before you travel.
