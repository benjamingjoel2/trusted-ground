To visit the temples of Angkor you need a pass from Angkor Enterprise, the official ticketing body. At the time of writing (November 2026) a pass costs US$37 for one day, US$62 for three days or US$72 for seven days. The park opens at 5am, early enough to be in place for sunrise. Outlying sites such as Beng Mealea, Koh Ker and Kbal Spean need their own tickets. Cover your shoulders and knees, or you may be refused entry.

## A city of stone in the forest

Angkor Enterprise says there are more than a thousand temples to discover around Siem Reap, and its main park pass covers more than 50 of them. Angkor Wat sits at the centre on a site of 162.6 hectares, which the authority describes as the largest religious monument in the world. Around it are temples from several centuries of Khmer kings. Phnom Bakheng, a hilltop temple dedicated to Shiva, dates from the end of the 9th century. Baphuon, a three-tiered temple mountain, was built in the mid-11th century. The richly carved Bayon was the state temple of King Jayavarman VII in the late 12th or early 13th century, and Preah Khan was built in the 12th century for the same king to honour his father. Banteay Srei, a 10th-century temple also dedicated to Shiva, lies about 25km north-east of the main group.

## Passes, prices and validity

Official prices from Angkor Enterprise at the time of writing:

| Ticket | Price | Valid for use within |
|---|---|---|
| Angkor Temples Park, 1 day | US$37 | 1 day |
| Angkor Temples Park, 3 days | US$62 | 7 days |
| Angkor Temples Park, 7 days | US$72 | 30 days |
| Koh Ker Temples Park, 1 day | US$15 | 1 day |
| Beng Mealea, 1 day | US$10 | 1 day |
| Kbal Spean, 1 day | US$5 | 1 day |

The multi-day passes do not have to be used on consecutive days. A 3-day pass can be spread across a week, and a 7-day pass across a month. That flexibility is the single most useful tool for a calmer visit, because you can mix early starts with rest days, or come back in different light.

Buy from the official ticket site (ticket.angkorenterprise.gov.kh), which is linked from the Angkor Enterprise website. Angkor Enterprise also has an official Angkor Pass app for Android and iPhone. Prices change, so check the official site rather than relying on figures quoted by third parties.

## Sunrise and opening hours

According to Angkor Enterprise, the main Angkor park allows entry from 5am and closes at 6.30pm. That early start is what makes sunrise visits possible. Koh Ker, Beng Mealea and Kbal Spean open later, at 6am, and also close at 6.30pm.

Angkor Enterprise does not publish crowd data, so there is no official guide to the quietest hours. A calmer visit is mostly about using your pass flexibly. Think about which temples you see at which times of day, spend some days on the outlying sites with their separate tickets, and allow time for the many smaller temples beyond the famous few.

## Dress, conduct and photography

The UK Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office (FCDO) says there is an official code of conduct for visitors to Angkor Wat and other religious sites. You may be refused entry if you wear shorts or skirts above the knee, and you should cover bare shoulders. A light scarf or shirt is easy to carry for this. The FCDO also advises asking permission before photographing people, especially monks. It adds that extensive photography or filming may need advance permission from the local authorities.

## Safety around Siem Reap

The FCDO warns that mined areas in Cambodia are often unmarked. It advises staying on main routes in rural areas, including around temple complexes, and never picking up metal objects. Its other points relevant to Siem Reap:

- Lone women have been sexually assaulted by men claiming to be motorbike taxi drivers around the Pub Street area. Be cautious about lifts offered by strangers.
- Bag snatching is common in Cambodia. The FCDO suggests tuk-tuks with metal grilles on the back and sides.
- Fake US dollar notes have been given as change in shops, so check your notes.

For entry, the FCDO says you need a passport valid for at least six months from arrival and a tourist visa, either an e-visa applied for at least four days ahead or a visa on arrival paid in US dollars. ## Before you go

- Buy your pass on the official Angkor Enterprise ticket site or app, not through a reseller.
- Choose a 3-day or 7-day pass if you want to spread visits and avoid rushing.
- Pack clothing that covers your shoulders and knees for every temple day.
- Agree the day's route and start time with your driver or guide the evening before, especially for sunrise.
- Stay on marked paths around remote temples.
- Submit Cambodia's electronic Arrival Card before flying into Siem Reap.
