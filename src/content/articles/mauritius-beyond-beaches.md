Turn inland from the lagoons of Mauritius and the island changes quickly: forested gorges and waterfalls in the south-west, the great bulk of Le Morne Brabant above the west coast, and, in the capital Port Louis, the stone remains of the depot through which hundreds of thousands of indentured labourers entered the island. Together they tell the story of how Mauritius was shaped, by nature and by people. All three fit easily around a beach holiday; the weather is the main thing to plan for.

## Black River Gorges National Park

The Mauritius Tourism Promotion Authority (MTPA) describes Black River Gorges as the country's biggest national park, covering about 17,000 acres, or 2 per cent of the island. It is a landscape of rolling hills, valleys, rivers, waterfalls and rainforest.

What to expect, according to the MTPA:

- **Trails:** more than 50km, from easy forest walks to demanding routes.
- **Black River Peak:** the highest point in Mauritius, at 828 metres, with wide views from the top.
- **Alexandra Falls:** signposted from the road, with a viewpoint over the waterfall and the park.
- **Wildlife:** more than 300 species of flowering plants and nine bird species found only in Mauritius, plus the Mauritian flying fox. Wild boar, macaques and deer also live in the park. Birdwatching walks with a biologist can be booked.

The park is the core of UNESCO's Black River Gorges-Bel Ombre Biosphere Reserve. UNESCO says it protects the last remaining fragments of the island's tropical evergreen forest, and that about a quarter of Mauritius's flora and fauna is endemic. Invasive species are a serious threat. Conservation work includes weeding out exotic plants, trapping introduced macaques, propagating native plants and breeding birds in captivity. An information centre in the park runs exhibitions and talks.

## Le Morne: mountain, memory and kitesurfing

Le Morne Brabant rises above the west coast, and the MTPA notes that the mountain is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Its meaning goes deeper than the view. When the International Kreol Festival opened at its foot in December 2024, the culture minister at the time, Mahen Gondeea, described the setting as "the expression of the soul and history of the former slaves". The MTPA's report of the event calls it a place where men and women chose to be close to death rather than live enslaved.

At the foot of the mountain lies Le Morne Beach. The MTPA says the south of the island has strong, steady trade winds, especially between April and November, which makes the area popular for kitesurfing; a spot called One Eye, off Pointe du Morne, is well known among kitesurfers. Kitesurf and surf schools operate there. For those who want to sit still, the sunsets are the reason to stay late.

Visit Le Morne with its history in mind. It is a beach, but it is also a memorial landscape.

## Aapravasi Ghat, Port Louis

Aapravasi Ghat in Port Louis is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and, says the MTPA, among the oldest surviving immigration depots associated with indenture. It was part of what the British government called the "Great Experiment", which began after slavery was abolished in the British Empire in 1834 and replaced enslaved labour with indentured workers.

The scale is striking. According to the MTPA:

- More than 462,000 indentured labourers passed through, 97.5 per cent of them from India, with others from China, the Comoros, Madagascar, Mozambique and South East Asia.
- They were recruited to work on the sugar plantations, for periods of five years.
- Two-thirds stayed in Mauritius for good, and about 70 per cent of today's Mauritians are their descendants.
- The success of the Mauritian experiment led to the migration of more than 2.2 million indentured labourers worldwide, including to the Caribbean, South America and the South Pacific.

At the site you can see the partial remains of three stone buildings, documents and artefacts such as pipes and medicines, and a ten-minute film of descendants' testimonies.

## When to go and weather risks

Mauritius Meteorological Services (MMS) describes two seasons: a warm, humid summer from November to April and a cooler, drier winter from June to September, with May and October as transition months. February and March are the wettest months and October the driest. Mean temperatures are 24.7°C in summer and 20.4°C in winter.

The weather affects inland trips more than beach days:

- The UK Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office (FCDO) says the tropical cyclone season usually runs from November to May. During a cyclone you are not allowed to leave your accommodation, and car insurance policies often become invalid.
- Some areas are prone to landslides, especially during cyclones and torrential rain. MMS issues phased cyclone warnings and five-stage landslide warnings.
- If you plan to hike in the gorges, bear in mind that MMS describes June to September as the cooler, drier season, and check forecasts and warnings before setting out.

## Before you go

- Check MMS forecasts and any cyclone, heavy-rain or landslide warnings before heading into the hills.
- Ask your guide which Black River Gorges trails are open and how long and steep they are.
- Book any birdwatching walk with a biologist in advance.
- Check the opening times of Aapravasi Ghat and allow time for the film.
- If you plan to kitesurf at Le Morne, ask the school about your experience level and the wind conditions on the day.
- Treat Le Morne as a memorial site as well as a beach.
