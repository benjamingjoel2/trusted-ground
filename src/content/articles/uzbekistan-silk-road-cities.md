Turquoise domes, tiled portals and mud-brick walls the colour of the desert: Samarkand, Bukhara and Khiva are the cities that made the Silk Road a byword for splendour, and all three are on UNESCO's World Heritage List. Samarkand is the grandest, Bukhara the most lived-in, Khiva the most complete walled town. Summers are very hot and winters can be cold, especially in Khiva, and visitors need to keep track of their registration, carry cash and take care with photography.

## Samarkand: Timur's capital

Uzbekistan's national tourism body says Samarkand was founded about 2,750 years ago, which makes it roughly as old as Rome. Its golden age came under Amir Temur (Tamerlane) and his descendants, when it was the capital of his empire, and his grandson Mirzo Ulugbek turned it into a centre of world science. UNESCO added the city to its World Heritage List early in the 21st century as "Samarkand – Crossroads of Cultures".

The essential sights are:

- **The Registan**, the square framed by three madrasahs: Ulugbek, Sherdor and Tilla-Kari.
- **Gur-Emir**, the mausoleum of Amir Temur, his mentor Mir Sayyid Barak and members of his family.
- **Bibi-Khanym** mosque and mausoleum, near the Siab bazaar in the old city.
- **Shah-i-Zinda**, the necropolis.

## Bukhara: the living old town

By the most conservative estimates, the tourism board says, Bukhara is more than 2,500 years old, and its historic centre was added to the World Heritage List in 1993. The Ark, the city's citadel, was destroyed and rebuilt many times over about a thousand years, each time stronger.

Around it are:

- **The Poi-Kalon ensemble**, with its great minaret, which the tourism board says the Shanghai Cooperation Organization has called the "Eighth Miracle".
- **The trading domes** next to Poi-Kalon, including the Tim Abdullah Khan dome, still used for shopping.
- **Lyabi-Hauz**, the ensemble and square where most shops are.
- **The Samanid Mausoleum**, built by Ismail Samani, the second emir of the Samanid state, and a prominent example of Islamic architecture in Central Asia.

## Khiva: a fortress city

Khiva is one of Uzbekistan's oldest cities, founded about 2,500 years ago, and was inscribed on the World Heritage List in 1990. Its inner town, Itchan Kala, is a medieval fortress within the city's ancient walls. Look for:

- **Kalta Minor**, near the western Ota-Darvoz gate, intended to be the tallest minaret in Central Asia but never finished.
- **The Juma (Friday) Mosque**, built in the 10th century, with no domes and no conventional entrance; its roof rests on 213 columns of different sizes, added over several centuries.

## When to go

The national tourism board describes very different climates across the three cities:

- **Samarkand** has very hot summers and usually mild winters, rarely falling below 0°C.
- **Bukhara** has hot, dry summers and fairly mild winters, though warm clothing is still advised.
- **Khiva** has a dry desert climate with hot, windy summers and winters that often fall below 0°C.

That makes the shoulder months, between the summer heat and the winter cold, the easiest for long days of walking between monuments. The FCDO notes that air quality can be poor in winter, between November and February, mainly in Tashkent and the Fergana valley, and that the Karakalpakstan and Khorezm regions, where Khiva lies, can have poor air quality all year because of storms over the Aral Sea that carry salt and dust.

## Rules visitors need to know

At the time of writing (November 2026) the FCDO says:

- **Visas:** UK nationals can visit visa-free for up to 30 days for tourism or business. Other nationalities should check with an Uzbek embassy.
- **Registration:** you must register with the local district authority within three days of arrival, weekends included. Hotels usually do this for you; in a private house, your host does it. Officials may ask for a full record of where you have stayed when you leave.
- **Customs:** you must declare amounts of money above US$10,000 and cannot leave with more foreign currency than you brought in. Keep both copies of your customs declaration.
- **Money:** the Uzbek so'm is not available outside the country. Change money only at official booths, because black-market exchange is illegal with harsh penalties. Damaged notes and US dollars issued before 2013 are refused, and ATMs that take international cards are rare outside the main cities.
- **Photography:** any photography can upset the authorities, especially of government buildings, airports, border points, military sites, bridges, police stations and metro stations. Ask first.
- **Trains:** you need your passport to buy rail tickets. On overnight trains, lock the compartment and keep valuables with you.
- **Religious sensitivity:** during Ramadan, take particular care not to offend, and expect areas around mosques to be busy at prayer times.

## Before you go

- Keep proof of every night's registration, from hotels or hosts, until you leave the country.
- Bring clean, undamaged US dollars issued from 2013 onwards and change them only at official booths.
- Draw cash in a main city before heading to Khiva or other regions.
- Ask before photographing anything official, including metro stations.
- Avoid the height of summer if you can; in winter, pack warm layers for Khiva.
- Check the FCDO's Uzbekistan advice for the latest entry rules before you book.
