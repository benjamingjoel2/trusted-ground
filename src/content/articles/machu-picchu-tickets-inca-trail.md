Entry to Machu Picchu is by a named ticket for a fixed date, entry time, circuit and route. You buy it from Peru's Ministry of Culture, and it cannot be changed or refunded once paid. Visitors walking the Inca Trail are different. Their entry is reserved and paid in advance through authorised agencies, from a limited number of daily places. Whichever way you arrive, the circuit and route you book decide what you will see.

## Circuits and routes

Peru's Ministry of Culture organises visits to the Inca city (the *llaqta*) into three circuits, divided into ten routes. This system has applied since 1 June 2024:

- **Circuit 1, Panoramic:** Machupicchu Mountain (1-A), the upper terrace (1-B), and, in peak season only, the Intipunku gateway (1-C) and the Inca Bridge (1-D).
- **Circuit 2, Classic Machupicchu:** the designed route (2-A) and the lower terrace route (2-B).
- **Circuit 3, Royal Machupicchu:** Waynapicchu (Huayna Picchu) mountain (3-A), the designed route (3-B), and, in peak season only, the Great Cavern (3-C) and Huchuypicchu (3-D).

The mountain climbs are routes within their circuits, so if you want to climb Machupicchu Mountain or Waynapicchu, book that specific route. Entry times for each circuit are published as a schedule on the [official Machu Picchu site](https://www.machupicchu.gob.pe/circuitos-y-rutas-de-visita/).

## Buying tickets

According to the Ministry of Culture, tickets are sold through [Tuboleto.cultura.pe](https://tuboleto.cultura.pe/), the state platform for visits to cultural sites, which was created on 3 August 2024. The process:

- Create an account with your identity document (a passport, for most foreign visitors) and email address.
- Choose the circuit, route, date and entry time, and enter your nationality.
- Pay by Visa or Mastercard. The terms say American Express and Diners are not accepted. You can also download your reservation and pay at an agency of the Banco de la Nación.

The platform's terms state that tickets are valid only for the date and time bought. Changes of date or of the ticket holder's name are not allowed, and there are no refunds. As exceptions, a passport number can be corrected after a genuine data-entry error, and the date and time may be changed in cases of force majeure. The terms also say tickets can be bought in person at the sales office in the Casa Garcilaso in Cusco, subject to availability.

Prices are shown on the platform when you book. Check the names, passport numbers and entry time carefully before you pay, whether you book yourself or an operator books for you.

## The Inca Trail

The trails leading to Machu Picchu, called the Red de Caminos Inka (Inca Trail network), are managed by the Ministry of Culture and SERNANP, Peru's protected areas service. Ministerial resolution 306-2025-MC sets out how trail walkers enter the site:

- Visitors arriving on the Inca Trail can enter on route 3-B, within the site's approved daily capacity.
- A limited number of daily places is set aside in the ticketing system for Inca Trail visitors only.
- These places "must be reserved and paid in advance through authorised agencies".

In other words, you cannot simply turn up and walk the Inca Trail. You book through an authorised agency, which reserves both the trail and your entry to the site.

The trail can also close. In 2025, the Ministry of Culture, SERNANP and the Cusco regional government announced that the network would reopen on 1 April 2025, with some diversions and a temporarily closed campsite after maintenance work. That notice says the official or licensed tourism guide is responsible for making sure their group follows the rules on the trail.

## Rules at the site

The Ministry of Culture publishes a list of what is prohibited inside the site. It includes:

- Backpacks, bags or purses larger than 40 x 35 x 20 cm.
- Food and tableware, alcohol, and smoking or vaping.
- Walking sticks, umbrellas, tripods, and camera or phone stabilisers and extensions.
- High heels or hard-soled shoes, and baby strollers.
- Drones, paragliders or any small aircraft over the site.
- Climbing on walls or touching or moving stones, and leaving the established circuits and routes.

The ministry says breaking these rules means immediate removal without a refund, and possible legal action.

## Weather and disruption

The UK Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office (FCDO) notes that the rainy season runs from November to May. Rockslides and mudslides can affect walking routes, including the alternative route to Machu Picchu via Santa Maria, Santa Teresa and the hydroelectric plant. It also warns that protests sometimes disrupt train services to and from Machu Picchu, and advises travellers to check the latest conditions with their tour operator.

## Questions to ask your operator

- Are you an authorised agency for the Inca Trail, and can you show us your authorisation?
- Which circuit, route and entry time have you booked for each of us, and can we see the tickets?
- Have you entered our names and passport numbers exactly as they appear in our passports?
- Will a licensed guide accompany us on the trail and at the site?
- What happens if the trail or the trains are closed on our dates?
- What is your cancellation policy, given that site tickets themselves cannot be refunded?
