At dawn on the Kinabatangan, mist lifts off brown water, hornbills cross the river in pairs and proboscis monkeys settle into the riverside trees. Far to the west, a granite summit more than 4,000 metres high catches the first light above the clouds. Sabah, the Malaysian state on the northern tip of Borneo, puts rainforest, river wildlife and a serious mountain within one itinerary. The short answer: see orangutans at Sepilok's scheduled feedings, give the Kinabatangan more than one night, and book Mount Kinabalu well ahead through the Sabah Parks system, because climbs need a permit, a licensed guide and a hostel bed.

## Sepilok: orangutans on their own terms

The Sepilok Orang Utan Rehabilitation Centre was established in 1964 to rehabilitate orphaned orangutans, according to the Sabah Tourism Board. It sits in the Kabili-Sepilok Forest Reserve, about 23km, or roughly 40 minutes by road, from Sandakan city centre.

Most visitors see orangutans at the platform during the scheduled feedings at 10am and 3pm, or from the outdoor nursery viewing area. The tourism board is candid that sometimes no orangutans turn up, usually because they have found food in the forest, especially in fruiting season. That is the point of rehabilitation: the animals are meant to depend on the wild, not on visitors.

The centre asks visitors not to touch orangutans, including any that wander onto the boardwalk, because many human diseases can pass to them.

## The Kinabatangan River

The Lower Kinabatangan is described by the Sabah Tourism Board as Sabah's first and Malaysia's largest Ramsar wetland site. At 560km, the Kinabatangan is Malaysia's second-longest river, running from south-west Sabah to the Sulu Sea. A total of 26,000 hectares was gazetted as the Kinabatangan Wildlife Sanctuary under the state's Wildlife Conservation Enactment of 1997.

According to the tourism board:

- The surrounding forest is one of only two known places in the world where ten primate species can be found, including orangutans and proboscis monkeys.
- All eight hornbill species found in Borneo live in the area, and the lower river is listed as an Important Bird Area.
- Bornean pygmy elephants and saltwater crocodiles can also be seen from the river.
- River cruises at dawn or dusk give the best chance of sightings, and night walks or night cruises can be arranged.

Lodges are clustered around Sukau, Bilit and Abai. The tourism board says the area can be reached by a boat ride of 1.5 to 2 hours from Sandakan or overland in about 1.5 hours, and recommends a stay of at least three days, since a longer visit improves the odds of seeing wildlife. Homestays in surrounding villages offer a chance to meet the Orang Sungai, the river communities.

## Climbing Mount Kinabalu

Kinabalu Park was gazetted in 1964 as Sabah's first park and is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Sabah Parks says it covers about 754 square kilometres, with park headquarters at around 1,520 metres, approximately 92km from Kota Kinabalu. Mount Kinabalu's summit, Low's Peak, stands at 4,095.2 metres. The park's published profile estimates 5,000 to 6,000 vascular plant species, from lowland dipterocarp forest to the pitcher plants of its ultramafic soils.

Sabah Parks describes a two-day climb but recommends a three-day visit to allow more time for acclimatisation. How it works, according to the official climbing page:

- **Book online through the Sabah Parks reservation portal**, choosing a hostel package (Lemaing or Panalaban) that matches your eligibility. Accommodation, meals, permit, insurance and service charge are paid with the booking.
- **Pay on time and check names.** Unpaid reservations are cancelled when the payment window expires, name replacements are not allowed and cancellations are non-refundable.
- **Check in online** from five days before the climb and complete an indemnity form for each climber.
- **On arrival**, pay the conservation fee cashlessly at the park gate, register at the visitor centre, then arrange and pay in cash for your mountain guide, the transfer to Timpohon Gate and any porter.
- **Guides are compulsory.** Sabah Parks sets one guide for five climbers, or one guide for two climbers aged 15 and under.
- **On the mountain**, display your permit tag at checkpoints and use the white rope on the section above Panalaban in both directions. The summit push starts in the early hours, aiming for sunrise; weather and safety decide whether you reach the top.

Sabah Parks asks people with conditions that may be triggered by severe cold, exertion or high altitude not to climb, and advises talking to a clinician beforehand. Our guide to [altitude sickness](/articles/altitude-sickness-travel/) explains the symptoms to watch for. Package rates are published on the official site and were last updated on 25 November 2025; check them when you book.

## Safety on the east coast

The UK Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office (FCDO) advises against all but essential travel to all islands and dive sites off the coast of eastern Sabah from Sandakan to Tawau, including Lankayan Island, because of the threat of kidnapping. It says this does not apply to the mainland of Sabah. Sepilok and the Kinabatangan are on the mainland, but check the current advice before adding any island or dive trip on this coast, and check that your insurance is valid for every place you plan to visit.

## Before you go

- According to the FCDO at the time of writing (November 2026), UK visitors can normally stay in Malaysia for 90 days without a visa and must submit an online arrival card before travelling. Check the rules for your passport.
- Carry your passport between Peninsular Malaysia and Sabah: the FCDO notes you pass through immigration and get entry and exit stamps.
- Book Kinabalu hostel packages early, and bring cash for the guide, transfer and porter.
- Plan Sepilok around the 10am or 3pm feeding, and keep your distance from the animals.
- Allow at least two nights on the Kinabatangan if you can.
