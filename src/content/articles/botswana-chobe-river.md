In the dry winter months, the Chobe River draws wildlife in remarkable numbers. Family herds of elephants file down to the water to drink, bathe and play, buffalo crowd the floodplain, and hippos and crocodiles share the channels with an extraordinary range of birds. Chobe National Park is the most accessible of Botswana's big game areas, starting just outside the town of Kasane, but it also reaches deep into wilder country around Savuti and Linyanti.

## How the park is laid out

According to the Botswana Tourism Organisation, Chobe National Park was established in 1968 and covers approximately 11,700 square kilometres of floodplain, swamp and woodland. The Chobe River forms its northern boundary. The park has four distinct areas:

- **The Chobe Riverfront**, the most accessible and most visited section, close to Kasane.
- **Savuti** (also spelled Savuté), at the heart of the park.
- **Linyanti**, where a small part of the park reaches the Linyanti River and swamps.
- **The Ngwezumba pans**, in the interior.

The river itself rises in the highlands of northern Angola and travels a long way before reaching Botswana at Ngoma. The tourism board notes that, like the Okavango and Zambezi, its course is shaped by fault lines that extend from the Great Rift Valley.

## The riverfront and Kasane

Kasane is the gateway. The Botswana Tourism Organisation describes it as spread along the banks of the Chobe, with hotels, guest houses and campsites, some with views of the river and its wildlife, and small shopping malls for provisions. The park lies about 10km away, and Victoria Falls is around 80km away, which is why many itineraries combine the two. Our article on [Victoria Falls](/articles/victoria-falls-guide/) covers that side of the trip.

From Kasane, visitors can plan morning and afternoon game drives and boat cruises.

**On a game drive**, the tourism board says you may see as many as 15 species on one drive along the riverbank tracks. Common species include waterbuck, lechwe, puku (which it describes as endemic to this area), giraffe, kudu, roan, sable, impala, warthog and bushbuck, with lion, leopard, hyena and jackal never far away. In the dry season, the main Serondella Road can become impassable as family herds of elephants cross it to reach the river.

**On a river cruise**, the perspective changes completely. The tourism board points out that from a boat you get closer to hippos, crocodiles and water birds than you would on land. More than 460 bird species have been recorded in the park, including Pel's fishing owl, carmine bee-eaters, African fish eagles, martial eagles, cormorants, darters and many kingfishers and storks.

## Savuti and Linyanti

Savuti feels very different from the river. The Botswana Tourism Organisation says it holds most of the Chobe region's species, apart from water-loving antelope, and is best known for large resident populations of predators, particularly lion, cheetah and hyena.

The landscape has its own story. The Savuti Channel runs about 100km from the Linyanti River into a vast swampland called the Savuti Marsh, and on to the Mababe Depression, once part of the ancient Makgadikgadi super-lake. Over the past century the channel has dried up and started flowing again several times, for reasons some attribute to plate tectonics. Trees germinate and grow in the dry channel and drown when the water returns, which is why dead trees line its banks. When the Mababe fills with water, it draws thousands of migratory birds and large herds of zebra.

To the west, the part of the park that reaches the Linyanti River has a government campsite with facilities for self-drive campers. Neighbouring private concessions (Kwando, Selinda and Linyanti) have small camps. In the dry season, the tourism board says, the permanent waters of the Kwando and Linyanti rivers become essential points for buffalo, elephant, wildebeest and zebra from across northern Botswana.

## Seasons: why the dry months matter

Botswana has two main seasons. The Botswana Tourism Organisation says:

- **Winter (May to August)** is the dry season, with virtually no rain. Days are sunny and cool to warm, but nights can fall below zero in some areas, and frost is common.
- **Summer (November to March)** brings high temperatures and the rains, with January and February the peak months. Shade temperatures can reach 38°C and higher before the rains arrive.
- **April to early May, and September to October**, tend to be dry, with cooler days than summer and warmer nights than winter.

For game viewing, the tourism board suggests April to October. As water dries up, wildlife gathers around what remains, and lower grass improves visibility. On the Chobe Riverfront, this is when the large herds of elephant and buffalo come to drink.

The extreme north-east of the Chobe District is also the wettest part of the country, with mean annual rainfall of more than 650mm, according to the tourism board. Cold early-morning drives in winter call for warm layers.

## Staying safe

The FCDO notes isolated room break-ins and thefts from lodges in the Chobe area, particularly river-fronting lodges, and its advice page lists precautions against burglary. It also advises that dangerous animals may roam on open highways inside and outside reserves, that driving at night carries extra risk from wildlife and livestock, and that you should not bathe in rivers and lakes because of wild animals and water-borne disease. Always follow park regulations and advice from park staff.

## Before you go

- Visit between April and October for the best game viewing on the riverfront.
- Pack warm layers for winter mornings and evenings, when temperatures can drop below zero.
- Plan both a game drive and a boat cruise from Kasane: they show very different sides of the river.
- If predators are your priority, consider adding Savuti or Linyanti to your itinerary.
- Avoid night driving and never swim in the river.
