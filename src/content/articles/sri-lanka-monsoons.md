Sri Lanka has two monsoons that soak opposite sides of the island at different times of year. Broadly, the southwest monsoon (May to September) brings its weather to the southwest and the hill country, while the northeast monsoon (December to February) brings rain to the north and east. That is why there is usually a drier coast somewhere, and why the right itinerary depends on the month you travel.

## The four seasons, according to the Department of Meteorology

Sri Lanka's Department of Meteorology divides the year into two monsoons and two transition periods between them:

- **First inter-monsoon:** March to April
- **Southwest monsoon:** May to September
- **Second inter-monsoon:** October to November
- **Northeast monsoon:** December to February

The department describes the southwest monsoon as the more significant of the two, because it brings rain to large parts of the island and lasts for nearly half the year. During the inter-monsoon periods, rain comes mainly from convective thunderstorms, with afternoon showers over most of the island.

These are average patterns, not fixed dates. Monsoons can arrive early or late, and a wet day can turn up in a "dry" season anywhere.

## Which coast to visit when

The department's own summary of the two monsoons explains the familiar advice about switching coasts:

- **May to September (southwest monsoon):** the weather is confined mainly to the southwest quarter of the island and the hill country. This is the wetter time for the west and south coasts, including Colombo and Galle, and for the tea country. The east and north lie largely outside the main reach of this monsoon.
- **December to February (northeast monsoon):** the eastern and northern regions receive their rain. The department notes that January and February bring long spells of dry days over most of the country, except in the north and east. This makes these months the drier window for the southwest coast and much of the interior.
- **March to April and October to November (inter-monsoons):** neither side is reliably dry. Expect afternoon thunderstorms across most of the island.

Rainfall also varies hugely by area. The department gives mean annual rainfall of under 1,000mm in the south-east and north-west of the island, rising to over 5,000mm on the western slopes of the central highlands.

## The hill country and temperature

Altitude matters as much as the monsoon. According to the Department of Meteorology, mean annual temperature ranges from about 27°C in the coastal lowlands to about 16°C at Nuwara Eliya, around 1,900m above sea level. Pack a warm layer for the highlands whatever the month.

On the coast, the temperature changes little through the year. The department puts Colombo's mean temperature in the cooler months of November to February at about 27°C, only about 3°C below the warmest months of April and May. Season, for most travellers, is about rain and sea conditions rather than heat.

## What the rain means for travel

Heavy rain is the main practical risk, and it affects transport more than sightseeing.

- **Floods and landslides.** The Department of Meteorology lists flooding as most associated with the onset of the southwest monsoon and with the second inter-monsoon period (October to November), mainly in low-lying areas. Heavy rain and strong winds can trigger earth slips in the hills, and the department has noted that landslides in the hill country have become more frequent. The UK Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office (FCDO) warns that monsoon rains can cause flooding in many places, and that heavy rain and landslips can close roads.
- **Cyclones.** The department identifies October to December as the period when tropical cyclones may affect Sri Lanka. Depressions forming in the Bay of Bengal can bring much rain and wind, although landfall on the east coast is rare. The FCDO also notes a risk of tropical cyclones.
- **Wind and the sea.** The department notes that strong gusts during the southwest monsoon can cause damage in the southwest quarter, and that sea erosion mainly occurs during the southwest monsoon. The FCDO warns that many beaches have dangerous surf or rip tides at certain times of the year, and advises asking your hotel or tour operator before going into the sea.

The FCDO advises monitoring the Disaster Management Centre and the Department of Meteorology for updates and following local advice. If you are travelling during a monsoon or an inter-monsoon, build slack into the itinerary, especially for road journeys through the hill country.

## Planning around the seasons

A few practical points follow from the patterns above:

- If your trip falls between December and February, the southwest coast is usually the drier choice, particularly in January and February.
- If you travel between May and September, an east-coast beach stay usually fits the weather better than the south-west.
- October and November bring inter-monsoon thunderstorms across the island, a raised flood risk and the start of the cyclone period.
- Whatever the month, check the sea conditions locally before swimming.

## Questions to ask your operator

- Given my travel dates, which parts of the itinerary fall in the wetter season, and which coast do you suggest for the beach days?
- If heavy rain or a landslip closes a road, what is the alternative route, and who pays for any extra night?
- How do you follow Department of Meteorology and Disaster Management Centre warnings during a trip, and how will you tell me about changes?
- Are the beaches you are planning safe for swimming at this time of year, and are any of them patrolled?
- Can the order of the itinerary be changed at short notice to follow the weather, and does that affect the price?
- What happens to my deposit if a cyclone or flooding forces you to cancel?
