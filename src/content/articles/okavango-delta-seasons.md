The Okavango Delta is unusual: its annual flood arrives in the middle of Botswana's dry season, peaking around June and July, rather than with the local rains. The Botswana Tourism Organisation calls April to October the best months to visit for weather and game viewing. The hot, wet summer from November to March brings a different and quieter experience.

## Why the delta floods in the dry season

The delta is fed by the Okavango River, which rises in the highlands of Angola. According to the Botswana Tourism Organisation, the river enters Botswana from Namibia at Mohembo, runs through a long channel called the Panhandle, and spreads across the fan-shaped delta at Seronga. The water never reaches the sea. As the IUCN World Heritage Outlook puts it, the waters disappear into the sands of the Kalahari.

What matters for visitors is the timing. UNESCO, as quoted in the IUCN assessment, notes that the annual flooding happens during the dry season, and that the flood is a critical life-force during the peak of the area's dry season (June and July). Plants and animals have adapted to this cycle. As a result, the delta is at its wettest while the surrounding country is at its driest.

The flood also changes the size of the delta. The Botswana Tourism Organisation says it covers about 15,000 km² in drier periods and as much as 22,000 km² in wetter ones. Flood levels vary from year to year.

## The seasons at a glance

The Botswana Tourism Organisation describes the climate as follows:

- **Summer, November to March:** hot and wet. Rain tends to fall in short, heavy, very local bursts, and January and February are usually the wettest months. Shade temperatures can reach 38°C or higher before the rains cool things down.
- **Winter, May to August:** the dry season, with almost no rain. Days are sunny and mild to warm, but without cloud cover nights can drop below freezing in some areas. Early-morning game drives are cold.
- **In between, April to early May and September to October:** generally dry, with days cooler than summer and nights warmer than winter. October is a transition month and can be very hot.

## What changes for wildlife viewing

According to the Botswana Tourism Organisation, from April to October wildlife gathers around the remaining water, and shorter grass in drier weather makes animals easier to see. In the delta, many mammals move during the drier winter months to the dryland areas in the south-east, around Chief's Island, the Moremi Tongue and the Matsebi Ridge.

Moremi Game Reserve covers about 3,900 km² in the central and eastern delta, including Chief's Island and the Moremi Tongue. Its mix of seasonal and permanent floodplains, lagoons and woodland is a big part of why the area changes so much through the year.

The tourism board also notes that game viewing in the delta is seasonal, depending on the time of year and the water and food available. A camp that is surrounded by water in one month may be on dry floodplain in another.

## How activities change with the water

The main activities in the delta are game viewing, birdwatching and boating, often in a mokoro, the traditional dug-out canoe. Whether boat and mokoro trips are possible, and where, depends on water levels at your camp. Some camps sit on permanent channels and others on seasonal floodplains. Ask your operator what the water usually does at each camp in the month you plan to travel.

The rainy season brings practical considerations too. The UK Foreign, Commonwealth & Development Office (FCDO) says the rains, from November to March, bring heavy downpours and a risk of flash flooding, which can make roads unsafe and close border crossings at short notice. For health, the FCDO lists malaria among the risks and notes reports in April 2025 that it had spread to areas where it is not usually present. Check current health advice and talk to a travel health professional before you go.

## Choosing your timing

- **For high water and the classic delta landscape:** plan around the flood, which peaks in June and July. Pack warm layers for cold mornings and nights.
- **For dry-season game viewing:** the Botswana Tourism Organisation points to April to October, when animals gather near water and vegetation is thinner.
- **For a green, hot season:** November to March brings rain and heat. Expect weather to affect road transfers and activities.

Flood size and timing vary from year to year, so treat any calendar as a guide rather than a promise.

## Questions to ask your operator

- How has the flood behaved in recent years at the camps you suggest, and what water levels should I expect in my month?
- Which camps are on permanent water and which on seasonal floodplain, and can I do mokoro or boat trips at each in my month?
- If there is too much or too little water, how would activities or transfers change?
- How do you handle transfers if rain or flooding closes roads or airstrips?
