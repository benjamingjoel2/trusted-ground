Pale limestone towers, scree slopes and high meadows strung with mountain huts: hiking in the Dolomites is about walking from one staffed rifugio to the next, with steel cables bolted to the rock where the trail turns vertical. The season is short and concentrated in summer, huts need booking, and via ferrata routes need proper climbing kit. Plan around afternoon storms and you will get far more out of it.

## A World Heritage mountain range

The Dolomites were added to the UNESCO World Heritage List on 26 June 2009, "for the beauty and uniqueness of their landscape and their geological and geomorphological importance", according to the Fondazione Dolomiti UNESCO. The site is not one block of mountains but nine separate systems, including the Marmolada, Puez-Odle, the Brenta Dolomites, Sciliar-Catinaccio and Latemar, the Northern Dolomites and the Dolomiti Friulane.

The range spreads across several provinces and regions: the foundation's members include the provinces of Belluno, Pordenone, Udine and the autonomous provinces of Trento and Bolzano (South Tyrol), plus the Veneto and Friuli Venezia Giulia regions. In practice, that means trail information, hut associations and tourism offices differ depending on which valley you start from. ## When the season runs

The hiking season is a summer affair. South Tyrol's tourism board describes July to September as the high season. In Trentino, the tourism board's 2024 press release said more than 140 alpine huts reopened from the weekend of 20 June, across a network of over 5,800 kilometres of paths.

Exact opening dates change every year and from hut to hut. Visit Trentino's advice is that the regional hut association gives general opening information, but "it is always best to contact the lodge directly (particularly at the beginning and end of the season)". Snow can linger on high passes early in summer, so check conditions for your specific route rather than assuming everything opens on the same day.

## Staying in a rifugio

A night in a hut is the heart of a multi-day Dolomites trek, and the rules are simple but strict:

- **Book ahead.** Visit Trentino states plainly that "it is always necessary to book to sleep in an alpine lodge". South Tyrol's tourism board says booking is strongly recommended in the July to September high season, especially for overnight stays.
- **Bring a sleeping-bag liner.** Visit Trentino says a liner is mandatory in most lodges, and that you should bring your own towel, slippers and toiletries. Confirm the requirements with the hut in advance.
- **Know the difference between huts.** Staffed lodges offer beds and meals; unstaffed bivouac huts are basic and, in Visit Trentino's words, "primarily only used in emergencies".

## Via ferrata: kit and experience

A via ferrata is a route equipped with fixed cables, ladders and anchors. The Autonomous Province of Trento describes them as "signposted routes of mountaineering interest that run mainly over rock or rough terrain", and says they require "good preparation and the use of specific mountaineering equipment".

The kit list is consistent across official sources:

- a climbing helmet
- a harness
- a certified via ferrata set with a shock-absorbing lanyard and carabiners
- gloves, which Visit Trentino calls "very important"

Visit Trentino also suggests carrying a rope and carabiner so you can secure yourself if you cannot continue. The province says each route has a designated person responsible for maintenance and monitoring, and publishes a list of routes and a trail map, so check a route's status before you set out. If you lack experience, hiring a qualified mountain guide is the sensible way to start.

## Weather, safety and mountain manners

Summer thunderstorms are the main hazard on exposed ridges and cables. Visit Trentino notes that storms "normally occur in the afternoon or evening", recommends hiking in the morning, and advises watching for towering cumulus clouds as a sign of unstable air. Read text forecasts rather than relying on weather icons, and turn back if in doubt. South Tyrol's tourism board suggests checking the mountain forecast the evening before or on the morning of a hike.

In an emergency, call 112. Visit Trentino says the number may work even without your own network's signal if another operator has coverage. Be ready to give your exact location, activity, number of people, injuries, weather and a callback number. South Tyrol also points walkers to the SOS EU ALP app.

The UK Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office (FCDO) warns that hiking and mountaineering carry specific risks and that you should check your insurance covers them. It advises leaving your itinerary with someone if you hike alone, and links to guidance on what to do if you see a brown bear while hiking in Trentino. Our article on [adventure travel insurance](/articles/adventure-travel-insurance/) covers what to look for in a policy.

Local authorities also ask visitors to stay on marked paths, take rubbish home, leave flowers unpicked and keep noise down so wildlife is not disturbed.

## Before you go

- Book every hut night in advance and confirm opening dates directly with each rifugio, especially in June and September.
- Pack a sleeping-bag liner, towel and slippers for hut stays.
- For via ferrata routes, carry a helmet, harness, certified via ferrata set and gloves, or hire a qualified mountain guide.
- Start early to be off exposed ground before afternoon storms, and check a text forecast the night before.
- Save 112 in your phone and check that your insurance covers hiking and via ferrata.
