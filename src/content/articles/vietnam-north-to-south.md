Vietnam stretches a long way from north to south, and its three regions do not share a single wet season. The north and south get most of their rain from roughly June to September, while central Vietnam is wettest from about September or October to December. Vietnam's official tourism site says March to May offers the best weather countrywide. A trip at other times works too if you plan the route around the regions' different seasons.

## Three climates in one country

From the misty limestone of Ha Long Bay to the rice paddies of Sa Pa, the old capital of Hue and the canals of the Mekong Delta, Vietnam packs very different landscapes into one trip. The weather varies just as much. The official tourism site, vietnam.travel, notes that some part of the country usually has good weather whatever the time of year. The UK Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office (FCDO) gives the rainy season as June to September in the north and south, and October to December in central Vietnam.

## The north: Hanoi, Ha Long Bay and Sa Pa

According to vietnam.travel:

- **Hanoi** is at its best from April to June and from September to December. Summers are humid and rainy, with highs near 40°C in June. From late December to March, lows fall to around 10°C.
- **Ha Long Bay** has misty, cool to cold winters, with the most sunshine in spring and autumn. The site suggests April to June or September to November, and warns that summer storms can cancel cruises. January can be very cold on the water.
- **Sa Pa** is the coolest part of the country, especially from late December to March, and occasionally sees snow in January. The site calls late April "near-perfect", and early October brings golden rice terraces before the harvest.

## The centre: Hue, Hoi An and the coast

Central Vietnam runs on a different calendar, and this is the stretch where timing matters most.

- **Hue** has a rainy season from September to February, and floods are possible from September to November. Summer is dry, with July highs of about 35°C.
- **Hoi An** is rainy and cool from September to January, with small floods possible in autumn. February to August is warm to hot and sunny.
- **Nha Trang** gets most of its rain from September to December, and vietnam.travel says it has more than 300 days of sunshine a year.
- **Da Lat**, in the highlands, stays mild all year. Its rainy season runs from April to October.

The FCDO says seasonal flooding hits central Vietnam in October and November. It warns that floods, mudslides and landslides can block roads and disrupt overland travel.

## The south: Ho Chi Minh City, the Mekong and Phu Quoc

In the south it is around 30°C all year, says vietnam.travel. Ho Chi Minh City and the Mekong Delta have a wet season from May to November, usually with a dependable afternoon downpour lasting a few hours. December to April is clear and sunny. The FCDO adds that flooding is also common in the Mekong Delta and around Hanoi.

**Phu Quoc** is hot all year. It is stormy from July to September, when ferry crossings can be dangerous, and clear and sunny from October to June. December and January are especially pleasant.

## Typhoons and planning the route

The FCDO says tropical cyclones affect Vietnam's east coast, normally from May to November, though they can happen at other times. The official source for forecasts and storm warnings is Vietnam's National Centre for Hydro-Meteorological Forecasting (in Vietnamese). Check it, and your airline or boat operator, as departure approaches.

Ways to travel the length of the country, according to vietnam.travel:

- **By train.** The Reunification Express, completed by the French in 1936, links Hanoi and Ho Chi Minh City. It stops at Hue, Da Nang, Nha Trang and Phan Thiet, and offers hard seat, soft seat, hard sleeper and soft sleeper classes. The full run takes well over a day, so most travellers break it into sections.
- **By plane.** There are daily flights to all the main destinations, which can save days on a short trip.
- **By bus.** Open tour buses, mostly sleeper buses or buses with deep reclining seats, run between Hanoi, Hue, Hoi An, Nha Trang, Da Lat, Mui Ne and Ho Chi Minh City. Book a day or two ahead, or at least two weeks ahead during Vietnamese holidays.

The FCDO notes that bus and coach crashes are more likely at night, and that petty theft happens on buses and trains, especially overnight trains. Motorbike accidents are common and have killed and injured British nationals.

As a rule of thumb from these seasons, a trip in the second half of the year might spend more time in the north and south. One early in the year can take in central beaches and Hoi An once the rains ease. Fixing your dates first and then shaping the route around them usually works better than forcing a fixed route into the wrong season. ## Before you go

- Match each leg of your route to that region's dry months, not the country as a whole.
- Leave slack in the itinerary from September to December for flood or storm delays in central Vietnam.
- Check storm warnings before booking boat trips to Ha Long Bay or ferries to Phu Quoc.
- Pack warm layers for the north and Sa Pa between December and March.
- Prefer daytime road travel where you can, and keep valuables close on overnight trains.
- Book trains and buses well ahead around Vietnamese public holidays.
