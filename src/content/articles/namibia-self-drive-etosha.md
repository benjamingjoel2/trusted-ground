Namibia is well set up for driving yourself, and the main sights, including Etosha, Sossusvlei and Swakopmund, can be reached on paved or gravel roads without four-wheel drive. The catch is the gravel itself: the UK Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office (FCDO) warns that accidents on gravel and dirt roads are common. Plan shorter days than the map suggests, keep to the speed advice, and know Etosha's rules before you arrive at the gate.

## Gravel roads: the real risk

Many Namibian routes include long stretches of gravel. The FCDO's advice for drivers is specific, particularly in the rainy season from January to April, when many gravel roads deteriorate. It recommends that you:

- do not go faster than 80 km/h
- carry two spare tyres for punctures
- carry plenty of water
- check road conditions before setting off
- avoid driving at night outside towns, because wildlife and livestock are serious hazards

Even in the dry months, loose surfaces, corrugations and sudden dips reward a slow, steady pace. Allow time for stops, photographs and the occasional tyre change, and aim to arrive before dark.

## Choosing a vehicle and checking the insurance

The Namibia Tourism Board's self-drive planning guide sets out the main trade-offs:

- **4WD vehicles** cost more to hire and run, but have better ground clearance and are usually fitted with tyres better suited to Namibian roads.
- **2WD vehicles** have less clearance and carry less, but the major attractions are reachable in one.
- **Camping vehicles** (2WD or 4WD) come equipped with tents and kitchen kit.
- **Motor homes** suit tarred roads better, as they tend to be top-heavy with poor clearance.

The same guide suggests vehicle spares should include a spare wheel (preferably two), an air compressor or pump, a tyre gauge, jump leads, a tow rope, a shovel and a basic toolkit.

Insurance needs careful reading. The FCDO advises taking out comprehensive cover and notes that most hire policies will not cover accidents that do not involve another vehicle or an animal. On gravel, a single-vehicle rollover is exactly the kind of accident that happens, so ask what is excluded and what excess you would pay. See our guide to travel insurance for what your own policy should cover.

On paperwork, the FCDO says UK visitors must have their valid UK photocard driving licence with them in the car. The Tourism Board's guide also mentions an international driving licence, so confirm with your hire company exactly which documents it requires. Since 1 April 2025 UK passport holders need a visa for Namibia, available on arrival or as an e-visa; check the FCDO entry requirements page for the current position.

## Etosha National Park: the rules that catch drivers out

Etosha is Namibia's flagship park, covering 22,935 km² according to the Ministry of Environment, Forestry and Tourism (MEFT). Its rest camps, run by Namibia Wildlife Resorts (NWR), include Okaukuejo, Halali, Namutoni, Dolomite, Onkoshi and Olifantsrus campsite. Many self-drivers stay inside the park and drive between waterholes.

The park regulations published by NWR list what is against the law. The ones that matter most to self-drivers are:

- **Sunrise to sunset only.** You may not leave a rest camp before sunrise or reach one after sunset, or cross the park boundary between sunset and sunrise. Plan each day's game drive so you are back in camp, or out of the gate, in time.
- **Stay in the vehicle.** Outside a rest camp or designated campsite you may not get out of, or hang out of, your vehicle.
- **Stay on marked roads** and keep to the official speed limit.
- **No overnighting** anywhere other than a rest camp.
- **No disturbing wildlife**, collecting plants, littering, or making noise that disturbs others.
- **No open vehicles** unless fitted with a cage or other effective protection, no pets, and no hitch-hikers.

The regulations also state that arrival must be reported to the nearest park management office, and that your visit is at your own risk. Night drives are offered by the resorts as guided activities, not something you can do in your own car.

Park entry fees change, so we have not quoted them here. MEFT says fees can be paid at its headquarters, at regional offices or on arrival at the park; check the current rates with NWR or MEFT, or ask your operator whether they are included in your package.

## Distances, driving days and fuel

Namibia is large and sparsely populated, and the map distances are misleading on gravel. Rather than relying on a figure from a website, ask your operator for realistic driving times for each leg at gravel-road speeds, and build in rest days. The Tourism Board's example two-week route uses a circuit (Windhoek, Etosha, Damaraland, the Skeleton Coast, Swakopmund, Sossusvlei and back) specifically to avoid long distances and backtracking.

Fuel and money need planning:

- **Fill up whenever you can**, and ask your operator about fuel availability on remote legs. The Tourism Board's guide advises packing water containers or jerry cans, and notes that there are no ATMs in small towns and villages.
- **Cash and cards.** Cards are accepted in towns and larger lodges but may not be in smaller shops, so carry some Namibian dollars.
- **Guard the car at fuel stops.** The FCDO warns that theft from vehicles, particularly at service stations, is common. Do not leave the vehicle unattended, keep doors locked and keep valuables out of sight.

## Questions to ask your operator

- How long will each driving day really take at gravel-road speeds, and does any leg risk arriving after dark?
- What does the hire insurance exclude, particularly single-vehicle accidents, tyres, windscreens and the underbody, and what is the excess?
- Does the vehicle come with two spare tyres, a compressor and recovery kit, and what happens if it breaks down in a remote area?
- Are Etosha rest camps booked inside the park, and are park entry fees included or payable on arrival?
- Where are the reliable fuel stops on my route, and where should I carry extra fuel or water?
- Will road conditions on my route be affected by the rains if I travel between January and April?
