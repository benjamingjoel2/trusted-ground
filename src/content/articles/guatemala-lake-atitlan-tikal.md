Guatemala's two great draws lie far apart: Lake Atitlán in the western highlands and Tikal in the lowland forests of Petén, about 484 km north of Guatemala City. Tikal's tickets can be bought online from the Ministry of Culture and Sports, and sunrise and sunset visits need a separate ticket. On the roads between the main sites, the UK government's advice on crime, buses and travel after dark matters as much as the itinerary.

## Tikal: a Maya city in the forest

Tikal National Park was created on 26 May 1955, according to the Agencia Guatemalteca de Noticias (AGN), Guatemala's state news agency, which made it the country's first protected area. UNESCO recognised it as a World Heritage site in 1979. The AGN notes that it was the first site UNESCO listed as mixed cultural and natural heritage. The park covers 575.83 square kilometres of tropical forest around what the AGN describes as the largest and most monumental Maya city.

Researchers cited by the AGN date the first settlement to around 1750 BC, with continuous Maya occupation until about AD 900. They estimate the city had about 100,000 inhabitants around AD 750. Visitors today come for the plazas, temples and ball court, and for the wildlife of the surrounding forest. The AGN also notes that contemporary Maya religious ceremonies are still held within the park, so treat any ceremony you come across with quiet respect.

## Tickets, hours and sunrise visits

According to the AGN, the park is open daily from 6am to 6pm. Extra sessions run from 4am to 6am for sunrise and from 6pm to 8pm for sunset, and each needs its own ticket. The Ministry of Culture and Sports (MCD) sells tickets online at boletos.culturaguate.com. Foreign visitors book with their passport, and the AGN reports that Visa and Mastercard are accepted. Buying in advance avoids queues on the day.

On fees, the AGN reported in May 2024 that foreign visitors paid 150 quetzales for park entry and 100 quetzales each for sunrise and sunset tickets. Prices can change, so check the current figures on the MCD ticket site before you go.

Most visitors reach Tikal from Flores. You can travel by road or fly to Mundo Maya International Airport. A sunrise visit means a very early start, so arrange transport the day before.

## Lake Atitlán: protecting a highland lake

Lake Atitlán sits in Guatemala's western highlands. Its basin has its own government authority, AMSCLAE (the Authority for the Sustainable Management of the Lake Atitlán Basin and its Environment). AMSCLAE was created by law in 1996 to plan and coordinate public and private action to conserve the lake and its ecosystems.

The lake is under pressure. In November 2026, AMSCLAE announced a strategic plan for its recovery, working with INGUAT (Guatemala's tourism institute) and other bodies. The plan has four priorities: solid waste, wastewater treatment, sustainable drinking water and sustainable tourism. Visitors can help in simple ways:

- Refill bottles rather than buying single-use plastic.
- Take your rubbish away with you.
- Choose locally guided activities.

AMSCLAE and INGUAT have recently trained local guides in birdwatching, timing the course to the autumn migration. Birdwatching with a local guide is a good way to see the basin and support the people who live there.

## Getting around safely

At the time of writing (November 2026), the FCDO advises against all but essential travel only to areas near the Mexican border, so Tikal and Lake Atitlán are outside those zones. Its wider safety advice is still important:

- **Road routes.** The FCDO reports armed attacks or robberies on most tourist routes between Guatemala City, Antigua, Tikal, Petén and Lake Atitlán. Avoid travelling at night, stay on main roads and travel in convoy where possible.
- **Buses.** The FCDO advises avoiding public buses, which are repainted US school buses. Private intercity coaches are generally safer, but gangs have targeted them too.
- **Taxis.** Use radio-dispatched taxis, Uber where available or hotel taxis. Pre-paid taxi vouchers are sold at the INGUAT office in the airport arrivals hall.
- **Volcano hikes.** Before climbing, check the websites of the Guatemalan Meteorological Office and CONRED, the disaster agency. The FCDO warns that local tour organisers tend to underestimate the risks, that deaths have occurred and that there is no mountain rescue service.
- **Help.** INGUAT's tourist assistance service, PROATUR, can help victims of crime.

## Before you go

- Buy Tikal tickets, including any sunrise or sunset ticket, on the MCD site, and carry your passport.
- Plan road journeys between Atitlán, Antigua and Petén for daylight hours, using private transport or reputable coaches.
- Before any volcano hike, check the Guatemalan Meteorological Office and CONRED, and ask your guide about safety equipment and what happens in an emergency.
- Pack warm, waterproof layers for the highlands and early-morning starts.
- Take a refillable bottle to Lake Atitlán and avoid single-use plastics.
- Read the FCDO's Guatemala advice the week before you travel.
