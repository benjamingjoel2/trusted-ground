Turquoise lakes spill into one another over curtains of waterfalls, wooden boardwalks skim just above the water, and forest closes in on every side. Plitvice Lakes is Croatia's most famous national park, and it is popular enough that entry is now managed by time slot. Buy your ticket online from the park's own webshop, arrive within your slot, and stay on the boardwalks.

## Why Plitvice is protected

According to the park, the World Heritage Committee inscribed Plitvice Lakes National Park on the UNESCO World Heritage List on 26 October 1979. Its special feature is tufa, also called travertine: mineral deposits that build natural dams across the watercourses and have, in the park's words, "created a series of lakes, barriers, and waterfalls".

The IUCN World Heritage Outlook, which assesses natural World Heritage sites, gives more detail. The park has 16 lakes: 12 Upper Lakes sitting on dolomite and four Lower Lakes in a limestone canyon. The tufa-building process is still going on, and it depends on water quality, temperature and pH, which is one reason the rules on touching the water are strict. Brown bear, wolf and lynx live in the surrounding forests, along with 168 bird species and 22 bat species.

The same assessment records how visitor numbers have grown, from about 350,000 in 1998 to 1.45 million in 2023, with pressure described as "extremely high" in June, July and August. The park's responses include daily caps, hourly entry limits at each entrance and online booking. For more on travelling in the country, see our [Croatia guide](/countries/croatia/).

## How timed entry works

The park says it is "highly recommended" to buy tickets online through the official Plitvice Lakes National Park webshop. The key rules, from the park's FAQ:

- **You choose an entrance and a time.** Online tickets must be validated at the entrance you selected when buying, within the time window shown on the ticket.
- **You cannot go in early.** Entering before your scheduled time is not allowed. Arriving late is tolerated for "an hour at the most".
- **Keep your ticket ready.** Store it on your phone or print it. The price list warns that visiting with an inactive ticket counts as having no valid ticket.
- **Card payment only online.** The webshop accepts credit and debit cards; some banks ask for an authorisation code from a token or banking app.
- **Walk-up tickets are not guaranteed.** Tickets are sold at the entrances in cash or by card only if still available, and queues can be longer in peak season.

The panorama train and electric boat rides inside the park are included in the ticket price.

## What tickets cost

Prices depend heavily on the season. According to the park's 2026 price list, an adult day ticket costs:

| Period | Adult day ticket |
|---|---|
| 1 January to 31 March, 1 November to 31 December | €10 |
| 1 April to 31 May, 1 October to 31 October | €23 |
| 1 June to 30 September, earlier entry ("price A") | €40 |
| 1 June to 30 September, later entry ("price B") | €25 |

In June to August, price A applies until 4pm and the cheaper price B after 4pm; in September the cut-off is 3pm. Students and children aged 7 to 18 pay less, and children under 7 enter free, as do people with impaired mobility or a disability of over 50 per cent and their assistants, with valid documents. Two-day tickets are also available, valid for two consecutive days. Check the official price list before you buy, as fares are set each year.

## Entrances and trails

There are three entrances: Entrance 1, Entrance 2 and the auxiliary Flora entrance. Luggage can be left free of charge at all three, and buses stop near Entrance 1 and Entrance 2 on the D1 state road.

The park has four hiking trails, and tours are self-guided: you simply follow the markings for your chosen route, and no guide is required. The park can arrange guided tours on request. Wheelchair access is not available throughout the park, but staff at the entrance can recommend the most accessible route.

Seasons change what you can see. In winter, the park says, the Lower Lakes are usually open and the Upper Lakes closed, with the Upper Lakes usually reopening in early April, depending on the weather.

## Park rules

- **No swimming.** Swimming in the lakes is "strictly prohibited".
- **Dogs on leads.** Pets are allowed but must be kept on a leash.
- **No drones for aerial photos.** The park's FAQ prohibits "guided aircraft" for aerial photography; commercial photography and filming need a request under its photography protocol.
- **Stay on marked routes.** The UK Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office (FCDO) warns that landmines remain in "more remote areas of the Plitvice Lakes National Park" and advises never stepping off marked paths onto uncultivated land.
- **Fire.** The FCDO notes that outdoor fires are restricted from 1 June to 31 October except in designated areas. In an emergency, call 112.

## Before you go

- Buy your ticket from the official park webshop, not a reseller, and note your entrance and time slot.
- Arrive on time: early entry is refused and late arrival is tolerated for an hour at most.
- In summer, compare price A and price B and consider a later slot for the cheaper price B.
- Check whether the Upper Lakes are open if you are travelling between late autumn and early spring.
- Wear sturdy shoes, stay on the boardwalks and marked trails, and leave the swimming for the coast.
