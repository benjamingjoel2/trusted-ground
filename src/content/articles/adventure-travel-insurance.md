A standard travel insurance policy is rarely enough for an adventure trip. Before you travel, check that your policy names the activities you will actually do, covers the altitude or depth you will reach, and pays for emergency evacuation from remote places. Buy it as soon as you book, and read the exclusions before you pay.

## Why a standard policy may not cover you

The UK Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office (FCDO) advises buying travel insurance that covers "any activities you will be doing whilst you are away". It warns that sports and adventure tourism may need "specialist insurance or an add-on", and that activities such as bungee jumping, jet skiing, winter sports and skydiving "are not usually included in standard policies".

The FCDO also points out what going without cover can cost. Its examples include a quad bike accident in Greece needing surgery and evacuation at £80,000 or more, and hospital treatment in the USA at £150,000 or more.

For an adventure trip, that means:

- **List every activity.** Trekking, high-altitude walking, scuba diving, rafting and climbing are often treated separately. If an activity is not named in the policy or an add-on, assume it is not covered and ask the insurer.
- **Declare health conditions.** The FCDO says failing to declare existing physical or mental health conditions, including those under investigation, may invalidate your policy. TravelHealthPro, the UK's national travel health service, gives the same advice.
- **Check the destination.** Many policies will not cover travel to places where the FCDO advises against all but essential travel or all travel. Check the [FCDO advice](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice) for your destination when you buy the policy and again before you leave.

## Altitude: check the limit, not just the word "trekking"

Altitude illness is a real risk on mountain trips. The FCDO notes it is a risk in parts of Nepal, "particularly in tourist and trekking areas", and lists hazards that apply all year round, especially above 3,000m: sudden weather changes, avalanches, landslides, glacial crevasses and altitude sickness.

Do not assume the word "trekking" in a policy covers every trek. Ask the insurer whether cover is limited by altitude, and check any limit against the highest point on your itinerary, including passes and side trips. Get the answer in writing.

TravelHealthPro advises travellers to take out "an appropriate level of travel health insurance that includes repatriation". Its [altitude illness factsheet](https://travelhealthpro.org.uk/factsheet/43/altitude-illness) is a useful briefing to read before you go.

## Evacuation and rescue: the costliest part

In remote places, the expensive part of an accident is usually getting out. In Nepal, the FCDO says healthcare is poor outside the Kathmandu Valley and Pokhara. It also says rapid helicopter evacuation may be hard to arrange from remote areas, and that it usually costs "between £1,000 and £2,000 or more per flying hour" (FCDO, at the time of writing in November 2026). It tells trekkers to "make sure your insurance includes cover for mountain rescue services and helicopter costs".

The FCDO's general guidance says to check whether your policy covers:

- emergency transport, which is often charged separately from other medical costs
- treatment in both state and private hospitals
- travel home on medical grounds, and getting home if you cannot use your original ticket
- every country you pass through, even in transit

Carry your policy number and the insurer's 24-hour emergency assistance number, and give both to your guide or operator and to someone at home.

The FCDO also warns of a specific scam in Nepal: some trekking guides have taken inexperienced trekkers too high too quickly, then called in expensive helicopter evacuations and taken a cut. It advises being wary of trekking deals that look very cheap. Read your policy's procedure for arranging an evacuation, and ask the insurer whether they need to be called first.

## Diving and other water sports

The FCDO's general rule applies to diving too: check that the policy covers "all activities you may undertake on holiday", and buy an add-on or a specialist policy if it does not. Ask the insurer directly whether cover has a maximum depth, whether it depends on your certification level, and whether treatment for diving injuries, including any decompression treatment, is included.

## Booking direct with a local operator

Booking a trek or expedition direct with an operator in the destination country can work well. But it changes what protects you if something goes wrong before you travel.

Citizens Advice explains that if you book at least two parts of a holiday with the same company at the same time, "it's probably a package holiday". UK package holiday rules give you rights if the company makes a significant change or you cannot travel because of exceptional circumstances. Before you book direct with a company based abroad, ask which rules, if any, protect your booking, and whether the parts you are buying from different companies count as a package.

The FCDO also notes that travel insurance "typically" does not cover an airline or travel agent going out of business. Check whether your policy includes supplier failure cover, or how else your payment is protected. Our guides to deposits and payment protection and [paying a local operator abroad](/articles/paying-a-local-operator-abroad/) explain the options.

Check the cancellation section of your policy carefully. If your operator takes a non-refundable deposit, check whether your policy would cover it. Citizens Advice notes that you may be able to claim for a cancelled trip under travel insurance, depending on your policy. ## Questions to ask your operator

- What is the highest altitude or greatest depth on this itinerary, including side trips?
- What is your procedure if someone develops altitude sickness, and who decides to call a helicopter evacuation?
- Will your guides contact my insurer's emergency line before arranging an evacuation?
- How do your guides communicate from areas without mobile signal?
- Which parts of the trip are you selling me directly, and which are booked with other companies?
- What are your cancellation terms, and how is my deposit protected if you cannot run the trip?
