Switzerland's mountain railways are a destination in their own right: trains spiral through loop tunnels, cross stone viaducts above gorges and climb past glaciers to lakes near the tree line. The short answer for planners is that the famous panoramic trains are ordinary scheduled services that need a valid ticket plus a paid seat reservation, and most are fully covered by the Swiss Travel Pass. Seasons matter more than many people expect: some routes pause or change in winter.

## The four classic panoramic routes

Switzerland Tourism highlights four journeys. Each runs in both directions.

**Glacier Express.** St. Moritz to Zermatt via Chur, Andermatt and Brig: 291 km in about 8 hours, through 91 tunnels.

**Bernina Express.** Chur or St. Moritz to Tirano in Italy: 148 km in about 4 hours 21 minutes, through 55 tunnels and over more than 196 bridges. The route runs through the Rhaetian Railway UNESCO World Heritage site. Highlights named by Switzerland Tourism include the 65-metre Landwasser Viaduct, the Montebello curve, views of the Morteratsch glacier, the lakes around Lago Bianco, Alp Grüm and the Brusio circular viaduct.

**GoldenPass Express.** Interlaken to Montreux via Gstaad and Château-d'Oex in 3 hours 15 minutes, with up to four trips a day. The two sections use different track gauges; adjustable bogies mean you stay in the same train throughout.

**Gotthard Panorama Express.** A train-and-boat combination: panoramic train between Lugano or Bellinzona and Flüelen or Arth-Goldau, then a steamboat or motor vessel across Lake Lucerne. In total it is 182 km and about 5 hours 30 minutes. The line's loop tunnels let you see the church at Wassen from three different angles.

## Tickets, passes and reservations

Switzerland Tourism says all four routes are fully covered by the Swiss Travel Pass, the Swiss Travel Pass Flex and the GA travelcard. What the pass does not cover is the seat reservation:

- **Glacier Express** and **Bernina Express**: a valid ticket and a seat reservation are both required, and a route-dependent surcharge applies.
- **GoldenPass Express**: reservations are mandatory in Prestige class and strongly recommended in first and second class, all with a surcharge.
- **Gotthard Panorama Express**: the panoramic section between Flüelen and Lugano is first class only, so second-class ticket holders need an upgrade as well as the reservation surcharge.

According to the Swiss Travel System, the Swiss Travel Pass gives unlimited travel on trains, buses and boats for 3, 4, 6, 8 or 15 consecutive days, while the Flex version is for non-consecutive days within a month. It includes free trips up Rigi, Stanserhorn and Stoos, up to 50% off most other mountain railways, and entry to more than 500 museums. Prices are set in Swiss francs and published in several currencies; check the current figure on the official site.

## Practical details on board

A few rules catch people out:

- On the Glacier Express, Switzerland Tourism says dogs are not permitted and each person may bring only one piece of luggage.
- The Bernina Express has no dining car: lunch is taken in Tirano, and there is a minibar on board.
- Because the Bernina Express ends in Italy, you need a passport or identity card.
- An Excellence Class on the Glacier Express carries an extra surcharge on top of a first-class ticket; check the current amount when booking.

## Seasons and timetables

All the panoramic trains run on separate summer and winter timetables, so a plan made from a summer schedule may not work in January.

- **Gotthard Panorama Express** runs from April to October only. Outside that window, the Treno Gottardo covers the same historic route year-round as a regular train.
- **Glacier Express**: Switzerland Tourism notes that no trains operate on certain dates from November to December. Check the timetable before fixing dates.
- **Bernina Express Bus**, the road extension from Tirano to Lugano (90 km, about 3 hours 20 minutes), runs from March to October.

Winter brings snow-covered scenery but also mountain hazards off the train. The FCDO advises that adventure sports in the Swiss Alps carry real risks and that insurance should include mountain rescue, helicopter costs and repatriation, and it points to its guidance on avalanches for anyone skiing or hiking at altitude.

## Building an itinerary

The routes join up naturally. The Glacier Express and Bernina Express both touch St. Moritz and Chur, so you can link Zermatt to Tirano over two days. The GoldenPass Express links Interlaken with Montreux, and the Gotthard Panorama Express links Lugano with Lucerne. A consecutive-day pass suits a tightly packed rail loop; the Flex version suits a trip with rest days in one base.

## Before you go

- Reserve panoramic seats as soon as your dates are firm; reservation is separate from your ticket or pass.
- Check the summer or winter timetable for your exact travel dates.
- Carry a passport or ID card for the Bernina Express into Italy.
- Pack light for the Glacier Express, which allows one piece of luggage per person.
- If you plan to hike or ski, confirm your insurance covers mountain rescue and helicopter evacuation.
