At Murchison Falls the Victoria Nile, after racing west through some 80km of rapids, is squeezed through a gap in the rift valley cliffs only a few metres wide and drops 45 metres into a pool known as the Devil's Cauldron. Below it, the river turns broad and slow, and hippos, Nile crocodiles, elephants and buffalo gather along its banks. The park is Uganda's oldest and largest conservation area, and most visits combine a boat trip on the Nile with game drives on the savanna north of the river.

## The park at a glance

The Uganda Wildlife Authority (UWA) says the area was first gazetted as a game reserve in 1926 and became one of Uganda's first national parks in 1952. It lies at the northern end of the Albertine Rift, where the Bunyoro escarpment drops to palm-dotted plains. UWA's park brochure gives the national park's area as 3,840 square kilometres; together with the neighbouring Karuma and Bugungu wildlife reserves it is managed as the Murchison Falls Conservation Area.

The Nile splits the park in two, and the landscapes differ on each side:

- **North of the river:** open savanna, Borassus palms, acacia and riverine woodland. This is where most game driving happens, and where UWA says giraffes have historically been found.
- **South of the river:** bush, woodland and forest patches, including the Kaniyo Pabidi section of Budongo Forest.

UWA's current park page counts 144 mammal species and 556 bird species. It reports more than 900 elephants from an aerial survey, buffalo numbers above 10,000 and around 35,000 Uganda kob.

## On the water: the Nile boat trips

Paraa, on the riverbank, is the park's tourism hub; UWA's brochure notes the name means "home of the hippo" in the local Luo language. A vehicle ferry crosses here at intervals through the day, linking the southern road from Masindi with the northern game tracks, and both main boat trips start here.

- **Upriver to the falls.** UWA calls the launch trip from Paraa to the foot of the falls the park's oldest and most popular excursion. Its brochure describes a three-hour return trip past resident hippos and crocodiles and visiting elephants and buffalo. Trips are run by UWA and by private operators.
- **Downriver to the delta.** Boats also head west towards Lake Albert, where the Nile breaks into channels between wetland islands. This is the place to look for the shoebill; UWA says it is best sighted here in the December to February and June to September dry seasons.

The **Top of the Falls** viewpoint is reachable by road, but UWA suggests a more rewarding route: leave the launch below the falls and climb through Fajao Gorge on foot. A ranger escort is required for that walk.

## Game drives and other activities

UWA describes the **Buligi Peninsula**, a triangle of grassland between the Victoria Nile and the Albert Nile, as the park's prime game viewing area. Expect elephant, buffalo, antelope and giraffe; lion and leopard are possible but not guaranteed. The tracks converge at Delta Point, where you can stop to watch waterbirds and distant hippos.

South of the river, the **Heart of Murchison** is a tract of savanna in the centre of the park, about 20km east of the Masindi to Paraa road. UWA's brochure says newer vehicle tracks there, and large herds of Uganda kob, have made it known as a place for lions.

Other options listed by UWA include:

- **Chimpanzee tracking** with forest guides at Kaniyo Pabidi, which is also a strong birding site for Albertine Rift species.
- **Sport fishing** below the falls, where UWA records a rod-caught Nile perch of 108kg.
- **Ziwa Rhino Sanctuary**, outside the park near the Kampala to Gulu road, home to Uganda's only wild rhinos.

## When to go and how to get there

UWA's brochure recommends the dry seasons from December to late February and from June to September, when animals concentrate around water. It says January to March is prime time for birdwatching, with fewer visitors. Daytime temperatures are around 25 to 32°C, making this one of the hottest parts of Uganda, with nights near 18°C. Rain is infrequent but can arrive as torrential storms.

By road, UWA puts the park 305km north of Kampala, four to five hours to the southern gate, with another hour or two from the gate to most lodges. From Fort Portal in the south-west, allow at least six hours. Northern gates (Chobe, Wankwar and Tangi) are reached from the Kampala to Pakwach road. Charter and scheduled flights from Entebbe or Kajjansi can land at Pakuba Airfield, the most convenient for the lodges around Paraa.

UWA operates a campsite and dormitory at North Paraa, and camping is possible at the Top of the Falls; privately run lodges are in and around the park. The 2024 to 2026 UWA tariff that set entry and activity fees ran to June 2026, so check the current tariff on the UWA website rather than relying on older figures. UWA says 20 per cent of park fees go to communities in parishes bordering the park.

## Safety and health

The UK Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office (FCDO) says terrorists are very likely to try to carry out attacks in Uganda, and that past attacks have included national parks, notably in western Uganda near the border with the Democratic Republic of the Congo. It also warns that road travel can be hazardous and advises against travelling outside main towns after dark. Ferry accidents on Lake Albert and Lake Victoria have killed many passengers; the FCDO advises not boarding a vessel that looks overloaded.

On health, the FCDO notes that the World Health Organisation declared Uganda's latest Ebola outbreak over on 27 August 2026, but that an outbreak continues in the DRC and screening may apply at entry points including Entebbe. ## Before you go

- Read the latest [FCDO Uganda advice](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/uganda), including the health page, and make sure your insurance covers your itinerary.
- Check current park entry and activity fees on the UWA tariff, not an old brochure or price list.
- Ask whether your launch trip to the falls is run by UWA or a private operator, how long it lasts and how many people the boat carries.
- If you want the Top of the Falls hike, confirm a ranger escort is arranged and plan for midday heat.
- Plan road journeys to arrive before dark, and allow time for the Paraa ferry crossing.
- For the shoebill, time a delta trip for the dry seasons UWA recommends.
