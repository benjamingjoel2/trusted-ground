Rwanda is famous for its mountain gorillas, but a trip built only around gorillas misses a great deal. In the same volcanic highlands you can track golden monkeys through the bamboo; in the far south-west, Nyungwe's rainforest has a suspension bridge strung above the treetops; and in the east, Akagera offers a classic savannah safari with lakes, hippos and the Big Five.

## Golden monkeys in Volcanoes National Park

Volcanoes National Park, in the far north-west, protects the steep slopes of the Virunga Massif: a chain of dormant volcanoes made up of Karisimbi (the highest, at 4,507m), Bisoke with its crater lake, Sabinyo, Gahinga and Muhabura. According to the Rwanda Development Board's Visit Rwanda site, the park's habitats range from evergreen and bamboo forest to open grassland, swamp and heath.

Golden monkeys live here and in Gishwati Forest. In Volcanoes, Visit Rwanda says there are two groups that can be tracked: one on the slopes of Mount Karisimbi and one in the bamboo forest on Mount Sabyinyo. Tracking is a standalone excursion that departs from the Kinigi headquarters once each day.

What to expect:

- **Clothing.** Visit Rwanda recommends sturdy clothing, solid walking shoes and, in case of rain, gaiters and a lightweight waterproof.
- **Booking.** Primate permits can be booked online, at a Rwanda Development Board office or through a tour operator. Permit prices change, so check the current figure on the official booking channels rather than relying on older guidebooks.
- **Combining activities.** Visit Rwanda also lists hiking, Buhanga Eco-Park and village experiences within the park, which makes it easy to fill a second or third day around a gorilla trek.

For gorilla permits themselves, see our article on [gorilla trekking permits](/articles/gorilla-trekking-permits/).

## Nyungwe: ancient rainforest and the canopy walkway

Nyungwe, in the south-west, is one of Rwanda's great forests and is now a World Heritage site. The IUCN, which assesses natural World Heritage sites, describes it as an important area for rainforest conservation in Central Africa, with intact forest, peat bogs, moors, thickets and grassland. It records 14 primate species, more than 1,400 native vascular plant species and 351 bird species, and rates the park's conservation outlook as "good with some concerns".

The best-known experience here is the canopy walkway. Visit Rwanda describes it as a suspension bridge 160m long and 70m high over a ravine in the montane forest, reached on a guided walk along the Igishigishigi trail. The walk from the Uwinka Reception Centre takes about an hour, past butterflies and orchids.

Practical notes from the official site:

- The walkway is open all year, with more chance of rain in the afternoons.
- Footpaths are well maintained but steep, and can be muddy after rain.
- Boots, raincoats and walking sticks can be rented at the park headquarters.

**Primates in Nyungwe.** Chimpanzees are the main draw. Visit Rwanda says tracking starts early, at 5.30am, can take anything from an hour to several hours, and visitors spend a maximum of one hour with the chimpanzees once they are found. The terrain is steep, slippery and muddy, so a reasonable level of fitness matters. The forest also holds a large group of colobus monkeys, owl-faced monkeys, Dent's monkeys, blue monkeys, vervets and L'Hoest's monkeys, which are endemic to the Albertine Rift.

## Akagera: Rwanda's savannah park

Akagera, on the eastern border with Tanzania, feels nothing like the misty highlands. Visit Rwanda describes just over a thousand square kilometres of forest-fringed lakes, papyrus swamps, savannah plains and rolling hills, containing central Africa's largest protected wetland.

The park has been managed since 2010 as a joint venture between the Rwanda Development Board and African Parks, a non-profit conservation organisation. Lions were reintroduced in 2015 and 18 eastern black rhinos in 2017, and Akagera is once again home to the Big Five. Visit Rwanda also lists leopard, hyena, elephant, buffalo, zebra, giraffe and many antelope species, including roan and eland.

Highlights to plan around:

- **Lake Ihema boat trip.** In the south of the park, with hippos, Nile crocodiles and nesting birds on the island at its centre.
- **Birdwatching.** Visit Rwanda gives a figure of 525 bird species, including the shoebill in the papyrus swamps.
- **Night drives.** Bushbabies are often seen after dark.

The only way to explore Akagera is by vehicle, either with a tour operator or on a self-drive. The park recommends hiring a guide, which can be booked through the park itself.

## Safety near the western border

The UK Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office (FCDO) advises against all but essential travel to the parts of Rusizi District that are within 10km of the border with the Democratic Republic of Congo, including the Rwandan islands in Lake Kivu and Kamembe Airport. It also warns that fighting in eastern DRC may affect people on the Rwandan side, that borders are often not well marked in remote areas, and that crossings can close at short notice. Nyungwe and Volcanoes both lie in the west of the country, so check the latest advice before you travel and discuss your route with your operator.

## Before you go

- Book primate permits through the official Rwanda Development Board channels or your operator, and confirm current prices at the time of booking.
- Pack waterproofs, gaiters and sturdy boots: both Volcanoes and Nyungwe have steep slopes, and rain is common.
- Allow early starts: chimpanzee tracking in Nyungwe begins at 5.30am.
- Plan Akagera as a vehicle-based safari, and consider booking a park guide.
- Check FCDO advice for the DRC border area, especially for itineraries near Lake Kivu or Rusizi District.
