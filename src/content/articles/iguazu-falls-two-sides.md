The Iguazú river breaks into a long chain of falls through subtropical forest on the border between Argentina and Brazil, and each country protects its bank as a national park. Both are UNESCO World Heritage Sites. The short answer: Brazil gives you the wide view in a couple of hours, and Argentina puts you on walkways in and above the falls and fills a full day. If you have time, do both.

## Two parks, one river

Argentina's Parque Nacional Iguazú, in the north of Misiones province, was created in 1934 and declared a World Heritage Site in 1984, according to APN, Argentina's national parks administration. The nearest town is Puerto Iguazú, 18 km away.

Brazil's Parque Nacional do Iguaçu, in western Paraná state, was created on 10 January 1939 and inscribed by UNESCO in 1986. Its official visitor site says it protects about 185,000 hectares of Atlantic Forest and shares a border of more than 60 km with the Argentine park. The park is run by ICMBio, Brazil's federal conservation agency. Visitor services have been run under a concession since 1999, by Urbia Cataratas since December 2022. The falls lie between Foz do Iguaçu in Brazil and Puerto Iguazú in Argentina.

## The Brazilian side: the panorama

The classic Brazilian visit is compact. Your ticket includes the park's panoramic buses from the visitor centre to the start of the Trilha das Cataratas and back, about 12 km in all. The trail itself is about a kilometre and a half long. The park describes a wide, panoramic view of the falls along the way, ending on a walkway with a view of the Devil's Throat (Garganta do Diabo). The park says this traditional visit takes about two hours.

Practical points from the official site:

- **Hours:** open every day of the year. Entry is from 9:00 to 16:00 on weekdays and from 8:30 to 16:00 at weekends. You book an entry time, not an exit time, and can stay until the park closes at 17:30.
- **No private cars inside:** the park is a strictly protected conservation unit, so only accredited vehicles enter and internal transport is included in the ticket. City bus line 120 runs to the park from Foz do Iguaçu's urban bus terminal.
- **Spray:** the park warns of constant light drizzle on the trail and suggests a rain cape if you want to stay dry.
- **A cycle path** of 11.6 km runs from the visitor centre to the start of the trail, and the park says it is included in the ticket.

## The Argentine side: up close

Argentina's park is spread out and needs more time. APN lists three main walkway circuits:

- **Paseo Inferior (Lower Circuit):** 1,750 m of walkways with stairs, so close to the falls that you feel the spray. APN notes that the stairs limit access.
- **Paseo Superior (Upper Circuit):** 1,850 m, one-way, with views along the top of the gorge where the falls begin. It has no stairs and is accessible to all.
- **Garganta del Diablo:** a 1,100 m walkway without stairs over the upper river to the main drop of the falls.

The Tren Ecológico de la Selva, a small train with electric or LPG locomotives, links the central station with the Cataratas and Garganta stations. For a walk into the forest, the Sendero Macuco is 7 km return, unpaved and for walkers only. APN suggests asking at the visitor centre for its leaflet and wildlife precautions. Boat trips into the falls run under concession. The Gran Aventura is for over-12s only, and APN notes that bags for your belongings are provided but rain capes are not.

APN's published hours are 8:00 to 18:00 every day, with last entry at 16:00 and the last train to the Garganta del Diablo at 15:30. It recommends arriving early to make the most of the day. Full-moon night walks to the Garganta del Diablo are offered on some dates. They last two and a half hours and are cancelled if it rains.

## When to go

APN says the park can be enjoyed all year. The climate is humid subtropical, with temperatures between about 12°C and 32°C. December to March is the warmest period and April to August the coolest. The busiest times on the Argentine side are the summer holidays (December to February), long weekends and the July winter holidays. The Brazilian park also publishes adjusted hours for holidays and long weekends, so check before you travel.

For either side, APN suggests light clothing, insect repellent, sunscreen and a hat, comfortable non-slip shoes that can cope with rain, and always a bottle of water.

## Crossing between the two

Visiting both parks means crossing an international border. At the time of writing, the UK FCDO says British visitors can enter Brazil and Argentina without a visa for tourism for up to 90 days each. Rules differ by nationality, so check your own government's advice before you go. Carry your passport on both days.

Both parks ask you not to feed the wildlife. The Brazilian park specifically warns that coatis are used to people and may come for your food. Stay on the marked trails, keep quiet, and take your rubbish with you. Pets are not allowed in the Brazilian park.

## Before you go

- Book Brazilian tickets for a set entry time on the park's official site, and check that day's opening hours.
- Give the Argentine side a full day and arrive early. Last entry is 16:00.
- Pack a rain jacket or cape and a dry bag for your phone, especially for the boat trips and lower walkways.
- Check border and visa rules for your nationality before crossing between Foz do Iguaçu and Puerto Iguazú.
- Avoid the December to February holidays, long weekends and July if you want quieter walkways.
- Keep food out of sight of coatis.
