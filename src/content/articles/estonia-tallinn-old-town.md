Tallinn's Old Town is a tight knot of towers, merchant houses and cobbled lanes climbing to the hill of Toompea, and it has been on the UNESCO World Heritage List since 1997. Step out of the capital, though, and Estonia changes character entirely: boardwalks across raised bogs, forests flooded each spring and more than two thousand islands. A trip that combines both gives a fuller picture of the country than the city alone.

## Tallinn's Old Town: merchants below, power above

Visit Estonia describes the Old Town as a well-preserved medieval trading town that was once part of the Hanseatic League. It falls into two parts. The lower town was the realm of merchants; Toompea, the upper town on the hill, was home to the ruling elite and is still the seat of government. Toompea Castle houses Estonia's parliament today.

Landmarks named by the national tourism board include:

- **Town Hall Square**, once the main market square. Visit Estonia calls the Town Hall the only surviving Gothic town hall in Northern Europe.
- **The Town Hall Pharmacy (Raeapteek)**, described as one of the oldest continuously operating pharmacies in Europe.
- **St Olaf's Church**, which was among the tallest buildings in the world in its day.
- **St Mary's Cathedral** on Toompea, the church of the knights, clergy and elite, in contrast to the merchant churches below.
- **The city walls**, with the Nun's Tower guarding one of the best-preserved sections.

Visit Estonia's self-guided walk covers about three kilometres, starting and finishing at Town Hall Square, which makes it an easy half-day on foot.

## Into the bogs

Bogs are part of Estonia's identity. Visit Estonia says you are "never more than 15 minutes from a bog anywhere in Estonia". It also corrects a figure you may see elsewhere: while 22% is often quoted as the share of the country covered by bogs, that includes peat areas such as mining sites, and the actual figure is "somewhere around 6-7%".

Because bogs are easy to sink into, the way in is usually a wooden boardwalk. Trails listed by Visit Estonia include:

- **Viru Bog Study Trail**
- **Riisa study trail**, with a boardwalk, eight rest stops and a watchtower
- **Pääsküla Bog nature trail**, near Tallinn
- **Koigi bog hiking trail** on Saaremaa, a 4.8km boardwalk across the largest bog in Saare County
- **Meenikunno hiking trail**, with cabins, an observation tower and boardwalk

For something more adventurous, guided bogshoe hikes let you step off the boardwalk; Visit Estonia describes the feeling as "essentially walking on water". In winter, some bogs offer snowshoe hikes instead. The tourism board says bog hiking is possible year-round.

## Soomaa and the fifth season

Soomaa National Park has a season of its own. Visit Estonia explains that around the end of March or early April the spring thaw overwhelms the park's four rivers, which overflow and submerge nearby meadows and forests until the land becomes a shallow lake. Estonians call this the "fifth season". The tourism board records a maximum water-level change of 5.5 metres.

Canoeing is the main way to see the flooded forest, and guided trips are available from local outfitters. People here once got around during floods in dugout canoes called haabjas. Timing is hard to predict more than a week or two ahead, and spring weather can bring sun, snow and freezing temperatures on the same day, so stay flexible and dress for all of it.

## The islands

Visit Estonia counts 2,317 islands, with regular ferries to the most visited. Saaremaa lies within the UNESCO West Estonian Islands Biosphere Reserve. The tourism board describes the islets around Hiiumaa as the best kayaking location in the Baltic region and highlights a lighthouse route across Saaremaa and Hiiumaa. Muhu, Saaremaa and Ruhnu were together named Estonia's Culinary Region for 2024.

Estonia has six national parks, according to Visit Estonia: Lahemaa, Soomaa, Alutaguse, Matsalu, Karula and Vilsandi. Wildlife includes bears, lynx and wolves, and the tourism board suggests beaver-watching in Soomaa and seal-watching near the Malusi Islands.

## Entry and planning

Estonia is in the Schengen area. The UK FCDO says British citizens can visit without a visa for up to 90 days in any 180-day period, counting time in other Schengen countries. Your passport must have been issued less than 10 years before you arrive and be valid for at least three months after you plan to leave the Schengen area. The EU's Entry/Exit System is being implemented, so border checks may take longer. Our explainer on [Europe's EES and ETIAS](/articles/europe-ees-etias-explained/) has the detail, and our [Estonia travel guide](/countries/estonia/) covers the wider country.

## Before you go

- Check your passport's issue and expiry dates against the Schengen rules.
- Wear comfortable shoes for Tallinn's cobbles and Toompea's slopes.
- Stay on boardwalks in bogs unless you are with a guide.
- For Soomaa's fifth season, keep your plans flexible and pack for snow as well as sun.
- Check ferry timetables in advance if you plan to visit the islands.
