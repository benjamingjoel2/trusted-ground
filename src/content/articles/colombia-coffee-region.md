Colombia's Coffee Cultural Landscape spans the departments of Caldas, Quindío and Risaralda and the north of Valle del Cauca, and it is a UNESCO World Heritage site. Most visitors base themselves in a city such as Armenia, Pereira or Manizales, or in a small town such as Salento or Filandia. From there you can tour working coffee farms and walk among the giant wax palms of the Cocora Valley. Before you book, check the UK government's current travel advice.

## Hills planted with coffee

Colombia's official tourism board, ProColombia, describes a region of mountains and hills covered in coffee plantations. Here coffee is "more than a product": the board calls it a symbol of the country and key to understanding its rural communities. Days in the region run to the crop's rhythm. You walk between rows of bushes, smell the beans roasting and finish with a cup on a veranda as the light fades over the ridges.

The region's working symbol is the Willys Jeep, known locally as the *yipao*. According to ProColombia, coffee farmers took to these jeeps in the 1940s for carrying heavy sacks over mountain tracks. Each year at the end of June and the start of July, Calarcá holds a Yipao parade in which loaded jeeps are driven up on two wheels.

## Visiting a finca

The heart of a trip here is a visit to a coffee farm, or *finca*. ProColombia says farms around Armenia show visitors how coffee is grown, threshed and roasted, and some show you how to prepare coffee in the regional style. On many visits you can pick coffee cherries yourself, and tastings usually compare different coffees by texture, origin, aroma and finish. Some estates and haciendas also offer accommodation.

Tours vary a lot in length, language and depth, from short walk-throughs to half-day visits that follow the bean from plant to cup. When booking, ask what is included: the field walk, processing, roasting and a tasting.

Away from the farms, the region's food and drink culture is easy to find. In Manizales, ProColombia points visitors to the *fondas*, traditional restaurants with flower-decked façades and terraces, decorated with ponchos, hats and *carrieles*, the traditional leather satchels.

## The Cocora Valley and the wax palm

The Cocora Valley lies in the municipality of Salento, about 25 km north of Armenia, according to ProColombia. It is home to the wax palm, Colombia's national tree, which can grow up to 60 metres tall. The palm is also essential to the yellow-eared parrot. Visitors can follow an ecological trail through the valley or ride on horseback, and camping is possible. ProColombia calls the valley the most beautiful natural enclave in the whole Coffee Cultural Landscape.

For most visitors, the walk among these towering palms is the highlight of a trip to the region. Wear sturdy footwear, carry rain gear and stay on marked paths. The FCDO warns of floods and landslides after heavy rain across the Colombian Andes and advises getting local advice before using mountain roads after storms.

## Towns and cities to base yourself in

ProColombia's suggested route around the villages of the coffee landscape includes:

- **Salento.** A heritage town known for colourful traditional architecture, and the gateway to the Cocora Valley.
- **Filandia.** Known for crafts and the La Colina Iluminada viewpoint.
- **Quimbaya.** Home to an artisans' village where crafts are made from *guadua* bamboo.
- **Santa Rosa de Cabal (Risaralda).** Known for its hot springs and as a gateway to Los Nevados National Natural Park.
- **Salamina (Caldas).** A heritage town declared a National Monument in 1982 for its adobe houses and clay roofs.

The three cities have their own festivals. The Manizales Fair takes place in January and an international theatre festival in September. Pereira's annual festival celebrates the coffee harvest.

## Safety and practicalities

At the time of writing (November 2026), the FCDO advises against all but essential travel to several parts of Colombia. These include the western part of Valle del Cauca Department, including Buenaventura. Use the FCDO map to check exactly where your route goes, especially if you plan to travel south from the coffee region.

The FCDO's general advice applies here as everywhere in Colombia:

- Use licensed taxis booked by phone or app, or through your hotel, rather than hailing them in the street.
- Be alert to drink spiking. The FCDO reports that criminals have used drugs such as scopolamine to incapacitate victims.

## Before you go

- Read the FCDO's Colombia travel advice and map, and check whether your route passes through any area where it advises against travel.
- Ask each finca what a tour includes, how long it lasts and which languages it is offered in.
- Pack waterproofs and good walking shoes for the Cocora Valley, and stay on marked trails.
- After heavy rain, ask locally whether mountain roads are open before you set off.
- Book taxis through your accommodation or a licensed app.
- If you want to see the Calarcá jeep parade or the Manizales Fair, check the dates for that year.
