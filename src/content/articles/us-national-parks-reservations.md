For a summer trip to a popular US national park, you may need two things: an entrance pass and, at a handful of parks, a timed-entry or tour reservation booked through Recreation.gov. Since 2026, international visitors have paid more. A $250 non-resident annual pass is available, and 11 of the best-known parks charge a $100 nonresident fee per person on top of the entrance fee.

## Entry, in two parts

To drive through Rocky Mountain National Park's Beaver Meadows entrance on a summer morning, you need two separate things. The first is a pass, which pays the entrance fee. The second, during certain hours, is a reservation for a time slot. They are bought separately.

The National Park Service (NPS) says most of the sites it manages are free, but parks that charge an entrance fee accept one of three types of pass:

- **Standard pass.** Covers one park for one to seven days.
- **Park-specific annual pass.** Covers one park, or a group of parks, for a year. Not every park offers one.
- **America the Beautiful pass.** Covers entrance fees and standard day-use fees at federal recreation sites, including national parks.

You only need one of these.

## The America the Beautiful pass

At the time of writing (November 2026), the NPS lists these prices:

- **Resident Annual Pass:** $80, for US citizens and residents.
- **Non-Resident Annual Pass:** $250, for non-US residents.
- **Senior passes:** $20 a year or $80 for life, for US citizens and permanent residents aged 62 and over.

Free passes are also available for current US military personnel and their dependants, US citizens and residents with a permanent disability, US fourth graders and volunteers who have completed 250 service hours.

The non-resident pass covers the entrance fee and any nonresident fees for the passholder and up to 15 passengers in a private vehicle, or for the holder and passengers on two motorcycles. Where a park charges per person, it covers the holder plus three more people aged 16 and over. Passes are non-refundable and non-transferable, and lost or stolen passes are not replaced. You must show photo ID with your pass.

You can buy a physical pass at more than 1,000 federal recreation sites or order one online from the USGS Store. Online orders can take up to three weeks to arrive, and a receipt is not accepted in place of the pass. A digital pass from Recreation.gov can be used straight away.

## The new nonresident fee

According to the NPS, nonresidents aged 16 and over pay a $100 nonresident fee, on top of the regular entrance fee, at these parks: Acadia, Bryce Canyon, Everglades, Glacier, Grand Canyon, Grand Teton, Rocky Mountain, Sequoia and Kings Canyon, Yellowstone, Yosemite and Zion. The fee is waived for visitors with an annual pass or an America the Beautiful pass. It also applies to nonresidents travelling with commercial tour groups.

Free-entrance days have changed too. From 2026, they apply only to US citizens and residents.

If you plan to visit two or more of these 11 parks, the $250 pass is likely to cost less than paying at each gate. Do the sums for your own itinerary.

## How timed entry works

The NPS says some parks use timed entry to manage vehicle traffic, and others use tour reservations to manage access to a building or natural feature. Reservations are usually released on Recreation.gov on a rolling basis. Systems change from year to year, so always check the park's own website. These examples from 2026 show how much they differ:

- **Rocky Mountain National Park** required reservations from 22 May to mid-October. Standard timed entry applied from 9am to 2pm. A "Timed Entry+ Bear Lake Road" reservation was needed for the Bear Lake Road corridor from 5am to 6pm. You choose a two-hour entry window and can then stay as long as you like. Reservations are released monthly at 8am MDT, with more released at 7pm the night before. The only charge is a non-refundable $2 Recreation.gov processing fee, and reservations cannot be made at the park itself.
- **Haleakalā National Park** requires a reservation to see sunrise from the summit. Reservations are released up to 60 days ahead, with a further batch released 48 hours before. You can make only one booking in any three-day period, and none are refunded because of weather.
- **Yosemite National Park** announced that it would not use a timed reservation system in 2026. It is managing traffic and parking directly and encouraging visitors to come on weekdays.
- **Glacier National Park** did not require vehicle reservations in 2026. Instead, it piloted a ticketed shuttle and three-hour time-limited parking at Logan Pass.

There is often a way in without a booking. At Rocky Mountain, for example, you can enter without a reservation before 9am or after 2pm.

## Planning around the rules

Treat release dates as fixed appointments. Rocky Mountain's slots sell out quickly, and the NPS says it is best to plan ahead. Allow for the weather at high parks too. At Haleakalā, the NPS warns that conditions around dawn are regularly wet, windy and below freezing, and that no food, fuel or clothing is sold in the park.

## Before you go

- Check each park's "Plan your visit" page for the current season's reservation rules. They changed at several parks between 2025 and 2026.
- If you are not a US resident, compare the $250 Non-Resident Annual Pass with paying the $100 nonresident fee and entrance fee at each park.
- Create a Recreation.gov account before release day and note release times in the park's time zone.
- Order a physical pass at least three weeks ahead, or buy a digital pass or one in person.
- Carry photo ID that matches the name on your pass.
- If you are travelling with a tour, ask whether the nonresident fee is included in the price.
