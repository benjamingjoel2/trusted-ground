Prague draws the crowds, but some of Czechia's most memorable places lie a train or bus ride away: a Renaissance town wrapped in a bend of the Vltava, a medieval silver city with a cathedral to the patron saint of miners, and spa towns built around steaming mineral springs. All three are on the UNESCO World Heritage List, and each makes a rewarding contrast with the capital.

## Český Krumlov: a castle above the river

In South Bohemia, Český Krumlov's chateau rises above a town of Renaissance houses, with the old town on the opposite bank of the Vltava, linked by a small bridge. CzechTourism describes the town as one of the gems on the UNESCO list.

The town owes its look to the Rožmberk family, whom CzechTourism calls one of the wealthiest families in the kingdom. They made Český Krumlov their seat and built towns and residences across South Bohemia under Italian Renaissance influence. The castle's own website says its buildings preserve their original layout from the 14th to the 19th century.

Highlights inside the castle complex, according to CzechTourism:

- **The Baroque Theatre**, described as one of the best preserved in the world, with its unusual revolving auditorium, which still hosts performances
- **The Masquerade Hall**, with its painted decoration
- **The castle tower**, with views over the old town
- **An illuminated five-storey bridge** linking the Upper Castle with the theatre building
- **Bears in the castle moat**, a long-standing part of the town's character

In the old town, the Church of St Vitus holds the tomb of the Rožmberk family. Practical notes from the castle: dogs and other animals are not allowed in the castle grounds, there is no tour designed for children, and step-free entrances are only in the Castle Garden. CzechTourism lists an Advent and Christmas programme in the town from 21 November 2026 to 3 January 2027.

## Kutná Hora: silver, saints and Sedlec

Kutná Hora grew rich on silver. CzechTourism calls it "the silver treasury" of the country and notes that under King Wenceslas II the only mint in the land operated here. Its historic centre, with the cathedrals of St Barbara and of the Assumption, is on the UNESCO list.

**St Barbara's Cathedral** is dedicated to the patron saint of miners. CzechTourism says building it took some five hundred years, with Matyáš Rejsek and Benedikt Ried shaping its late Gothic form. Inside, one chapel's frescoes show mining and coin minting.

In the suburb of Sedlec, the **Cathedral of the Assumption of the Virgin Mary** was built in about 30 years at the turn of the 13th and 14th centuries, according to Prague City Tourism. After it burned in the 15th century, architect Jan Blažej Santini-Aichel remade it in Baroque Gothic style. Prague City Tourism says the cathedral was inscribed on the UNESCO list in 1995.

About five minutes' walk away is the **ossuary** in the Church of All Saints, a two-storey Gothic church built in the first half of the 14th century on the grounds of a former Cistercian monastery. At the time of writing (November 2026), Prague City Tourism lists a combined ticket for the cathedral and ossuary at 220 CZK (150 CZK reduced). The ossuary is a place of human remains within a church, so behave as you would in any sacred space.

## The West Bohemian spa towns

In West Bohemia, Karlovy Vary, Mariánské Lázně and Františkovy Lázně form what CzechTourism calls the West Bohemian Spa Triangle. All three belong to the Great Spa Towns of Europe, a group of 11 spa towns in seven countries that UNESCO recognised as a transnational World Heritage site on 24 July 2021.

The towns grew up around mineral springs. In Karlovy Vary, CzechTourism points visitors to the Vřídlo spring and to the Market Colonnade and Mill Colonnade, where people stroll in the shade between drinks of spring water. Spa treatments in the region focus on conditions of the musculoskeletal and cardiovascular systems, among others. Nearby Jáchymov is described as having the world's first radon spa.

Look out for local specialities such as spa wafers and herbal liqueur. CzechTourism lists Goethe, Chopin, Mark Twain and Peter the Great among the region's past guests.

## Getting around safely

Most trips beyond Prague start and end in the capital, so the usual city precautions apply. The UK FCDO says petty theft is a problem in Prague's main tourist areas, and advises using a major taxi company rather than hailing one in the street, and checking rates before getting in. It also advises changing money only at a bank or exchange office, never on the street.

The FCDO says it is a legal requirement to carry ID, so keep your passport with you. See our [Czechia travel guide](/countries/czechia/) for more planning help.

## Before you go

- Check castle tour times and seasonal opening on the Český Krumlov castle website before you travel.
- Confirm current ticket prices for the Sedlec cathedral and ossuary; they change.
- Dress respectfully and keep voices low in churches and the ossuary.
- If booking spa treatments, ask what each treatment involves and whether a medical consultation is needed.
- Carry your passport and change money only at banks or exchange offices.
