To visit the Galápagos, you pay a national park entry fee when you arrive and buy a transit control card before you fly. You also need mandatory travel health insurance. Once there, the Galápagos National Park's rules require you to keep your distance from wildlife, stay on marked trails and visit protected areas with a specialised guide. Whether you choose a cruise or a land-based trip, those rules are the same, so the decision comes down to how you want to spend your time.

## Before you fly: entry fee, transit card and insurance

**The park entry fee.** According to the Galápagos National Park Directorate, under provincial ordinance No. 001-CGREG-30-06-2025, the fee at the time of writing (November 2026) is:

- **Foreign adults (over 12):** USD 200.
- **Foreign children (under 12):** USD 100.
- **Nationals of Andean Community (CAN) and Mercosur countries:** USD 100 for adults and USD 50 for children under 12.
- **Children under 2:** exempt.

Park rangers collect the fee at Baltra and San Cristóbal airports. The directorate says the money funds conservation of land and marine wildlife, park conservation work, and services and sustainable development projects in the islands' communities.

**The transit control card.** The UK Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office (FCDO) says you must buy this card at your departure airport before checking in for your flight to the islands, and keep a copy.

**Other conditions.** The FCDO also lists these requirements:

- Travel health insurance, which it describes as "mandatory for foreign tourists". See our [insurance guide](/guide/insurance/).
- Evidence of a hotel booking or an invitation letter from your host.
- Evidence of a return flight to the Ecuadorean mainland.
- Tourists can stay in the Galápagos for up to 60 days.

If your route goes through Guayaquil, the FCDO advises against all but essential travel to the city, except for airside transit. Check with your airline that you will stay airside before you book.

## The park's visiting rules

The Galápagos National Park publishes its rules for the national park and marine reserve. They include:

- Keep a distance of 2 metres from wildlife, including when using cameras.
- Do not touch or feed the animals.
- Walk on marked trails.
- Take photos without flash. Professional filming needs authorisation. Do not use drones.
- Do not remove anything from the ecosystem, and do not introduce anything external to it.
- Do not smoke, drink alcohol or make campfires.
- Do not write on rocks or the landscape, and leave rubbish sorted in the right place.
- Use sun protection that is safe for marine ecosystems.
- Use headphones with any music player.
- Camp only at designated sites, with prior authorisation.
- Do not use motorised watercraft or submarines, and no air tourism.
- Check and respect visiting hours.

Two further rules shape the way trips are run. The park asks visitors to "visit protected areas with a specialised guide" and to "use authorised tourism services". The FCDO adds that Ecuador's Ministry of Tourism publishes a directory of accredited tourism providers, covering accommodation, transport, tour operators and restaurants.

## Cruise or land-based trip?

Both are common ways to see the islands. The park rules apply equally, and either way your visits to protected sites should be led by a specialised guide.

- **A cruise** (a "live-aboard" trip) means you sleep on the boat, which moves between visitor sites, often overnight. Ask which islands and sites are on the itinerary. Ask too how many passengers each guide leads.
- **A land-based trip** means you stay in hotels on the inhabited islands and join day trips by boat to visitor sites. You will spend more time ashore in towns. Ask which sites the day trips can reach.

Either way, the questions that matter are the same. Is the boat or day-tour provider authorised? Is each landing led by a guide? Is the itinerary fixed in writing before you pay? The national park fee and transit card are normally paid separately from the trip price, so check what your quote includes.

## Questions to ask your operator

- Is the boat or day-tour provider authorised to operate in the Galápagos National Park, and is your company in the Ministry of Tourism's directory of accredited providers?
- Will every visit to a protected site be led by a specialised guide, and how many people will be in each group?
- Which islands and visitor sites does the itinerary include, and can you confirm them in writing?
- Does the price include the park entry fee and the transit control card, or do we pay those ourselves?
- What happens if the itinerary changes or a site is closed on our dates?
- Do you need proof of our travel health insurance before departure?
