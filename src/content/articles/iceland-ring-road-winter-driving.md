You can drive Iceland's Ring Road in winter, but it is a different undertaking from a summer road trip. Storms can close roads with little warning, ice and drifting snow are routine, and daylight is short, so the safest plan is a flexible one with fewer kilometres a day and time built in to wait out bad weather.

## What winter does to the roads

The Icelandic Road and Coastal Administration (IRCA) is blunt: winter driving is dangerous for inexperienced drivers. It lists bad weather, freezing temperatures, ice and short daylight hours as the main causes of serious accidents.

Safetravel, the safety service run by the Icelandic Association for Search and Rescue (ICE-SAR), adds that strong winds, snow, rain and drifting snow can make a road dangerous even when it looks clear. It also warns that a 4x4 "does not eliminate the risk of skidding on ice".

Some other things worth knowing:

- **Speed limits assume perfect conditions.** IRCA gives 90 km/h as the top legal speed on highways outside towns and 80 km/h on gravel. Safetravel points out that in winter, conditions are rarely good enough to drive at the posted limit.
- **Gravel sections.** The UK Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office (FCDO) notes that some main routes are gravel or dirt. Safetravel advises slowing down before you reach gravel, because tyres lose grip in the change from tarmac.
- **Single-lane bridges.** IRCA says single-lane bridges and tunnels, used by traffic in both directions, are still relatively common. Safetravel gives a 50 km/h limit on single-lane bridges, and the vehicle that arrives first has right of way.
- **Wind damage.** The FCDO warns that strong winds can damage hire cars with blown sand or ash, and that this may not be covered by insurance.

## Road closures

Closures are a normal part of a winter trip. IRCA says roads can close suddenly during severe storms, asks travellers to respect closures, and notes that violators face heavy fines. Safetravel explains that roads are only closed when it is urgently needed. The word to look for on signs is *Lokað*, which means closed.

Closures are not only a winter issue. IRCA notes that storms, especially in the north, can close roads into June. Glacial outburst floods can also shut roads for up to weeks, with the highest risk south and east of Vatnajökull.

The highland mountain roads (F-roads) are not part of a winter itinerary. IRCA says they often do not open until late June or early July. Driving off marked roads is strictly forbidden at any time of year.

## Checking conditions every day

Make these three official sources part of your routine, every morning and again during the day. Safetravel stresses that this matters most in winter, when weather changes quickly.

- **Umferdin.is**, IRCA's traffic information site, gives real-time information on road closures and weather alerts in English, Polish and Icelandic. The traffic service phone number is 1777.
- **The Icelandic Meteorological Office (en.vedur.is)** publishes forecasts with wind, temperature and precipitation maps. Wind is often the deciding factor on whether a stretch of road is safe.
- **Safetravel.is** posts alerts, and its app gives weather and road information.

If conditions become too difficult, Safetravel's advice is to wait in a safe place until they improve, and to be ready to change or cancel a trip.

## Daylight, distances and pacing

Safetravel notes that limited daylight makes winter driving harder and advises allowing extra travel time. Check sunrise and sunset times for your dates before you set your daily distances, and plan to do most of your driving in the light.

Distances between towns are long. The FCDO points out that towns are far apart and that breakdown recovery can be expensive, especially in remote areas. IRCA notes that an ambulance may take up to an hour to reach the Ring Road, and longer in remote areas.

In practice, this means:

- Plan shorter driving days than you would in summer. Safetravel also warns against long driving days and driving while tired.
- Leave spare days in the itinerary so that one closure does not unravel every booking that follows.
- Keep the tank topped up and carry warm clothing, food and water in the car in case you have to wait.

## Car, rules and safety basics

- **Lights.** Front and rear lights must be on at all times. Safetravel warns that the AUTO setting may not turn on the rear lights.
- **Tyres.** The FCDO says tyres must suit the conditions, including winter tyres when needed. Check what your hire car comes with.
- **Before you set off.** Clear snow and ice from the windows, mirrors, lights and roof.
- **Stopping.** Never stop on the road or the hard shoulder to take photos. Safetravel says many accidents have happened this way.
- **Licences.** According to the FCDO, a UK photocard licence is valid for up to 30 days, but hire companies may set stricter rules on age and experience. Check our guides to insurance and deposits and payment protection before you pay.
- **Emergencies.** Call 112. Safetravel recommends sharing a travel plan with someone you trust, and its app can send your GPS location to the 112 emergency service.

## Questions to ask your operator

- Who drives in winter, and what experience do they have of Icelandic winter roads?
- Does the vehicle have winter tyres, and what does the insurance exclude, such as wind, sand or gravel damage?
- How many kilometres does the itinerary cover each day, and how much of that driving will be in darkness?
- What happens if a road closes: are there spare days, and who pays for extra nights or rebooked activities?
- Which road and weather sources do you check, and at what point do you cancel or change a day's route?
- Is a travel plan lodged with Safetravel, and what is the emergency procedure if the car is stuck?
