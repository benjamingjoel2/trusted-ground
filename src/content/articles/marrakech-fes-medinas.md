The medinas of Marrakech and Fes are the walled old towns at the heart of each city. Both are World Heritage sites, and both are largely car-free, so you explore them on foot through narrow lanes and souks. Go with time to spare, use only official guides, and respect local laws on dress, alcohol and photography, especially during Ramadan.

## What a medina is

The Moroccan National Tourist Office describes the country's medinas as typically walled towns whose treasures are best found by wandering their narrow streets. It names Fez, Tetouan, Essaouira and Marrakech as car-free, well-preserved historic towns that have become World Heritage sites. In Marrakech, the tourist office points to the architectural richness of the medina, with its many riads: small palaces built around a patio.

## Marrakech: the ochre city

The tourist office calls Marrakech's medina "the beating heart of the 'ochre' city". Its centre of gravity is Jemaa el-Fna, the great square beside the Koutoubia mosque, which was built under Almoravid rule. The tourist office says the square becomes "an open-air theatre as the night falls".

UNESCO first proclaimed the cultural space of Jemaa el-Fna Square in 2001, and inscribed it on its Representative List in 2008. UNESCO's description lists what happens there: storytellers, poets, snake charmers, Berber musicians, Gnaoua dancers and players of the senthir (hajouj), alongside henna tattooing, water-carriers, fortune-tellers, traditional medicine and stalls selling fruit and traditional food. UNESCO also notes the pressures on the square, from urbanisation and road building to tourism changing the character of the performances.

Beyond the square, the tourist office's list of Marrakech sights includes:

- The souks of the medina
- The Koutoubia mosque
- El Badi Palace and the Saadian Tombs
- Majorelle Garden
- The Menara gardens and their large basin

## Fes: the older, deeper medina

The tourist office calls Fez "the famous medina of Fez", recognised by UNESCO, and suggests allowing three days for the city. The oldest district, Fez el-Bali, was founded by the Idrisid dynasty. It is home to Al-Quaraouiyine, which the tourist office calls "the oldest university in the world". The office describes Bab Boujloud as the easiest way into the medina, and its list of sights includes:

- The Quaraouiyine mosque
- The leather tanneries
- The Nejjarine Museum
- The tombs of the Merinids
- Jnan Sbil garden
- The entrance to the former Royal Palace

The tourist office lists day trips to Meknes (about an hour), Volubilis (about an hour and a half) and Ifrane (about an hour). It also mentions the Festival of World Sacred Music, held in early May.

## Guides, scams and getting around

The UK Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office (FCDO) warns that petty crime is common in tourist areas, especially in medinas and on beaches. It says people posing as official tourist guides often harass visitors. If you use a guide in a medina, the FCDO advises making sure they carry an official badge and permission from the local authority. It also warns that credit card fraud and scams are common, including sellers swapping inferior goods for the ones you bought.

For journeys across town, the FCDO advises agreeing a taxi fare in advance or checking that the meter is running. It notes that petit taxi drivers often pick up other passengers along the way, and advises caution with petit taxis late at night. It also warns that many taxis booked through ride-sharing apps may be unregulated and unlicensed.

## Customs, laws and when to go

The FCDO asks visitors not to cause offence, especially during Ramadan or near religious sites. During the holy month it advises checking opening hours, following local dress codes, and being patient: fasting can cause tiredness, and driving may be erratic around dusk. Some other rules to know:

- **Alcohol.** Alcohol is sold in licensed venues, but drinking it in the street or anywhere other than a licensed restaurant or bar is illegal and can lead to arrest.
- **Photography.** It is illegal to take photographs near sensitive political or military sites.
- **Methanol.** The FCDO reports deaths and serious illness from alcoholic drinks containing methanol, which cannot be detected by taste or smell.

On timing, the tourist office recommends March to May and September to November for Fez. It says summer is very hot, and winter days are mild with cool evenings. ## Before you go

- Check the Ramadan dates for your trip and plan meals and opening hours around them.
- Use only guides with an official badge, and agree the price and route before you set off.
- Agree taxi fares in advance or make sure the meter is on.
- Dress modestly, and take extra care near mosques and other religious sites.
- Keep valuables out of sight in crowded souks and in Jemaa el-Fna after dark.
- Plan Fez for spring or autumn if you can: the tourist office says summers are very hot.
