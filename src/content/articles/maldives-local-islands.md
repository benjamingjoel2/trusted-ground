Most images of the Maldives show private resort islands, but many visitors now stay in guesthouses on inhabited "local" islands, where Maldivians live and work. It can be a quieter, more affordable way to see the country, but the rules are different. Outside resorts there is no alcohol, dress codes are more modest, and Ramadan changes daily life. Know these rules before you book.

## Resorts, guesthouses and local islands

The Maldives is a nation of islands in the Indian Ocean that spans the equator. According to the national tourism promotion body, Visit Maldives, it has 1,192 islands stretching across 871 kilometres. At the time of writing (November 2026), its site lists 175 resorts, 16 hotels, 158 liveaboards and 843 guesthouses.

Visit Maldives sums up the difference simply: with a guesthouse, "your stay will be in a local town". That means mosques, schools, shops and homes alongside you, rather than an island run entirely for visitors. Its guesthouse directory lets you search by atoll and by distance from international and domestic airports. That helps with the boat and flight transfers between islands.

## Alcohol: resorts only

This is the rule that most surprises first-time visitors. The UK Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office (FCDO) is clear:

- "Alcoholic drinks are only available on resort islands."
- "Do not take any alcohol out of your resort."
- You can be "arrested and deported for possessing and drinking alcohol, or being intoxicated outside resorts and on inhabited islands".

You cannot bring alcohol into the country either. The FCDO says it is illegal to import "anything deemed contrary to Islam", including alcohol, pork and pork products, bibles and "idols for worship". Drug laws are severe, with zero tolerance, and airports scan the baggage of transiting passengers too.

## Dress, beaches and religion

The Maldives is an Islamic country, and the FCDO says it is illegal to publicly observe a religion other than Islam. Take particular care around mosques and during Ramadan.

On dress, the FCDO advises being "sensitive to local dress standards when outside holiday resorts, including on beaches used by locals". Men and women should:

- keep shoulders covered
- avoid short or tight-fitting shorts
- keep arms and upper legs covered when bathing

Nudism and topless sunbathing are not allowed anywhere, including on resort islands. Practices differ between islands, so ask your guesthouse where visitors are expected to swim and sunbathe before you arrive.

**Ramadan.** During the holy month, the FCDO advises not eating, drinking or smoking in public in the daytime outside resorts. It says Ramadan 2027 may start on 7 February and last 29 or 30 days. Dates vary by year, so check before booking.

**LGBT+ travellers.** The FCDO notes that same-sex relations are illegal in the Maldives, with possible fines or lengthy prison sentences.

## Sea, boats and diving

On a local island there may be no resort staff watching the water, so local knowledge matters. The FCDO warns that strong tidal currents can drag swimmers away from the shore, resulting in drownings. Swim only in supervised or designated areas, and always take local advice before going in.

On boats, pay attention to safety briefings and make sure life jackets are provided, especially on smaller excursion boats. The FCDO reports fatal propeller accidents in the South Ari Marine Protected Area and nearby whale shark sites. Regulations requiring propeller guards "are not yet fully enforced". For divers, it notes there are three hyperbaric chambers in the islands, and many islands are more than an hour from the nearest one. Ask your dive operator how they would get you there.

The FCDO also says it is illegal to take coral or tortoiseshell items out of the country.

## Entry paperwork

According to the FCDO at the time of writing, tourists get a 30-day visa on arrival. Your passport must be valid for at least one month after you arrive.

You must also complete the Imuga traveller declaration. Maldives Immigration describes it as a "mandatory declaration form within 96 hours of arrival or departure", so do it on the official immigration site before you fly. A yellow fever certificate is required if you arrive from a country with a risk of transmission.

## Questions to ask your guesthouse

- Where on the island can visitors swim and sunbathe, and what should we wear there?
- How do we get from the airport to the island, how long does it take, and is the transfer by speedboat, ferry or domestic flight?
- If we want a resort day or an evening drink, how is that arranged, and how do we get back?
- What changes on the island during Ramadan or Friday prayers?
- Are your boats fitted with propeller guards, and do they carry life jackets in all sizes?
- For divers: which dive centre do you use, and what is its plan for reaching a hyperbaric chamber?
