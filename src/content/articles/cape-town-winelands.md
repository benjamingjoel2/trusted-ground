Cape Town packs a mountain, a peninsula of beaches and penguin colonies, and historic wine country into a short drive. Most visitors split their time between Table Mountain National Park and the Winelands around Stellenbosch and Franschhoek. Plan hikes around the weather, keep to busy trails, and take official safety advice seriously on the roads.

## Table Mountain National Park

South African Tourism describes Table Mountain National Park as running from Signal Hill in the north to Cape Point in the south, including the surrounding seas and coastline. It calls the park "globally recognised for its biodiversity", and notes that Table Mountain is one of the New 7 Wonders of Nature.

There are two ways up the mountain. The Table Mountain Aerial Cableway opened in 1929, and South African Tourism says more than 22 million people have ridden it to the top. At the upper station there are footpaths across the summit plateau. Hiking trails start from the Camps Bay side, from Kirstenbosch National Botanical Garden and from the city centre.

The mountain is not to be underestimated. South African Tourism warns that people die every year after setting out under-prepared for a sudden change in the weather. It advises hiking in a group, carrying water and warm clothing, and considering a guide. The UK Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office (FCDO) adds that the trails are strenuous and that mountain rescuers get regular call-outs. It recommends checking South African National Parks (SANParks) for current hazards, including wildfires.

## Hiking safely

The FCDO reports recent violent attacks and muggings against hikers and foreign tourists in Table Mountain National Park, including on Lion's Head and Signal Hill. It advises visitors to:

- Avoid quieter parts of the park, especially in the early morning and evening
- Stay on busy, marked trails
- Hike on popular days such as weekends
- Hike in groups of four or more

## The Cape Peninsula and Boulders

The southern section of the park runs down the Peninsula towards Cape Point. Near Simon's Town, Boulders Beach falls within the park's Marine Protected Area and is home to a colony of African penguins. South African Tourism describes these as the only penguins found on the continent and notes that they are endangered. Boardwalks lead across the beaches to an information centre managed by SANParks. South African Tourism says more than 60,000 people a year visit Simon's Town to see them. Entry fees apply. Check the current rate with SANParks, as published figures date quickly.

Along the coast, the FCDO warns that seals and other marine animals can bite, and that rabies has been detected in seals along the Cape Peninsula coastline. Keep your distance from wildlife on beaches and rocks.

## The Winelands

South African Tourism says Stellenbosch lies about 50 km east of Cape Town. It was founded in 1679 by Simon van der Stel, then governor of the Cape, on the banks of the Eerste River, and is described as the second-oldest formal settlement in South Africa. Franschhoek, whose name means "French corner", is around 70 km and an hour's drive from the airport. South African Tourism counts 50 active wineries on its route, mainly planted with sauvignon blanc, merlot, chardonnay and semillon.

On timing, South African Tourism says Franschhoek is especially lovely from September to April. Stellenbosch can be visited at any time of year, though it rains in winter. For walkers there is a full-day Vineyard Hiking Trail of 24 km, best in autumn or spring.

Wines of South Africa, the industry body, lists more than 20 regional wine routes. Several are within reach of Cape Town:

- Constantia Wine Route
- Durbanville Wine Valley
- Stellenbosch Wine Routes
- Franschhoek Wine Valley
- Paarl Wine Route
- Elgin Valley Wine Route

Each route office publishes its own maps and contact details, which is the most reliable place to check opening days and tasting arrangements.

## Driving and getting around

Many visitors hire a car, and car hire is available at Cape Town International Airport. The FCDO reports an increase in violent crime and targeted attacks on the roads to and from the airport, including against visitors in hire cars. It advises staying on the M3 and N2 rather than the R300, using the "airport approach road" (exit 16 on the N2), and not following GPS routes away from main roads. Carjacking and robbery are common, particularly after dark and at traffic lights, junctions and petrol stations.

The FCDO says speed limits are usually 120 km/h on highways and 60 km/h on major roads. For getting around the city, it names the MyCiTi bus in Cape Town's central business district and Atlantic Seaboard among the safer, more reliable options. ## Before you go

- Check the weather and SANParks updates before any hike, and turn back if conditions change.
- Hike in a group of four or more on busy, marked trails.
- Plan your airport route on the M3 and N2, and avoid dropping off a hire car at night.
- Confirm current fees for Boulders and other paying sites with SANParks before you travel.
- Contact the relevant wine route office for opening days and tasting arrangements.
- Arrange a driver or transport for wine tasting days rather than driving yourself.
