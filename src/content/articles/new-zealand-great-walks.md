New Zealand's Great Walks are multi-day tracks managed by the Department of Conservation (DOC), and in the main summer season you need to book every night in a hut or campsite in advance. Book through DOC's online system as early as you can, pay the international rate unless you can prove New Zealand residency, and arrange transport to and from the track separately.

## Eleven tracks, one booking system

The Great Walks thread through some of New Zealand's most dramatic country. There are golden beaches and coastal forest on the Abel Tasman Coast Track, volcanic craters and emerald lakes on the Tongariro Northern Circuit, and the waterfalls and glacier-carved valleys of the Milford Track in Fiordland. DOC describes them as "premier tracks" that are well formed and easy to follow.

DOC's comparison page lists eleven journeys:

- **North Island:** Lake Waikaremoana, Tongariro Northern Circuit and the Whanganui Journey. The Whanganui Journey is a river trip, paddled rather than walked.
- **South Island:** the Abel Tasman Coast Track, Heaphy Track, Paparoa Track, Routeburn Track, Kepler Track, Milford Track and Hump Ridge Track.
- **Stewart Island/Rakiura:** the Rakiura Track.

They range from two-day outings to river journeys of up to five days. The Paparoa Track can be walked or mountain-biked. DOC says mountain bikes are allowed on the Heaphy Track between 1 May and 30 November.

Most bookings go through DOC. The exception is the Hump Ridge Track, which DOC says is booked through the Hump Ridge Trust website. At the time of writing (November 2026), DOC says bookings are open for stays up to 30 June 2027.

## When you must book

On DOC's track pages, the Great Walks season generally runs over the southern summer. The Milford Track page, for example, gives the 2026/27 season as 1 November 2026 to 30 April 2027, with the last departure on 28 April 2027. During the season, bookings are required for huts, and on the Milford Track for transport as well.

DOC is clear about what happens if you don't book. On the Milford Track page it warns that without a valid booking "you may be charged a penalty, or turned away if the hut is full."

Outside the season, things change. On the Milford Track, bookings are not required, bunks are first come, first served, and you pay with Backcountry Hut Tickets or a Backcountry Hut Pass. Facilities are greatly reduced, and DOC says the track should only be attempted by fit, experienced walkers with alpine, river-crossing and navigation skills. Check each track's own page, because seasons and rules differ.

Not every Great Walk offers camping. DOC states that camping is not permitted on the Milford Track, which has three huts that "must be booked well in advance".

## What it costs: huts, campsites and the two rates

Fees are charged per person, per night. Most Great Walks have different prices for international visitors and New Zealand residents, and the summer fees are higher than winter and shoulder-season fees. Some examples of summer hut fees from DOC's pricing page at the time of writing (November 2026), in New Zealand dollars:

- **Milford Track:** $152 per adult per night for international visitors, $106 for New Zealand residents.
- **Kepler Track:** $132 international, $88 New Zealand resident.
- **Abel Tasman Coast Track:** $84 international, $55 New Zealand resident.

Lake Waikaremoana is an exception: DOC lists the same $35 adult hut fee for both groups. Campsite fees are listed separately on the same page. Prices change, so check the pricing page before you book.

**Children.** DOC charges international children a fee of half the adult rate, except on Lake Waikaremoana. On the Milford Track, international children are charged from age 0 to 17. Children of New Zealand citizens and residents can qualify for the New Zealand rate.

## Proving you qualify for the New Zealand rate

The lower rate is for New Zealand citizens, citizens of the realm nations (Cook Islands, Niue and Tokelau) and people who are "ordinarily resident" in New Zealand. DOC defines ordinarily resident as someone who:

- has lived in New Zealand for at least six of the 12 months before starting the walk;
- holds a residence class, student or work visa; and
- has New Zealand as their primary place of residence.

DOC staff carry out random checks at visitor centres and huts. If you have paid the New Zealand rate but cannot show evidence, such as a New Zealand driver's licence or passport, DOC says you will pay the difference plus a $50 administration fee. In some cases you may not be able to continue your walk. Travellers on holiday should simply book at the international rate.

## Getting to the track and staying safe

Many Great Walks are one-way, so you need to plan how you get to the start and back from the finish. The Milford Track has no vehicle access to either end. You reach the start by bus and boat across Lake Te Anau and leave by boat from Sandfly Point to Milford Sound/Piopiotahi, and DOC says this transport must be booked in advance. The track can only be walked in one direction, over three nights and four days.

Huts are comfortable but basic. On the Milford Track they have bunks, mattresses, heating, toilets and cold running water, but no cooking utensils or showers, and tap water should be boiled. DOC warns that Fiordland's weather can turn at any time of year, that the track has no cellphone coverage and that track closures can disrupt itineraries. It recommends travel insurance.

## Before you go

- Check the booking opening dates on DOC's Great Walks page, and book huts or campsites for every night of a season walk.
- Book the Hump Ridge Track through the Hump Ridge Trust, not DOC.
- Book transport to and from the trailhead at the same time as your huts, especially on one-way tracks such as the Milford.
- Carry your booking confirmation and ID. If you paid the New Zealand rate, bring proof of eligibility as well.
- Read the track page for season dates, hut facilities and hazards. Outside the season, conditions and skill requirements change sharply.
- Check that your travel insurance covers multi-day tramping and itinerary changes caused by track closures.
