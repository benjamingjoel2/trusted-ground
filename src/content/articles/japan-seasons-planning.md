Spring and autumn are Japan's easiest seasons. Cherry blossom moves north from late March into April, and autumn colour peaks in many places from mid-November to early December. The early summer rainy season, the July and August heat, and a typhoon season running from June to December all need more careful planning, though none of them rules out a trip.

## Spring: cherry blossom and Golden Week

According to the Japan National Tourism Organization (JNTO), cherry blossom begins at the southern tip of Kyushu in late March and spreads north through Honshu and on to Hokkaido during April. JNTO describes most of Japan as mild at this time of year. Temperatures can drop suddenly, though, so bring layers. Hokkaido stays cold, and JNTO notes that many of its ski resorts stay open well past March.

Blossom dates move from year to year, so treat any forecast as an estimate. The Japan Meteorological Agency (JMA) records the actual dates when cherry trees flower and reach full bloom, using designated sample trees at its observation stations. From December to June, its page showing this year's cherry blossom progress is updated three times a day. It is in Japanese, but it is the official record of what has actually bloomed.

Two other points from JNTO:

- **Golden Week** starts at the end of April. It is a run of public holidays when much of the country travels, so expect crowds and higher demand for transport and rooms.
- **Hay fever.** Spring pollen causes many cases of hay fever. If you have allergies, bring medication.

## Early summer: the rainy season

The rainy season (*tsuyu*) is how spring turns into summer. JMA defines it as a period with more rain and less sunshine than the weeks either side. JMA notes that its start and end are not sharp: each change usually takes about five days.

When JMA compares rainy-season rainfall with the usual amount, it uses June and July for most regions and May and June for Okinawa and Amami. That is a good guide to when to expect it. JMA's rainy-season records cover regions from Okinawa to northern Tohoku. Hokkaido is not one of them.

Rain does not shut the country down, but the FCDO warns that heavy rain during the rainy and typhoon seasons can cause flooding and landslides and disrupt transport, especially in low-lying, coastal and mountainous areas.

## High summer: heat, festivals and Mount Fuji

The FCDO says Japanese summers are extremely hot and humid, with temperatures often above 35°C in July and August, and that heatstroke is a significant risk. It points travellers to the heatstroke alerts published by Japan's environment authorities. Plan outdoor sightseeing for early morning and evening, and carry water.

JNTO notes that many Japanese people travel home for **Obon** in mid-August, so expect busy transport around then. If you plan to climb **Mount Fuji**, JNTO gives the climbing season as early July to around the first week of September and advises booking mountain accommodation well in advance.

## Typhoons: June to December

The FCDO gives Japan's typhoon season as June to December, peaking between July and September, with the highest risk in the south of the country. A typhoon can cancel flights, ferries and trains for a day or more, so leave slack in tight itineraries during those months.

JMA issues three levels of weather alert, which the Japan Tourism Agency's safety site explains in English:

- **Advisories**, when heavy rain or strong wind may cause damage.
- **Warnings**, when a serious disaster may occur.
- **Emergency warnings**, for a strong possibility of the kind of devastating disaster that happens once every several decades.

The FCDO advises following local authorities' instructions, including evacuation orders. The same safety site lists current JMA warnings and earthquake information, and explains that some hotels and Japanese inns are designated to take in evacuees. Ask your accommodation about this.

## Autumn: leaves and festivals

JNTO says trees turn red, orange and yellow in many places from mid-November to early December. Most of Honshu and Kyushu are pleasantly mild in October and November, while Hokkaido is cold enough for a winter jacket for much of the season. JMA also records the dates when maple and ginkgo trees change colour. JNTO notes that many Japanese people take holidays in late September, so that week is busier.

Autumn's lower humidity and settled weather make it a good time for walking. Typhoon season does not officially end until December, though, so the September and October warnings above still apply.

## Questions to ask your operator

- If my dates fall in cherry blossom or autumn colour season, how do you adjust the itinerary when the season runs early or late?
- What happens if a typhoon or heavy-rain warning cancels a day's transport: who rebooks, and who pays for extra nights?
- Is my trip timed around Golden Week, Obon or the late-September holidays, and have transport and rooms already been booked for those dates?
- In July and August, how do you plan days to avoid the worst of the heat?
- How will you contact me if JMA issues a warning or emergency warning where I am staying?
- Does my travel insurance need to cover weather disruption, and what does your cancellation policy say about it?
