At dawn southeast of Ankara, balloons rise over a valley of cones, pillars and cave doorways cut into pale volcanic rock. Cappadocia's landscape is both a natural wonder and a human one: people have carved homes, churches and whole underground towns into the soft stone for centuries. The essentials are simple. Göreme is the heart of the World Heritage site, the best sights are spread across several valleys, and balloon flights depend entirely on calm weather.

## How the fairy chimneys formed

Göreme National Park and the Rock Sites of Cappadocia was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage list in 1985. The IUCN World Heritage Outlook describes the Göreme valley as carved from a plateau of lava-covered tuff, a fine-grained rock made of consolidated volcanic ash. Erosion has cut it into hundreds of cones and lava-capped pillars: the fairy chimneys.

Go Türkiye, the country's official tourism site, explains why many chimneys wear a "hat". At Paşabağ, the conical bodies of tuff and volcanic ash are capped by harder rock, which protected the softer stone beneath as the surroundings wore away.

## Churches, valleys and underground cities

The IUCN notes rock-hewn sanctuaries with Byzantine art from the post-Iconoclastic period, along with troglodyte villages and underground towns dating back to the 4th century. Göreme was an important Christian centre between the 7th and 13th centuries.

Highlights named by Go Türkiye include:

- **Göreme Open Air Museum.** A cluster of rock-cut churches, among them the Tokalı Church, noted for its wall paintings, and the Dark Church (Karanlık), dated to the late 11th or early 12th century. Little light enters through its small window, which helped preserve its frescoes.
- **Paşabağ.** Fairy chimneys about 1 km off the Göreme–Avanos road, including a three-headed chimney holding a chapel dedicated to St Simeon and a hermit's shelter.
- **Devrent Valley** and its "Three Beauties" chimneys, and **Zelve**, with the most fairy chimneys of the sites listed.
- **Red Valley (Kızıl Vadi)** near Çavuşin and Ortahisar, at its best around sunset; **Pigeon Valley**, walkable from Uçhisar Castle.
- **Ihlara Valley**, about 14 km long with 14 open churches. The descent has 382 steps.
- **Underground cities.** Derinkuyu is about 85 m deep, with only around 10% open to visitors. Kaymaklı has eight levels, four of them open.

## Balloon flights: what to know

Balloon flights are the image most people bring home. Go Türkiye describes them ascending at sunrise under a clear sky, and the practical reality is that the weather decides everything.

According to Türkiye's Ministry of Culture and Tourism:

- Balloons can stay aloft for long periods in calm conditions, with wind under 10 km/h.
- A suitable flight altitude is 500 to 1,500 feet.
- Before flying, operators must notify the Directorate General of Civil Aviation (DGCA, known in Turkish as SHGM) of the registration, purpose, date, time and region of the flight.

Because conditions must be calm, flights can be cancelled at short notice. Build in more than one morning if the flight matters to you, and read the operator's written cancellation and refund terms before paying.

The FCDO says British nationals have been injured and killed doing extreme sports in Turkey. It advises checking that safety precautions are in place, getting full instructions before the activity and making sure your travel insurance covers everything you do. It gives similar warnings for jeep safaris and quad biking.

## Visiting responsibly

The IUCN's 2025 assessment rates the site's conservation outlook as "significant concern". It lists uncontrolled tourism as a high threat, estimating 2 to 4 million visitors a year. The Göreme Open Air Museum and the Three Beauties area have the highest visitor and vehicle density, and the IUCN notes that ATV tours and road construction damage the landscape.

You can help by visiting busy sites early or late, keeping to marked paths on soft rock, and choosing walking routes through the valleys over motorised tours.

## Entry and getting around

The FCDO says British citizens can visit Turkey without a visa for up to 90 days in any 180-day period for tourism. If you plan to drive, you need a 1968 international driving permit or a UK licence notarised in Turkish, plus a green card for third-party insurance; the FCDO advises taking care on the roads, particularly at night.

## Questions to ask your operator

- Is your balloon operation authorised by the Directorate General of Civil Aviation (SHGM)?
- What happens if my flight is cancelled for weather: rebooking, refund, or both?
- How many passengers will be in the basket, and what safety briefing do you give?
- Does my travel insurance cover balloon flights, ATV or jeep tours?
- Which valleys and churches can we reach on foot from my hotel?
