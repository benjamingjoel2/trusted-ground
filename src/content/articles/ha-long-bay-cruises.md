Cruising Ha Long Bay is not a free-for-all: boats follow fixed sightseeing routes set by the provincial authorities, and every visitor needs an entrance ticket for the route and length of stay they are booked on. The weather matters too, because tropical storms affect Vietnam's coast for much of the year. The UK government's advice is blunt about safety: check a boat's registration and record before you board, especially for an overnight trip.

## How the routes and tickets work

Ha Long Bay is a UNESCO World Natural Heritage Site managed by the Ha Long Bay – Yen Tu World Heritage Management Board, which runs the official site at halongbay.com.vn. Visits are organised around numbered routes from the main passenger ports, Tuan Chau International Marina and Ha Long International Cruise Port. Each route takes in a set group of caves, islets and beaches, such as Thien Cung grotto and Dau Go cave on Route 1, or Sung Sot cave and Ti Top island on Route 2.

According to the board, entrance fees are charged per person, per route, under a decision of the Quang Ninh Provincial People's Committee. There are separate prices for:

- **Day visits**, which the board lists as running between 06:30 and 18:30.
- **Overnight stays**, priced by route and by number of nights. One night means 24 hours from departure and two nights means 48 hours. Overnight boats moor only in designated areas on certain routes.

The board's published fee table and its list of itineraries do not fully match at the time of writing (November 2026), so we have not quoted prices here. Ask your operator which route your boat is licensed for, whether the entrance ticket is included in your price, and check the current figures on the board's Entrance tickets page.

## Rules on the water

The board's visitor notes ask travellers to:

- Follow the rules of the ports, piers and attractions.
- Not throw rubbish or other waste into the bay.
- Not break plants or take flowers, leaves or stalactites from the caves.
- Not buy or sell coral.
- Buy tickets for the sightseeing route or overnight stay as prescribed.

The same notes warn against touts. The board advises visitors not to hire boats from people soliciting outside the passenger ports, and not to deal with brokers offering hotels, meals or cruises along the roads into Ha Long and Tuan Chau. Instead it suggests going into the port and using the board's information centre for routes, tickets and services.

## Seasons and weather

The UK Foreign, Commonwealth & Development Office (FCDO) says tropical cyclones affect Vietnam's eastern coastal regions. The season normally runs from May to November, but storms can happen at other times. They bring strong winds, heavy rain, flooding and transport disruption. In the north, the FCDO says the rain season runs from June to September.

What this means for a cruise:

- **Build in slack.** The FCDO advises being ready to change plans at short notice if you travel during the storm season.
- **Watch the forecasts.** The FCDO points travellers to Vietnam's National Centre for Hydro-Meteorological Forecasting and the Japan Meteorological Agency for approaching storms.
- **Follow local instructions,** including any evacuation orders.

Before you pay, ask what happens to your booking if sailings are cancelled because of the weather. See our guide to deposits and payment protection and check your travel insurance covers weather disruption.

## Choosing a boat safely

The FCDO says overcrowding and poor safety standards have led to accidents on Ha Long Bay in past years, and that some boats have sunk quickly and without warning. It advises travellers to:

- Check the safety record and registration of the boat, and the certification of its crew.
- Make sure they get a full safety briefing.
- Have a life jacket when joining any boat.
- Think carefully about safety standards before taking an overnight trip.

The management board publishes a list of tourist boats on its website, grouped as overnight boats, daytime sightseeing boats and restaurant boats. Each entry shows the boat's registration number, home port and passenger capacity. Use it to check that the boat you are offered is listed for the type of trip you are booking, and that the group size you are quoted is within its capacity. A listing does not prove a boat is well run, but a boat missing from the list for the trip you are booking is worth questioning.

During peak season, the board says it carries out inspections at busy sites such as Ti Top beach. These cover lifeguard services, lifesaving equipment, weather warning systems and emergency response plans for incidents at sea.

## Questions to ask your operator

- What is the boat's name and registration number, and is it on the management board's list for overnight or day trips?
- Which numbered route will we follow, and are the bay entrance tickets included in the price?
- How many passengers will be on board, and what is the boat's licensed capacity?
- Will there be a safety briefing, and are there life jackets for every passenger, including children's sizes?
- What happens to our booking and payment if the authorities stop sailings because of a storm?
- Which port do we leave from, and how will we be met there so we are not approached by touts?
