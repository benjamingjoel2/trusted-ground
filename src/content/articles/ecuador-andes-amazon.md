Mainland Ecuador packs high Andean cities, glaciated volcanoes, misty cloud forest and Amazon rainforest into one compact country. Most trips start in Quito, at 2,819 metres. Plan for altitude first, check which vaccines your route needs, and read the latest UK travel advice, which currently warns against travel to parts of the coast and the northern border.

## The Andes: cities, markets and volcanoes

Ecuador's official tourism site describes Quito as the oldest and highest capital in Latin America. Its historic centre is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Further south, Cuenca is also on the World Heritage list. It sits in a spring-like valley crossed by four rivers and is a centre for toquilla straw hats.

North of Quito, Otavalo is known for its Plaza de los Ponchos, which the tourism board calls the largest Indigenous market in Latin America. Crater lakes such as Quilotoa and Cuicocha are other highlights.

The volcanoes are the backdrop to all of this. Chimborazo, near Riobamba, is Ecuador's highest peak and, according to the tourism board, the point on Earth farthest from its centre. Baños de Agua Santa has volcanic hot springs and the Pailón del Diablo waterfall.

Volcanoes bring real hazards. The UK Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office (FCDO) warns of a "high risk" of lahars, which are mixed flows of water, mud, lava and debris, around glaciated volcanoes. Ashfall can close airports at short notice. The FCDO also says that:

- it is illegal to do high mountaineering or climb a glaciated mountain without an official accredited guide
- high mountaineering in Cotopaxi National Park is restricted, and since November 2023 has been allowed only under mandatory park rules and safety regulations

## Cloud forest: Mindo and the Chocó Andino

West of Quito, the Andes drop into cloud forest. Ecuador Travel describes the Chocó Andino around Mindo as a region of unique biodiversity. Its wildlife includes the olinguito, small wild cats, deer and the spectacled bear. Mindo is known for birdwatching, orchids and butterflies. Visitors can also go rafting or tubing on its rivers, see cacao being made into chocolate, and visit the Tulipe archaeological site of the Yumbo culture.

The FCDO's advice on river sports applies here and in the Amazon. Check that weather and currents are safe, avoid river crossings in heavy rain, and use operators with proper licences and insurance.

## The Amazon: Yasuní and beyond

East of the Andes, the land falls into the Amazon basin. Ecuador Travel describes Yasuní National Park as the largest protected area in continental Ecuador. It lies between the provinces of Orellana and Pastaza and is home to more than 1,400 animal species. Yasuní is a biosphere reserve and the ancestral territory of the Waorani people.

The tourism board also highlights the Cuyabeno reserve, with river dolphins, manatees, caimans and otters, and Tena as a gateway town on the Napo River. It encourages visitors to explore by canoe or on foot with guides, and to learn from Kichwa, Shuar and Waorani communities.

Before you choose a lodge, check where it is. The FCDO advises against all but essential travel within 20 km of the border with Colombia, apart from some listed areas of Carchi province.

## Altitude, vaccines and health

**Altitude.** TravelHealthPro, the UK's national travel health service, says altitude illness "usually occurs at altitudes over 2,500m". Quito is above that line. Flying straight in means you cannot climb gradually, so TravelHealthPro says rest days "should be strongly considered". Above 3,000 metres, it advises not raising your sleeping altitude by more than 500 metres a day. Descend if symptoms get worse.

**Yellow fever.** TravelHealthPro recommends vaccination for travellers aged nine months and over visiting areas below 2,300 metres in the Amazon provinces east of the Andes. These are Morona-Santiago, Napo, Orellana, Pastaza, Sucumbíos and Zamora-Chinchipe. Esmeraldas, on the west side, is also on the list. Vaccination is not recommended if you are only visiting areas above 2,300 metres, the cities of Quito and Guayaquil, or the Galápagos.

**Malaria.** TravelHealthPro says there is "a low risk of malaria in areas below 1,500m" on the mainland, including the Amazon basin. Bite avoidance is the main advice. Discuss whether you need tablets with a travel health professional.

## Safety: read the current advice

At the time of writing (November 2026), the FCDO advises against all but essential travel to several coastal provinces. These are Esmeraldas, Manabí, Santa Elena, Guayas, El Oro, Los Ríos and Santo Domingo de los Tsáchilas. The advice does not apply to airside transit at Guayaquil Airport. The FCDO also notes a State of Emergency, with a heightened police and military presence nationwide. This advice can change quickly, and it may affect your insurance, so check it when you book and again before you fly.

## Before you go

- Plan one or two easy days in Quito before going higher.
- Use an accredited guide for any glaciated peak or high climb, as the law requires.
- Check how close your Amazon lodge is to the Colombian border, and read the FCDO advice for that area.
- See a travel health professional well before you travel about yellow fever and malaria.
- Check your insurance covers altitude, adventure activities and evacuation from remote areas.
- Re-check the FCDO advice on coastal provinces and the State of Emergency before you travel.
