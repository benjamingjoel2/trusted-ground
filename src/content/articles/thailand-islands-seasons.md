Thailand's two island coasts have opposite wet seasons. On the Andaman side (Phuket, Krabi, Phang Nga, Trang and Satun), the rainy season generally runs from May to October, and some marine national parks close for the monsoon. On the Gulf side, around Koh Samui, the UK government says the rainy season is usually November to March. In broad terms, the Andaman coast suits the northern winter, and the Gulf suits the middle of the year.

## Two seas, two calendars

To the west, the Andaman coast runs south from Surin, near the Myanmar border, to Ko Lipe in Satun province. To the east lie the islands of the Gulf of Thailand, Koh Samui among them. The two coasts are on opposite sides of the peninsula, and the monsoon reaches them at different times of year.

The UK Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office (FCDO) says Thailand's rainy season is generally from May to October, but that in Koh Samui and south-east Thailand it usually runs from November to March. Heavy rain can bring flash flooding, which the FCDO says puts lakes, caves and waterfalls particularly at risk.

## The Andaman coast: November onwards

The Tourism Authority of Thailand (TAT) describes the Andaman coast reopening for a new season each autumn. In 2026, Maya Bay on Ko Phi Phi Le, in Krabi province, reopened to visitors on 1 October. The Similan Islands, in Phang Nga, reopened on 15 October after a five-month monsoon closure, with more than 1,300 visitors arriving on the first day.

TAT says October and early November can still bring rain, and boat operators sometimes stay in port when the waves are high. It expects the dry season proper to settle in by December. Underwater visibility for divers peaks in the dry months. TAT advises booking ferries and island accommodation early for December and January.

The coast covers several provinces, each with its own character, according to TAT:

- **Phuket**
- **Phang Nga**, including Khao Lak, Phang Nga Bay and the Similan Islands, with Surin further north near the Myanmar border
- **Krabi**, including Railay, Ao Nang and the Phi Phi islands
- **Trang**, with Ko Libong, Ko Muk and Ko Kradan
- **Satun**, with Tarutao and Ko Lipe

Since October 2025, TAT says, six marine national parks have used electronic tickets. They include the Similans, Surin, Phi Phi, Phang Nga Bay and Ko Lanta. Buy tickets in advance and carry your passport.

## The Gulf coast: the other half of the year

Because the Gulf's rainy season usually runs from November to March, according to the FCDO, it is the natural choice when the Andaman side is wet, roughly May to October. In practice that means the Gulf is often the better bet for a summer trip, and the Andaman coast for the northern winter.

Neither coast shuts down entirely in its wet season, although some marine parks close. The bigger change is the sea. Rough water can cancel boat trips, and boat safety deserves more thought (see below). TAT notes that marine activities depend on local weather and advises checking schedules before you travel, as ferry timetables may change with the conditions.

## Safety at sea

The FCDO's advice for Thailand's beaches and boats:

- **Riptides.** People have drowned in strong riptides. Take care when swimming, especially in the monsoon season, and follow warning signs, especially red flags. Lifeguards are on duty at designated beaches.
- **Jellyfish.** Jellyfish can come close to shore from May to October, and their stings can be fatal. Ask your hotel or dive centre for local advice.
- **Boats.** Boats have sunk or collided, causing deaths, including of British nationals. Overloading, poor maintenance and rough seas, particularly in the monsoon season, are among the causes.
- **Diving.** Standards vary. Ask to see a dive operator's credentials, check there is oxygen on the boat, and ask how you would be taken to a hyperbaric chamber in an emergency.
- **Parties.** The FCDO notes that violent sexual assaults and unprovoked attacks are most common during full moon parties or similar events, and near bars late at night.

## Before you go

- Pick your coast by month: Andaman for roughly November to April, Gulf when the Andaman is wet.
- If you are going early or late in the season, check whether marine parks such as the Similans or Maya Bay are open.
- Buy marine park e-tickets in advance and keep your passport with you on boat days.
- Book December and January ferries and island rooms early.
- Ask boat operators about life jackets and passenger numbers, and do not board an overloaded boat.
- Leave a spare day either side of island hops in case rough seas cancel crossings.
