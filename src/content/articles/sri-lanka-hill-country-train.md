Sri Lanka's hill country railway was built for coffee and tea, and it still winds slowly through tea estates on the way from Kandy towards Ella and Badulla. It is one of the island's great journeys, but it needs some planning. Reserve seats through Sri Lanka Railways' own system, check that the line is running, especially in monsoon season, and stay inside the carriage while the train moves.

## A railway built for plantations

Sri Lanka Railways tells the story itself. Rail arrived in 1864 "to transport coffee from plantations in the hill country district of Kandy to the port city of Colombo". After the coffee blight of 1871 destroyed many plantations, tea replaced coffee. Tea growing expanded in the 1880s, and so did the railways. Another 100 miles of track was built in the planting districts to carry tea down to Colombo and bring workers, machinery and food up to the hills. A branch was laid from Nanu Oya to serve the plantations around Nuwara Eliya.

That history explains how the line feels today. According to Sri Lanka Railways, the hill country alignment was set about 140 years ago. To keep earthworks and bridges small, engineers allowed gradients of 2 to 3%, giving a route that is "winding with very sharp curves". Trains reached speeds of only 25 to 40 km/h in the hill country. Allow plenty of time, and treat the journey as the point.

## The tea country outside the window

The Sri Lanka Tea Board lists seven tea regions, several of them in or near the hill country:

- **Nuwara Eliya.** Its mountains rise to more than 6,000 feet above sea level. The board describes cool mornings, cold nights touched with frost, and "the lightest and the most refined tea".
- **Dimbula.** It is cool and dry from December to March, with rain from May to September. Dimbula is described as "probably, the most famous Ceylon Tea of all".
- **Uva.** Exposed to both monsoons, Uva gets winds that arrive dry, which the board says gives its tea a concentrated aroma.
- **Uda Pussellawa.** It has two seasons shaped by the southwest and northeast monsoons, and the board describes its tea as medium-bodied and tangy.
- **Kandy.** Here tea is grown at mid elevation, 2,000 to 4,000 feet, producing "robust, full-bodied teas".

The other two regions, Sabaragamuwa and Ruhuna, are lower-lying.

## Classes and reservations

Sri Lanka Railways lists intercity and express trains between Colombo Fort and Badulla, with first, second and third class and an observation saloon. It also runs a Kandy to Badulla slow train with an observation saloon. Ella is among the stations in its reservation system. Timetables and seat availability change, so check them in the official system rather than relying on old lists.

Seats can be reserved online at seatreservation.railway.gov.lk or through the railway's mobile app. Under Sri Lanka Railways' terms at the time of writing (November 2026):

- You must create an account and log in before reserving.
- You can reserve a maximum of five seats at once, and identity details are required for each passenger.
- An e-ticket and booking details are sent by email. A reservation "only becomes guaranteed once full payment has been received".
- Reserved tickets are valid only on the specified train.
- To collect a ticket at a station counter, you need your booking reference and your NIC or passport. Tickets "will not be issued to 3rd party".
- Customer cancellations are refunded at 75% more than seven days before departure and 50% between two and seven days. Nothing is refunded within 48 hours.

Be cautious about anyone reselling reserved seats, and buy through the official system or a station wherever you can.

## Riding safely, and the weather

The UK Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office (FCDO) is blunt about one risk on this route: "When travelling by train, make sure you stay within the train carriage while the train is moving". It says "there have been fatal accidents when visitors have hung out of open doorways or windows of trains". It also warns of theft, so keep belongings close.

The weather matters too. The FCDO says monsoon rains can cause flooding, and that "heavy rains and landslips can also lead to road closures and affect local transport links". It advises monitoring Sri Lanka's Disaster Management Centre and Department of Meteorology. For how the two monsoons divide the year, see our article on [Sri Lanka's monsoons](/articles/sri-lanka-monsoons/).

**Entry.** According to the FCDO, British visitors need an Electronic Travel Authorization (ETA) before travelling. It is issued free of charge and valid for 30 days. Your passport must be valid for at least six months after arrival. Other nationalities should check with Sri Lanka's Department of Immigration and Emigration.

## Before you go

- Check that the hill country line is running on your dates, particularly after heavy rain.
- Reserve seats through the official Sri Lanka Railways system, and bring the passport you booked with.
- Check the cancellation terms before you pay: refunds shrink close to departure.
- Allow plenty of time, as hill country trains run slowly.
- Stay inside the carriage while the train is moving, however tempting the open door.
- Apply for your ETA through official channels before you fly.
