Torres del Paine is run by CONAF, Chile's national forest and parks service, and its rules are stricter than many visitors expect. You need an entry pass bought through CONAF's Pases Parques platform, every night in the park must be booked at an authorised campsite, refuge or hotel, and lighting any fire outside the permitted cooking areas is a criminal offence. When you go shapes the rest: summer and winter have different opening hours, and parts of the W trek are guide-only in winter.

## Entry passes and opening hours

CONAF says official information on the park is only available from CONAF itself and from [Pases Parques](https://www.pasesparques.cl/), its digital ticketing site. Passes are bought for a specific date, priced by nationality and age group, and capacity is limited, so CONAF advises buying as early as possible, especially in high season, at weekends and on public holidays.

Some practical points from the Pases Parques help pages:

- Passes are non-transferable: only the named person can use them.
- If you arrive after the published "last entry" time you will be refused entry, with no refund.
- You can ask to change the date up to 24 hours before your visit by emailing CONAF. Refunds are only given when the park closes by official order, for example for weather or emergencies.

At the time of writing (November 2026), Pases Parques shows the park open 07:00 to 21:00 in the summer season (October to April) and 08:00 to 18:30 in winter (May to September), with hours subject to weather and safety decisions. It also says that sales of 2027 passes are paused during November 2026 while fees are adjusted for inflation, and will reopen from December 2026. We do not quote fees here: check the current price on Pases Parques when sales reopen.

## Campsites and refuges

Overnight stays are only allowed in authorised, signposted camping areas, refuges and hotels; CONAF states that sleeping anywhere else is strictly prohibited. Accommodation inside the park is run by concession holders. CONAF's park page lists refuges without road access at Paine Grande, Grey, Los Perros and Dickson, and hotels reachable by road.

On booking, CONAF is explicit on the Pases Parques page:

- Concession services (campsites, refuges, camping areas and hotels) have limited capacity, for conservation and logistical reasons.
- Bookings must be made in advance, directly with the authorised concession companies.
- Anyone with an overnight booking inside the park is guaranteed an entry pass for those dates.

The practical upshot: book your nights first, then your entry passes. If an operator is arranging a trek for you, ask for the confirmed booking for every night on the route, not just the first.

The Paine Massif circuit (the "O") was temporarily guide-only, but CONAF says that from 1 January 2026 visitors may again walk it independently, provided they meet all the safety, registration and booking requirements in the park's rules.

## Fire, stoves and other prohibitions

Fire is the rule most likely to land a visitor in serious trouble. CONAF's park page says lighting any heat source, naming barbecues, campfires, gas stoves and cigarettes, is prohibited and punishable under Law 20.653, article 22. Offenders are handed to the Carabineros (police) and penalties can reach fines of up to CLP 2,000,000, up to three years in prison, or expulsion from Chile for foreigners.

The one exception is cooking: CONAF allows stoves that do not burn plant material, only in the specially designated cooking areas of each campsite. Anywhere else, using a stove is prohibited and sanctioned under the same law. The UK FCDO adds that foreigners caught starting a fire in a Chilean national park may lose their tourist permit and be asked to leave within 72 hours or face deportation.

Other CONAF rules worth knowing:

- No smoking anywhere in the park, and no alcohol or drugs.
- No pets.
- No drones, except for research or professional filming with CONAF authorisation.
- Stay on the official trails shown on the park map; no off-trail walking or photography.
- Do not feed or disturb wildlife, or remove plants or stones.
- Carry all rubbish out or use the sealed bins provided.
- Cycling is allowed on vehicle roads but not on the W or Paine Massif trails.

## Trail safety and walking hours

CONAF prohibits walking on trails outside daylight hours, citing the risk of getting lost, falling or encountering a puma, and asks visitors to respect trail closing times and walk accompanied. It says most fatal accidents in the park have involved people walking alone and leaving the trails. CONAF lists mobile signal only at Villa Monzino, and help in an emergency can take hours to arrive.

Trails close at short notice. Since 1 January 2026 CONAF has used a new mountain trail protocol with set thresholds for wind, rain and temperature to decide preventive closures, working with Chile's disaster agency SENAPRED and the national weather service. When we checked, Pases Parques showed a weather-alert closure of several trails, including Base Torres.

## When to go

CONAF divides the year into two seasons:

- **Summer (October to April):** longer opening hours and the full range of concession services.
- **Winter (May to September):** CONAF lists snow risk on the main trails, and avalanche and landslide risk on some sections. Several W sections can only be walked with a guide in winter under the park's winter visiting protocol. Pases Parques says microcrampons and trekking poles are required, and points to CONAF's list of accredited guides and its winter activities regulation.

Whatever the season, the FCDO notes that Chile often has severe forest fires that have closed national parks and roads, so build some flexibility into your plans and check our [Chile guide](/countries/chile/).

## Questions to ask your operator

- Can you send confirmed bookings, from the concession companies, for every campsite or refuge night on my route?
- Who buys my Pases Parques entry pass, for which dates, and what happens if the park closes a trail or sector while I am there?
- If I am travelling between May and September, is my guide on CONAF's accredited list, and which sections require one?
- Where exactly can we cook, and what stove and fuel will we use?
- What is the plan if a weather alert closes trails mid-trek, and who pays for extra nights or transport?
- How do you communicate with the park and emergency services where there is no mobile signal?
