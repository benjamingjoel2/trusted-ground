Brazil offers two very different wildlife journeys. In the Amazon, you travel by boat through black-water channels and forest that floods and drains with the river. In the Pantanal, a vast wetland in the centre of the country, wildlife becomes easier to see as the floodwater recedes. Choose by what you want to see and when you can travel, then plan health precautions for both.

## Two kinds of water country

Brazil's Ministry of Tourism describes the Pantanal as "the largest continuous flood plain on planet Earth", a biome "in the heart of the country". Life there follows what ICMBio, Brazil's federal protected areas agency, calls the Pantanal's "flood pulse". In March 2026, ICMBio announced that it had enlarged the Pantanal Matogrossense National Park from 135,922 to about 183,000 hectares. It named the jaguar, giant armadillo, giant otter and marsh deer among the threatened species that use these flooded areas.

The Amazon is reached mainly from Manaus. About 40 km upstream on the Rio Negro lies the Anavilhanas archipelago. INPA, Brazil's national Amazon research institute, describes it as one of the largest river archipelagos in the world, with more than 400 islands. The islands change shape as the river's water level varies by up to 10 metres between high and low water. The Rio Negro's dark "black water" feeds igapó, a type of flooded forest.

The archipelago is protected as the Anavilhanas National Park, which covers 350,469.8 hectares according to ICMBio. The Amazon river dolphin, or pink boto, is among the threatened species it protects.

## Seasons: water levels decide the trip

**Pantanal.** ICMBio's visitor information for the Pantanal Matogrossense National Park gives these seasons:

- **Best time:** May to September, when it rains less. Wildlife viewing improves as the waters start to fall.
- **Rainy season:** October to April, with January and February the wettest months.
- **From December:** mosquito numbers become high, and the rains make the Transpantaneira road towards Porto Jofre "practically impassable".

**Amazon.** Here the experience changes with the river. INPA notes that the Anavilhanas islands change with the water level, which can rise and fall by up to 10 metres. Forest that is dry land in one season can be under water in another. Ask anyone you book with what the water level will be on your dates and what that means for the activities on offer.

## Getting into protected areas

The national park protects only part of the Pantanal, and it is remote. ICMBio says it is reached only by river, either about 150 km down the Cuiabá River from Porto Jofre or about 230 km up the Paraguay River from Corumbá in Mato Grosso do Sul.

There is no entrance fee, but you need prior authorisation to visit. There is also no tourism infrastructure inside the park, so visitors stay on houseboats from Corumbá or at hotels in Porto Jofre. ICMBio's visitor page is an older one, so contact the park office (listed by ICMBio in Cáceres, Mato Grosso) to confirm the current arrangements.

In the Amazon, ICMBio manages the Anavilhanas National Park from an office in Novo Airão, in Amazonas state. Check the current visiting arrangements with ICMBio, and find out what any trip includes for safety and communications, as these are remote waters.

## Health: plan both trips the same way

Both regions need the same preparation:

- **Yellow fever.** TravelHealthPro, the UK's national travel health service, recommends yellow fever vaccination for travellers aged nine months and over visiting a list of Brazilian states. The list includes Amazonas (where Manaus is), Mato Grosso and Mato Grosso do Sul, the two Pantanal states.
- **Malaria.** TravelHealthPro says there is "a low risk of malaria in the Amazon basin of Brazil, including in the city of Manaus". Ask a travel health professional whether you need antimalarial tablets for your route.
- **Mosquito bites.** Avoid bites at all times. TravelHealthPro advises extra care during the day against dengue, Zika and chikungunya. The FCDO lists dengue and yellow fever among the health risks in Brazil.
- **Timing.** The FCDO advises checking vaccination recommendations at least eight weeks before you travel.
- **Emergencies.** The ambulance number in Brazil is 192, but help is far away in both regions. Make sure your insurance covers evacuation.

## Which one suits you?

- **For wildlife viewing in open wetland,** the Pantanal in the drier months fits best. ICMBio's list of threatened species includes the jaguar, giant otter and marsh deer.
- **For rainforest, rivers and the feel of the Amazon basin,** a boat-based trip from Manaus onto the Rio Negro suits better.
- **For a tight schedule in the rainy season,** remember that ICMBio warns the Transpantaneira can become nearly impassable.

## Questions to ask your operator

- What will the water level be on my dates, and how does it change the itinerary?
- Does the trip enter a national park, and if so, who arranges the authorisation?
- How do you avoid disturbing wildlife, and how close do boats approach animals?
- What emergency communications and evacuation plans are in place?
- Are local guides from the area employed on the trip?
- What should I bring for mosquito protection?
