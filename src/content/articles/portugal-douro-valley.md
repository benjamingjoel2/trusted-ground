Steep schist hillsides carved into vineyard terraces, a broad green river curling between them, and wine estates, or quintas, perched above the water: the Douro Valley is where port wine comes from, and the landscape is its own advertisement. You can reach it by train or boat from Porto, taste at the source, and in late summer watch the harvest under way.

## The world's oldest demarcated wine region

The Instituto dos Vinhos do Douro e do Porto (IVDP), the public body that regulates the sector, describes the Douro as "the oldest demarcated and regulated region in the world (1756)", and notes that it is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Its head office is in Peso da Régua, in the heart of the valley.

The demarcated region covers 250,000 hectares, according to the IVDP, split into three sub-regions running upriver from west to east:

- **Baixo Corgo (Lower Corgo)**, the closest to Porto
- **Cima Corgo (Upper Corgo)**, the largest area under vines, around Pinhão
- **Douro Superior (Upper Douro)**, the driest and most remote, towards Spain

The IVDP explains that the Marão and Montemuro mountains shelter the valley from Atlantic winds, giving "very cold winters and hot, dry summers". Annual rainfall ranges from 1,200 mm at Fontes to 380 mm at Barca d'Alva near the border. Most of the soil is schist, and the IVDP notes that people "built the terraces prior to planting the vines", reshaping whole hillsides by hand. Red grapes include Touriga Nacional, Touriga Francesa, Tinta Roriz, Tinta Barroca, Tinta Amarela and Tinto Cão. Our [Portugal guide](/countries/portugal/) has more on the wider country.

## How port is made, and what the labels mean

Port is a fortified wine. According to the IVDP, producers add grape spirit to the fermenting must to stop fermentation while natural sweetness remains, typically one part spirit to four parts wine, giving a finished strength of 19 to 22 per cent. Traditional producers still crush grapes by foot in large stone tanks called lagares. Each spring, winemakers decide whether a wine will age in the Douro or in the bonded lodges of Vila Nova de Gaia, across the river from Porto.

On a tasting menu, the IVDP's special categories are a useful decoder:

- **Ruby Reserve:** young, powerful and fruity.
- **Late Bottled Vintage (LBV):** a ruby port from a single year, bottled after four to six years in wood.
- **Vintage:** from a single harvest, bottled two to three years later; the IVDP calls it "the only Port that ages in bottle".
- **Single Quinta Vintage:** a vintage port from one harvest and one estate.
- **Tawny with an indication of age (10, 20, 30 or 40 years):** oak-aged blends whose character reflects the stated average age.
- **Colheita:** a tawny from a single year, aged in cask for a minimum of seven years.
- **White port** also comes in aged styles, including Reserve, age-indicated and Colheita.

## Up the valley by train or river

**By train.** CP's Douro line runs from Porto to Pocinho, covering 172 km and serving 37 stations and halts. Much of the route follows the river past vineyard terraces, through Peso da Régua, Pinhão (whose station CP calls "one of the most beautiful railway stations in Portugal") and Tua. Regional and InterRegional services run on the line, and CP also operates a tourist service, the Douro Historical Train; check cp.pt for its current dates and route.

**By river.** The Douro waterway, managed by the port authority APDL, extends for about 210 km from the river mouth at Foz do Douro to Barca d'Alva. The 125-metre rise between the sea and Barca d'Alva is overcome by five locks, so a river trip involves rising through dams as well as admiring the view. Cruises range from day trips to multi-day voyages; compare what is included, how much time is spent on the water and how many locks you pass.

## The harvest

The vindima, or grape harvest, is the busiest and most atmospheric time in the valley. The IVDP says picking typically runs between "the end of August and mid-October". Exact timing depends on the weather each year.

CP runs a special harvest festival train each year. In 2026 it ran on Saturdays 19 and 26 September, from Porto-Campanhã to Pinhão, with coach transfers to a quinta for grape picking, lunch and a tasting, returning by train from Régua. At the time of writing, the 2026 adult fare was €86 in second class. Look for the next year's dates on cp.pt in late summer.

Late summer is also hot. The UK Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office (FCDO) warns of higher than normal temperatures in summer and an increased risk of wildfires from April to October. Starting a fire, even by accident, is illegal in Portugal.

## Before you go

- Decide between train and boat: the Douro line lets you hop off at Régua, Pinhão or Tua, while a cruise shows you the locks and the river itself.
- Book harvest-season accommodation and quinta visits early if you want to be there between late August and mid-October.
- Learn a few port categories before tasting, so you know what you are comparing.
- If you plan to drive between estates, choose a designated driver or join a tour, as tastings add up.
- In summer, carry water, avoid the midday heat on vineyard walks and follow local fire-risk advice.
