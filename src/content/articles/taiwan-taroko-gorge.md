Rock walls close in overhead, the river runs far below, and the road threads through tunnels cut into the cliff. Taroko Gorge, in Hualien County on Taiwan's east coast, has long been the island's signature landscape. It is also still recovering. The short answer: a magnitude 7.2 earthquake on 3 April 2024 and the typhoons that followed damaged the gorge road and many trails; a growing list of sites has reopened, but several of the best-known walks, including Shakadang and Swallow Grotto, are not expected to be repaired until 2031. Check the latest closures the week you travel, not the month you book.

## What happened, and what is open

According to the Taipei Times, the April 2024 earthquake seriously damaged the eastern section of Provincial Highway 8 inside Taroko Gorge, initially blocking 13 points on the road. Loosened rock and soil then made things worse: over the following two years, nine typhoons and heavy rains brought the number of damaged road sites to 28. By the end of 2025, 23 had been restored and five were still under construction, including four tunnels due for repair by July 2028. The government allocated NT$3 billion to restoration.

As reported in March 2026, these sites had reopened:

- The park visitor centre and the Taroko Terrace Trail
- Tiansiang and Dacingshui recreation areas
- Chongde Trail and the Dali-Datong Trail
- Liwushan and Cianyanlishan hiking trails

The paper reported that Buluowan Terrace and the Tunnel of Nine Turns were due to reopen later in 2026; check whether that has happened before you go.

## The trails still closed

Some of Taroko's most photographed walks are on a long timeline, according to the same March 2026 report:

- **Changchun (Eternal Spring) Shrine:** not expected to reopen until 2029 at the earliest. Repairs are complicated by the risk of rockfalls.
- **Shakadang Trail, Swallow Grotto Trail and Baiyang Trail:** not expected to be repaired until 2031.

These dates can move in either direction, as each typhoon season can cause new damage. Treat any third-party list of "open" sites with caution unless it is recent and matches the park's own notices.

## The high trails and permits

Beyond the roadside walks, Taroko's backcountry routes in the Ecological Protection Area need an advance application through Taiwan's official mountain and park entry system. On 28 August 2026, Taroko National Park Headquarters announced that trails in the Ecological Protection Area would reopen from midnight on 29 August, after a typhoon closure, and that existing applications would be restored without reapplying. At that point the reopening of the Mt Qingshui route and the Zhuilu Old Road was still to be announced.

The same notice warned that heavy local rain remained possible after the typhoon, that mountain trails could have loose soil and a high landslide risk, and asked hikers to report blocked trails or damaged facilities. The application system is also where closures and reopenings are published, so check it before you set off.

## Roads, driving and the east coast

The park headquarters lists three main roads in the park: Provincial Highways 8, 14A and 7A. It directs visitors to the Directorate General of Highways, Ministry of Transportation, and its "Happy Highway" app for road repair progress, traffic controls and opening hours on these routes.

Expect controls rather than a free-flowing road. As an example, Taiwan's Central News Agency reported that during emergency repairs on Highway 8 at km 157.7, in the mountains above the gorge, traffic between 2 and 24 September 2026 was released in only five windows a day (6.30am to 8am, 9.45am to 10.25am, 12pm to 1pm, 2.45pm to 3.25pm and 5pm to 6pm), with an overnight closure remaining in place. Schedules change with the work and the weather, so check them the day before you drive.

Weather is the other constraint. The UK Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office (FCDO) says Taiwan's typhoon season normally runs from May to November, with a risk of road blockages and landslides afterwards. It also notes that earthquakes are a risk in Taiwan, and advises checking the Taiwanese authorities' announcements of road closures after a typhoon or earthquake. That applies to the whole east-coast journey, not just the gorge.

## Before you go

- According to the FCDO at the time of writing (November 2026), UK visitors can stay in Taiwan without a visa for up to 90 days and must complete an online Taiwan Arrival Card within three days before arrival. Check the rules for your passport.
- Check the park's notices and the Taiwan mountain entry system for closures the week you travel.
- Apply in advance for any route in the Ecological Protection Area, and have a fallback walk ready if it stays shut.
- If you drive, check Highway 8 release windows the day before and allow for waits.
- Avoid stopping under cliffs or rockfall zones, and follow any closure signs.
- Make sure your travel insurance covers hiking and disruption from typhoons.
