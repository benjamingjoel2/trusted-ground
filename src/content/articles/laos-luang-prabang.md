Before sunrise, a line of monks walks barefoot and in silence through the old town, and people at the roadside place sticky rice in their bowls. Luang Prabang, a UNESCO World Heritage town between the Mekong and Nam Khan rivers, is one of Southeast Asia's most graceful places. It is also a living religious centre. Watch the alms giving quietly and from a distance, cover your shoulders and knees at temples, and remember that the ceremony is not put on for visitors.

## The morning alms giving

Tak bat, also known as sai bat, is an ancient daily Buddhist practice. Tourism Laos describes devotees rising before dawn to prepare sticky rice as an offering for "barefoot monks who walk in silence and meditation through the town". Laypeople provide the monks' daily food, and the monks in return give merit.

According to Tourism Laos, monks and novices appear with their alms bowls from about 5.30am. The main procession passes in front of Wat Xieng Thong, Luang Prabang Primary School and the National Museum.

## How to watch it respectfully

The official Tourism Luang Prabang website sets out guidelines for visitors. In summary:

- **Observe in silence.** The whole ceremony takes place without talking.
- **Offer only if it means something to you.** The site asks visitors to give only if it is meaningful to them and they can do so respectfully.
- **Buy rice with care.** If you do give, buy sticky rice at the local market earlier that morning, or from vendors along the route. Tourism Laos also advises buying offerings in advance and arriving in good time.
- **Dress modestly:** shoulders, chests and legs should be covered.
- **Keep your distance.** Do not touch the monks, and try to stay at least 5 metres away while observing.
- **No flash.** The site says camera flashes are "very disturbing for both monks and the lay people", and asks photographers not to stand too close.
- **No buses.** Large buses are forbidden within the World Heritage site. Never follow the procession on a bus, as it puts you above the monks.

## Temples worth seeing

**Wat Xieng Thong.** Founded in 1560 under King Setthathirath, according to Tourism Luang Prabang, at the tip of the peninsula. Tourism Laos says it hosted the highest royal ceremonies and holds the remains of King Sisavangvong. The main hall has a low, three-tiered roof, glass mosaic murals and, at the back, a large Bodhi tree picked out in coloured glass. Tourism Luang Prabang gives its opening hours as 6am to 6pm daily. At the time of writing (November 2026), it lists the entry fee for foreigners as 30,000 kip, rising to 60,000 kip from 15 November 2026. Lao nationals enter free.

**Wat Visounnarath.** Founded in 1512 during the reign of King Visounnarath. Its stupa, That Pathoum, is known locally as That Makmo, the "watermelon stupa", for its rounded top. Tourism Luang Prabang says the dome form is found nowhere else in Laos. The stupa collapsed in 1914 and was restored in 1932.

**Mount Phousi.** In the middle of the old town, 328 steps lead to the 20-metre Chomsi stupa and views over the Mekong and the city, especially at sunset. Tourism Laos notes that it is a sacred site: dress respectfully, and alcohol is strictly prohibited.

**Royal Palace Museum.** Built in 1904, during the French colonial era, it houses the Pha Bang Buddha image. Tourism Laos says it is in a small room that is easy to miss, at the eastern end of the palace's south terrace.

## Temple etiquette and local customs

Tourism Luang Prabang's guidance for Wat Xieng Thong applies at any temple:

- Cover your body from shoulders to knees.
- Remove your hat and shoes.
- Show respect to monks and novices. Women may not touch them.
- Avoid public displays of affection.

The UK Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office (FCDO) also asks visitors to cover shoulders and knees at religious sites, whether male or female. It adds that walking around in swimwear in public is not culturally appropriate. It points out that Laos has a zero-tolerance policy on drugs, and that photographing military sites is prohibited.

After dark, Tourism Laos lists a night market on Sisavangvong Road, open daily from 5pm to 10pm. Our [Laos travel guide](/countries/laos/) covers the rest of the country.

## Before you go

- Pack light clothing that covers shoulders, chest and knees.
- Decide in advance whether you will give alms. If you do, buy sticky rice that morning and give quietly.
- Turn off your camera flash and stand at least 5 metres from the procession.
- Choose a small group or go on foot: large buses are banned in the World Heritage area.
- Bring small notes in kip for temple entry fees.
- Leave alcohol behind when you climb Mount Phousi.
