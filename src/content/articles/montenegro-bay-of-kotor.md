Mountains rise almost straight out of the water, and Kotor's stone walls zigzag up the slope behind the town to a fortress high above the roofs. The Bay of Kotor, known locally as Boka Kotorska, is Montenegro's best-known landscape and one of the busiest cruise stops in the Adriatic. Climb the walls early, check which days the big ships are in, and give the rest of the bay time too.

## Kotor's walls and fortress

The Montenegro National Tourism Organisation describes Kotor's defences as a single system. The walls rise from the sea and climb the surrounding peaks in a zigzag to the fortress guarding the town, about 300 metres above sea level. Then they come back down to the sea, forming a stone circuit 4.5 kilometres long. The tourism board puts the climb at about 1,400 steps and suggests allowing an hour to explore.

UNESCO lists the Kotor region as World Heritage. Its fortifications are also part of the Venetian "Stato da Mar" defence system, which the tourism board describes as a network of six fortified sites in Montenegro, Croatia and Italy that guarded the sea routes from the Adriatic to the Levant. Inside the walls, the tourism board highlights the Cathedral of St Tryphon (Tripun), the churches of St Luke and St Mary, the 17th-century Prince's Palace and the 15th-century Church of Our Lady of Health.

Check opening arrangements and any charges locally before you climb, as they can change. The steps are steep and exposed, so take water and set off early in summer.

## Cruise days

The Port of Kotor calls the town the third cruise destination in the Adriatic, after Venice and Dubrovnik. It notes that simply sailing through the bay is an attraction in itself. Its berths sit right in front of the old town walls, so on busy days the lanes fill quickly. The port publishes a **cruise ship arrivals** schedule on its website. Check it before fixing your Kotor day, and if you can, visit the old town early in the morning or in the evening, or on a day with fewer ship calls.

## The quieter shore

Much of the bay's charm lies outside Kotor. The tourism board suggests:

- **Perast and Our Lady of the Rocks.** Boat tours from Kotor and Perast often call at the islet church of Gospa od Škrpjela, which the tourism board describes as almost 400 years old and Venetian-built. Inside is a tapestry that Jacinta Kunić of Perast wove from 1803 to 1828 while waiting for her sailor husband to come home, partly using strands of her own hair.
- **Risan,** where you can see Roman mosaics.
- **Herceg Novi,** at the mouth of the bay, with fortresses and façades from Turkish, Venetian, Spanish and Austrian times. Its Mimosa Festival welcomes the spring with music, parades and flowers.
- **Tivat and Luštica.** Tivat has a Nautical Museum where you can see a submarine. Luštica has the Blue Cave, a sea cave with deep blue water, often included in boat tours.
- **The fortresses at the bay's mouth.** On a cruise towards the open sea you pass Arza, Mamula and Prevlaka, three Austro-Hungarian forts from the 19th century.
- **Shellfish farms** around Herceg Novi and Risan, where mussels and oysters are raised. A classic local preparation is *buzara*, cooked with olive oil, wine, garlic and herbs.

## Boka traditions

The tourism board describes the Boka Navy as a seafaring brotherhood founded in 809 and still active today, the oldest of its kind in the world. Its members perform the traditional *kolo* dance of St Tryphon, which has 12 named figures, at festivals including the outdoor St Tryphon celebration, the Day of the Boka Navy and the Day of the Municipality of Kotor. Kotor also hosts a carnival, a tradition the tourism board says goes back half a millennium.

## Safety and practicalities

The UK Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office (FCDO) warns of pickpockets in tourist hot spots and advises always carrying ID, such as a passport or photocard driving licence, because police can fine you if you don't have it. It also notes that coastal roads are often congested in summer and journey times can be much longer than expected. Use licensed taxis and check the fare per kilometre or agree the price in advance. When swimming, watch for speedboats and other watercraft, and use designated swimming areas where they exist. The emergency number is 112. ## Before you go

- Check the Port of Kotor's cruise arrivals schedule and plan your old-town visit around the busiest days.
- Climb the walls early in the day, with water and proper shoes, and check current access arrangements locally.
- Base yourself or spend a day elsewhere in the bay, such as Perast, Herceg Novi or Tivat, to see its quieter side.
- Carry ID at all times, and watch your belongings in crowded areas.
- Allow extra time for coastal roads in summer, and use licensed taxis.
