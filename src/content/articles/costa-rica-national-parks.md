Many of Costa Rica's most visited national parks sell entry tickets online through the national conservation agency SINAC, and some, such as Manuel Antonio, sell them only online, so buy before you go. Guiding is usually an optional extra, but Corcovado can be visited only with an authorised guide. On the Pacific side the rainy season runs from about May to October and can close park sectors and roads.

## Buying tickets online through SINAC

Park entry is managed by SINAC (Sistema Nacional de Áreas de Conservación). Its online reservation and payment platform, SICORE, sells tickets for the parks listed in its FAQs: Volcán Poás, Manuel Antonio, Volcán Irazú, Braulio Carrillo (Volcán Barva sector), Tapantí, Tortuguero, Isla San Lucas, Chirripó, Volcán Tenorio, Rincón de la Vieja and Carara. The booking menu also lists Los Quetzales.

What SINAC says you need to know before buying:

- **You must register first.** You create an account and receive a password by email before you can book.
- **Have everyone's details ready.** You will need the park, the date and the entry time or sector, the number and type of visitors (residents, non-residents, children), and each visitor's full name, identity document and country of origin.
- **The ticket covers entry only.** Guiding, equipment hire, food and transport are booked separately.
- **Refunds are limited.** SINAC says it is responsible only as set out in the terms and conditions accepted at purchase, and that bad weather is not grounds for a refund. Date changes depend on each protected area's own terms.
- **Parks not on the system** sell tickets at the park entrance, payable by credit or debit card. SINAC says bank transfers are not accepted there.
- **Free days.** Under the tariff decree, the third Wednesday of each month is free for nationals and residents only, and 24 August (National Parks Day) is free for all visitors.

The Costa Rica Tourism Board's (ICT) sheet for Manuel Antonio states that tickets there are sold online only and that the purchase code must be shown at the park office. It also lists the park as closed on Tuesdays. Opening days and hours can change, so check SICORE before you set your itinerary.

## When a guide is required

**Corcovado.** The ICT's Corcovado sheet states that you may visit the park only with an authorised guide and a reservation, and that you need a passport or national ID. It gives a lead time of at least six months for reservations. SINAC's FAQs say Corcovado (and Isla del Caño) are not sold through SICORE: you request a reservation by email to the park's reservations office, then pay by bank transfer receipt. The El Tigre sector is booked through a local conservation association rather than SINAC. In practice, your operator or guide usually makes these arrangements, so ask who holds the reservation in your name.

**Chirripó.** No guide requirement is stated for Costa Rica's highest peak, but booking has two steps. According to the ICT sheet, you reserve park entry with SINAC, then separately reserve the mountain lodge, meals and porters. You must also register at the park office in San Gerardo de Rivas the day before you climb. The sheet warns that temperatures can drop below freezing.

**Other parks.** Elsewhere, SINAC's ticket covers entry only, and guiding is a separate service you can choose to book. At Manuel Antonio, the ICT advises hiring certified guides only at the park entrance, checking the price and what is included first, and not trusting strangers who approach you outside. The UK Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office (FCDO) advises going with an experienced local guide in jungle areas, and notes that there are fines for entering restricted conservation areas without authorisation.

## The rainy season, coast by coast

Costa Rica's National Meteorological Institute (IMN) describes two very different rainfall patterns:

- **Pacific slope** (including Guanacaste, Manuel Antonio and the Osa Peninsula): a well-defined dry season from December to March, with April as a transition month. The rainy season runs from May to October, and November is a transition month. Rainfall dips in July and August (the *veranillo*). September and October are the wettest months. The dry season starts first in the north-west and last in the south-east.
- **Caribbean slope** (including Tortuguero): no defined dry season. The IMN says the coast has two relatively drier spells, February to March and September to October, and that the heaviest rains fall from November to January, with December the wettest month.

So September and October, the wettest months on the Pacific side, can be a relatively drier spell on the Caribbean coast.

The FCDO describes June to November as the peak rainy season and the hurricane season. It warns that flooding and landslides can close roads. It also advises avoiding volcanic areas during and immediately after heavy rain, and notes a recent series of significant eruptions at Poás.

## How the weather affects park visits

- **Sectors can close.** The ICT's Corcovado sheet says sectors open and close according to weather conditions, and that the Sirena station is closed to visitors in October. It names December to April as the best time to visit.
- **Plan for slow roads.** Landslides and potholes can add hours to transfers in the wet months, so avoid tight connections.
- **Book flexibly.** Because SINAC does not refund tickets for bad weather, ask your operator what happens if a park or sector closes on your date.
- **Insurance.** The FCDO notes that activities such as zip-lining, rock-climbing and surfing are often excluded from general travel insurance. See our [insurance guide](/guide/insurance/).

## Questions to ask your operator

- Will you buy our SINAC tickets for us, and will they be issued in our names with the right identity documents?
- For Corcovado, who is our authorised guide, which sector are we entering, and is the reservation confirmed in writing?
- If a park or sector closes because of weather on our date, what is the alternative and is any of the cost refunded?
- Does the guide fee cover only the guide, or entry, transport and meals too?
- For Chirripó, have both the park permit and the lodge been booked, and who handles the registration the day before?
- How do you adjust routes and transfer times in the rainy season, especially in September and October?
