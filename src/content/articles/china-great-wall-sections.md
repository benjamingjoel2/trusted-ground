Grey brick ramparts ride the ridgelines north of Beijing, dropping into saddles and climbing again to watchtowers that fade into the haze. In autumn the slopes below turn red; in winter a light snow can outline every battlement. The Great Wall is not one site but many, and near Beijing the main choice is between a handful of restored, ticketed sections. The short answer: Badaling is the best known and the busiest; Mutianyu runs a reservation-based system with cable cars and a long open stretch of towers; Huanghuacheng is the section where the wall meets water. Whichever you choose, carry the passport you booked with.

## Choosing a section

**Badaling.** According to the Beijing Municipal Government, Badaling is in Yanqing District, 60km north-west of Beijing, and was listed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1987. It was a strategic military barrier protecting the Ming dynasty capital and imperial tombs, and is the best-known face of the wall. It is also the busiest: when Badaling introduced real-name online ticketing in June 2019, China Daily reported a daily cap of 65,000 visitors, set to manage crowd pressure and safety.

**Mutianyu.** Mutianyu is in Mutianyu Village, Huairou District. Its official site says that in the daytime visitors can walk the section between watchtowers No. 1 and No. 20; at night the section from towers No. 10 to No. 15 is open. Cable car, ropeway and slide services are run by separate companies, are ticketed separately from wall entry, and their tickets are not interchangeable.

**Huanghuacheng Lakeside.** The Beijing government describes Huanghuacheng, in Jiuduhe Town, Huairou District, as about 70km from downtown Beijing. Three sections of the wall here are submerged in water, which the government page describes as making it the only scenic area in Beijing where the Great Wall meets water; boat tours are part of the visit.

## Tickets and ID checks

China's main heritage sites run on real-name ticketing: the ticket is tied to the identity document you book with, and that document is checked at the gate.

**Mutianyu**, according to its official ticketing page at the time of writing (November 2026):

- Tickets must be reserved with real-name details and can be booked up to 15 days ahead.
- Each ID card or passport is limited to one ticket per day, and every visitor needs their own ticket.
- Foreign visitors who booked with a passport enter by showing the original passport at the designated gate; there is no need to exchange the booking for a paper ticket.
- Unused tickets can be refunded or changed on the ticketing platform up to midnight on the day of the reservation.
- The shuttle bus station is about 3km from the ticket gate, and the shuttle bus is a separate ticket. Visitors with cable car tickets must use the North Ticket Gate; those with ropeway or slide tickets use the Slide Ticket Gate.
- Discounts apply to foreign minors aged over 6 and up to 18, and to some other groups; take the relevant document.

**Badaling**, according to the Beijing Municipal Government's page at the time of writing, needs no reservation, and tickets can be bought on site. It lists adult tickets at CNY 40 in peak season and CNY 35 off-season, with free entry for minors under 18 and seniors aged 60 and over on presentation of valid ID. Rules at Badaling have changed several times since 2019, so check the official channels shortly before you go, especially around Chinese public holidays.

## Seasons and opening hours

Both main sections run peak and off-peak timetables:

- **Badaling:** peak season 1 April to 31 October, 6.30am to 4.30pm; off-season 1 November to 31 March, 7.30am to 4pm (Beijing Municipal Government).
- **Mutianyu:** peak season 16 March to 15 November, with the cable car running 7.30am to 6pm on weekdays and to 6.30pm at weekends; off-peak from 16 November to 15 March, 8am to 5.30pm. Ticket sales stop an hour before closing.

Mutianyu's own seasonal notes describe red autumn leaves lasting until mid-November and early snow on the wall. The scenic area warns that it may close fully or partly in severe weather, including thunderstorms, heavy rain, snow, hail, fog and strong winds, so watch for announcements on the day.

Whatever the season, the wall is steep in places. Mutianyu's visitor rules ask people not to climb on the battlements, carve or write on the wall, or bring open flames, and advise older visitors and those with health conditions to choose activities that suit them.

## The "wild wall" is off limits

Unrestored sections of the wall, often called the "wild wall", attract hikers looking for solitude. They are not open to visitors. The Global Times, a state-run newspaper, reported in 2020 that Beijing prohibits organising climbs on sections of the Great Wall that are not open, with fines of between 200 and 30,000 yuan, and that heritage authorities in Yanqing and Huairou districts were stepping up patrols. A Yanqing District cultural relics official told the paper that some sections slope at 60 to 70 degrees and have no safety facilities, so visitors can get lost or fall. Climbing them also damages the monument itself. Stick to the open, managed sections.

## Before you go

- Book Mutianyu up to 15 days ahead with the exact passport you will carry on the day; one passport, one ticket per day.
- According to the FCDO at the time of writing (November 2026), UK nationals can enter China visa-free for up to 30 days for tourism until 31 December 2026. Check the rules for your passport and dates.
- Avoid Chinese public holidays if you can; Badaling in particular gets very crowded.
- Budget separately for shuttle buses, cable cars and slides, which are not included in wall tickets.
- Check the weather on the morning of your visit, since sections can close at short notice.
- Wear grippy shoes and carry water: the steps are uneven and steep.
