Beyond the towers of Dubai and Abu Dhabi, the United Arab Emirates is desert country, warm and sunny in winter, hot and humid in summer. A few things are worth planning around: the season, the dates of Ramadan, laws on dress and behaviour that are stricter than in the UK, and the basics of desert safety. Check the current regional security picture before you book, too.

## Check the regional situation first

At the time of writing (November 2026), the UK Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office (FCDO) describes the situation in the Middle East as deeply unpredictable, with a high risk of sudden escalation since conflict began in early 2026. It says there have been strikes and retaliatory attacks across the region, and that travellers should be prepared for flight cancellations, airspace closures and other disruption.

The FCDO advises signing up for its travel advice email alerts, following local authorities' instructions, keeping travel documents up to date and keeping departure plans under review. If you are told to take shelter, stay indoors, and do not approach or touch any drone or missile fragments. Make sure your travel insurance covers your trip under current advice.

## Heat: choosing your season

The UAE government's own fact sheet sums up the climate simply: a desert climate, warm and sunny in winter, hot and humid in summer, and generally cooler in the eastern mountains. In practice that means:

- **Winter** is the warm, sunny season, the time many people choose for walking, outdoor sightseeing and desert trips.
- **Summer** is hot and humid; plan outdoor activities around the hottest part of the day.
- **Heavy rain can flood.** The FCDO notes that heavy rain can cause flooding and recommends checking forecasts from the National Centre of Meteorology.

Sun protection matters all year. TravelHealthPro, the UK's National Travel Health Network and Centre service, advises limiting sun exposure between 11am and 3pm, using sunscreen of SPF 30 or above with a 4 or 5-star UVA rating, and covering up with shade, clothing, hats and sunglasses. It also notes that sand reflects UV radiation, raising the risk of sunburn, which is worth remembering on beaches and dunes.

## Ramadan: how it changes your trip

Ramadan is the ninth month of the Islamic calendar. The UAE government portal explains that capable Muslims fast from dawn to dusk, that the month begins with the sighting of the new crescent moon, and that it lasts 29 or 30 days. Because the Islamic year has 354 days, Ramadan arrives about 11 days earlier each year. The UAE follows an official announcement from its moon-sighting committee.

Evenings centre on *iftar*, the meal that breaks the fast, traditionally begun with dates and laban (buttermilk), and in many places the firing of the iftar cannon signals sunset. But the FCDO says it may cause offence if you eat, drink, smoke or chew gum in public during the day, including in your car. It also advises:

- checking opening hours of shops and restaurants;
- expecting hotels and restaurants that serve food during fasting hours to screen you from fasting guests;
- following local dress codes with extra care;
- watching for erratic driving around dusk, when people rush home;
- being patient, as fasting can cause tiredness, especially late in the afternoon.

## Dress and behaviour: the laws to know

The FCDO stresses that some things that are legal in the UK are offences in the UAE, with serious penalties. Key points from its advice:

- **Dress:** dress modestly in public places such as shopping malls. Women should cover shoulders and knees, and underwear should not be visible. Avoid transparent clothing or clothes with slogans or images that could offend. Swimwear is for beaches and pools only.
- **Public behaviour:** showing affection in public is frowned upon, and you can be arrested for kissing in public. Swearing and rude gestures, including online, are illegal.
- **Alcohol:** being drunk or drinking in a public place is illegal everywhere in the UAE. The legal drinking age is 21 where alcohol is permitted, and alcohol is illegal in the emirate of Sharjah.
- **Photography:** do not photograph people without permission, government buildings or military sites.
- **Medicines:** some UK prescription and over-the-counter medicines are controlled in the UAE. The FCDO's health page says you must get approval in advance from the UAE authorities to bring them.
- **Drugs:** there is zero tolerance, and traces in the bloodstream count as possession.

## Desert safety

If your trip includes desert driving or quad biking, note that the FCDO warns that desert excursions can be dangerous unless you are in a properly equipped four-wheel-drive vehicle. If you drive yourself, travel with other cars, carry water and a mobile phone, and leave a copy of your plans with someone. If you join a desert driving or quad-biking tour, the FCDO recommends a company with adequate health and safety procedures, and checking that your insurance covers the activity. On boat trips, make sure there are life jackets for everyone.

## Before you go

- Read the latest FCDO advice for the UAE and sign up for email alerts, given regional tensions.
- Check whether your dates fall in Ramadan, and how that affects your plans.
- Apply for approval for any medicines you carry before you travel.
- Pack clothing that covers shoulders and knees for malls and public places.
- Ask desert tour providers about vehicles, safety briefings, water and insurance.
- Plan outdoor activities early or late in the day, and use strong sun protection.
