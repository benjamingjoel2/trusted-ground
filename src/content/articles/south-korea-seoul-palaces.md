Seoul's Joseon-era palaces sit in the middle of a modern city: tiled roofs and gates framed by mountains, guards in royal uniform at the gate, and lanes of traditional hanok houses between them. They are easy to visit, but each site has its own closing day, some areas need timed tickets, and Bukchon Hanok Village now restricts visitor hours. Check the day of the week before you set out.

## Gyeongbokgung: the main palace

Gyeongbokgung "was the first and largest of the royal palaces built during the Joseon Dynasty", according to Visit Seoul. It was built in 1395 and served as the dynasty's main palace. The National Palace Museum of Korea and the National Folk Museum of Korea are both on its grounds.

At the time of writing (November 2026), Visit Seoul and the Korea Heritage Service give these details:

- **Closed on Tuesdays.** If a public holiday falls on a Tuesday, the palace opens, and the first non-holiday after it becomes the closing day.
- **Seasonal hours.** It opens 09:00 to 17:00 in January, February, November and December, and 09:00 to 18:00 from March to May and in September and October. From June to August it opens 09:00 to 18:30. Last entry is one hour before closing.
- **Admission.** Visit Seoul lists KRW 3,000 for foreign adults aged 19 to 64.
- **Hanbok.** You can enter free "if you wear a complete hanbok", subject to the palace's hanbok guidelines.
- **Guided tours.** Free English tours run daily at 11:00, 13:30 and 15:30, except Tuesdays.

## The changing of the guard

Royal guards once opened and closed the palace gates, inspected visitors and kept watch. Visit Seoul says the ceremony was re-enacted from 1996 after historical research. Guards wear royal uniforms, carry traditional weapons and play traditional instruments.

According to Visit Seoul, the schedule is:

- **Changing of the Royal Guard:** 10:00 and 14:00, in front of Gwanghwamun, the main gate. Not on Tuesdays.
- **Gwanghwamun Gate guard-on-duty ceremony:** 11:00 and 13:00.
- **Gatekeeper military training:** 09:30 and 13:30, lasting 15 minutes.

The weather can change things. In heavy rain or snow, the ceremony may be cancelled. On winter days of −10°C or lower, or summer days of 30°C or higher, the ceremony continues in full but fewer guards may stand watch.

## Changdeokgung and its Secret Garden

Changdeokgung was built by King Taejong in 1405 and rebuilt in 1610 after the Imjin War, says Visit Seoul. It then served as a royal palace for about 270 years and is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Visit Seoul lists admission at KRW 3,000 for foreign adults aged 19 to 64. The palace is closed on Mondays.

The highlight is Huwon, the Secret Garden, which takes up 60% of the palace grounds. At its centre is the Buyongji pond with its pavilions. Visits are restricted. The Korea Heritage Service says Huwon has fixed entry times and capacity limits:

- English entries at 10:30, 11:30 and 14:30, plus 15:30 from March to November.
- Each language session is capped at 100 people: 50 tickets online and 50 on site.

Book the garden through the palaces' official reservation service if your dates are fixed.

## Jongmyo, Deoksugung and Changgyeonggung

- **Jongmyo Shrine** is closed on Tuesdays. On Mondays, Thursdays and Fridays, visits are by timed guided tour, lasting about 50 minutes. English tours start at 10:00, 12:00, 14:00 and 16:00. On Saturdays, Sundays, public holidays and Wednesdays (Culture Day), you can visit freely.
- **Deoksugung and Changgyeonggung** are closed on Mondays and open late, from 09:00 to 21:00 with last entry at 20:00, according to the Korea Heritage Service.

## Bukchon: a village where people live

Bukchon Hanok Village lies between Gyeongbokgung and Changdeokgung. Visit Seoul stresses that, unlike some hanok villages, Bukchon "was not created for tourists". It is a residential neighbourhood where people live.

Parts of Bukchon are now a Special Management Area, or "Red Zone". Visitor hours are 10:00 to 17:00. From 17:00 to 10:00 the next day, visitors may not enter the restricted areas. Visit Seoul asks visitors to practise "silent tourism":

- Keep noise down, especially around 31 Gahoe-dong.
- Do not litter.
- Keep groups to a maximum of 10.
- Do not use microphones or loudspeakers.
- Do not photograph or film inside houses, even if the doors are open.

## Seasons and paperwork

Opening hours change through the year, so check them for your month. The UK Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office (FCDO) notes heavy summer rain, usually in June and July, and a typhoon season that normally runs from June to November. Both can disrupt outdoor plans.

On entry, at the time of writing the FCDO says British nationals travelling visa-free are temporarily exempt from the K-ETA travel authorisation until 31 December 2026. An e-Arrival card can be submitted up to 72 hours before arrival, free of charge. Other nationalities should check Korea's official K-ETA site, and the FCDO warns that other sites offering K-ETA applications "are generally scams".

## Before you go

- Match palaces to days: Gyeongbokgung and Jongmyo close on Tuesdays, and Changdeokgung, Deoksugung and Changgyeonggung close on Mondays.
- Book the Secret Garden at Changdeokgung in advance if you can.
- Arrive at Gwanghwamun before 10:00 or 14:00 for the changing of the guard.
- If you wear hanbok for free entry, check the palace's guidelines on what counts as complete.
- Visit Bukchon between 10:00 and 17:00, quietly, and never photograph inside homes.
- Check K-ETA and e-Arrival requirements before you fly, using official sites only.
