Three cities carry most of Rajasthan's royal story: Jaipur with its pink-washed old town and the sandstone walls of Amber Fort, Jodhpur under the cliff-top bulk of Mehrangarh, and Udaipur, whose City Palace rises from the shore of Lake Pichola. All three are best seen in the cooler months, roughly November to February, when the official climate tables show daytime highs far below the 40°C-plus of April to June.

## Jaipur: the planned Pink City and Amber Fort

According to India's Ministry of Tourism, Jaipur was established in 1727 by the Kachhwaha Rajput ruler Jai Singh II, who gave the city his name. It is believed to be India's first planned city of the Common Era, laid out by Vidyadhar Bhattacharya on Vastu Shastra principles in nine blocks. The pink came later: in 1876 Maharaja Ram Singh had the city painted to welcome the Prince of Wales, pink being a colour of hospitality, and buildings in the old city are still required to be painted pink.

Inside the city, the main sights are:

- **Hawa Mahal**, the Palace of Winds, built in 1799 and known for its latticework and 953 windows.
- **City Palace**, which blends Rajasthani and Mughal architecture.
- **Jantar Mantar**, a UNESCO World Heritage Site with 19 astronomical instruments built between 1728 and 1734, including what the ministry calls the world's largest stone sundial.
- **Jal Mahal**, a palace in Man Sagar Lake that can be seen but not visited.

Amber (or Amer) Fort sits on a hill about 11km from the centre. Maharaja Man Singh I commissioned it in 1592 in pale yellow and pink sandstone, and it is part of a UNESCO World Heritage Site. It is arranged around four courtyards, entered through the Suraj Pol gate. Look for the Diwan-e-Aam, where the ruler met his subjects; the Sukh Niwas, cooled by water channels; and the Sheesh Mahal, the palace of mirrors, whose walls and ceilings are set with thousands of small mirrors. At the time of writing (November 2026) Incredible India lists opening hours of 9am to 6pm, with an evening light and sound show in English and Hindi.

## Jodhpur: Mehrangarh and the Blue City

Jodhpur was founded in 1459 by Rao Jodha, chief of the Rathore clan, and was the capital of the Marwar kingdom. The Mehrangarh Museum Trust, which runs the fort, says Rao Jodha began building on a rock about six miles north of Mandore, the clan's earlier capital, and that the town grew up at the fort's base. The name means "fort of the sun", a nod to the Rathores' claimed descent from the sun god Surya, and the fort has been the clan's seat for more than five centuries. Today Maharaja Gaj Singh II, head of the clan, preserves it and runs its museum.

Inside are the Moti Mahal, Phool Mahal and Sheesh Mahal, plus the Chamunda Mata temple. Below, many houses in the old city are painted blue, which gives Jodhpur its nickname. Nearby, Jaswant Thada is a 19th-century white marble memorial to Maharaja Jaswant Singh II, and Umaid Bhawan Palace, built in the 20th century, is partly a heritage hotel and partly a museum and royal residence.

The trust lists the Mehrangarh Museum and Jaswant Thada as open daily from 9am to 5pm. The Rao Jodha Desert Rock Park, below the walls, opens from 8am to 5.30pm between October and March and from 7am to 6.30pm between April and September. Tickets can be booked through the trust's website.

## Udaipur: lakes and the City Palace

Udaipur, often called the City of Lakes, was founded in the mid-16th century by Maharana Udai Singh II as a refuge for the Mewar dynasty. The City Palace was commissioned by Udai Singh II and expanded and decorated by his successors over the following centuries. It stands on the shore of Lake Pichola, which the Ministry of Tourism dates to 1362. Its museum displays paintings, tapestries, chandeliers and carved furniture, and Incredible India lists opening hours of 9.30am to 5.30pm at the time of writing.

From the water you can see both the City Palace and Jag Mandir, a three-storey marble and sandstone palace on the lake's southern island, with life-sized marble elephants and gardens. The ministry suggests a sunset boat ride and the palace's light and sound show.

## When to go

Incredible India publishes monthly temperature ranges for each city, and they tell a clear story:

- **Jaipur** reaches highs of 42°C to 45°C between April and June, while January lows fall to about 3.5°C.
- **Jodhpur** ranges from about 5°C to 30°C in January but up to 45°C in May.
- **Udaipur** is a little milder, from about 3°C to 30°C in January to around 43°C in May. Its tourism page recommends visiting between September and March.

The UK Foreign Office notes that India's monsoon usually runs from June to November and can bring flash flooding, road closures and disrupted services. Udaipur's Monsoon Palace is often wrapped in mist in the rains, but for long fort visits on foot, the months from November to February are the most comfortable. They are also the busiest, so book rooms and trains early.

## Staying safe and comfortable

The FCDO warns that pickpockets, scammers and ticket touts target foreign visitors in tourist areas and at railway stations, so buy tickets at official counters or through official websites. It also advises extra care around large crowds and religious gatherings, and around national holidays such as Republic Day (26 January), Independence Day (15 August), Diwali and Eid. Women travellers should read the FCDO's advice on harassment before travelling.

## Before you go

- Check current opening hours and ticket prices on the official sites before each visit; Mehrangarh's trust sells tickets online.
- Start early, when the forts open: from April onwards, daytime highs climb above 40°C.
- Plan the circuit for November to February if you can, and book ahead for that busy season.
- Allow time for the evening light and sound shows at Amber Fort and the City Palace.
- Buy tickets only at official counters or websites, not from touts.
- Read the FCDO's India advice for monsoon disruption and personal safety.
