Every visitor to Banff and Jasper national parks needs a valid Parks Canada pass, including anyone simply driving the Icefields Parkway between them. Moraine Lake cannot be reached by private car, so plan on a reserved Parks Canada shuttle or another authorised option. And remember that, on the Parkway, Parks Canada says winter weather lasts from early November to mid-June.

## A road through the ice

The Icefields Parkway (Highway 93N) runs 232 km from Lake Louise in Banff National Park to the Jasper townsite. Parks Canada calls it a remote road with limited services and no cell coverage, passing some of the most dramatic landscapes in the Canadian Rockies. Stops include Bow Lake, the Peyto Lake viewpoint near Bow Summit, the Columbia Icefield and Athabasca Falls. Bow Summit is the highest point on the road, at 2,070 m.

It is a drive for unhurried days and early starts. In summer, Parks Canada warns, car parks can fill early and stay full all day, with nowhere to wait for a space, so keep a back-up plan for every stop.

## Which park pass you need

Parks Canada says a valid national park entry pass is required to drive the Icefields Parkway. You have two main choices:

- **Daily admission.** According to Jasper National Park, a day pass covers entry to Jasper, Banff, Kootenay, Yoho, Mount Revelstoke, Glacier, Waterton Lakes and Elk Island national parks. It is valid until 4pm on the day after you buy it. At the time of writing (November 2026), Banff lists the daily fee at CAD 12.25 per adult, CAD 10.75 per senior (65 and over) and CAD 24.50 for a family or group of up to seven people arriving in one vehicle.
- **Parks Canada Discovery Pass.** This covers admission to more than 80 Parks Canada destinations for 12 months. Banff lists it at CAD 83.50 per adult, CAD 71.50 per senior and CAD 167.50 per family or group. For a week or more in the mountains, compare the cost with that of daily passes.

Admission is free for young people aged 17 and under. Passes are sold online, at park gates, at information centres and, in Jasper, at automated kiosks. Parks Canada's free admission under the Canada Strong Pass ended on 7 September 2026, and regular fees have applied since 8 September.

## Lake Louise and Moraine Lake

These two lakes are among the busiest places in the Rockies, and Parks Canada says planning ahead is essential.

- **Moraine Lake Road is closed to personal vehicles all year.** The only ways in are Parks Canada shuttles, licensed commercial operators and, for registered guests, Moraine Lake Lodge.
- **Shuttles need a reservation.** For 2026, reservations opened on 15 April, with 40% of seats released that day. The other 60% go on sale at 8am MDT two days before each departure. Parks Canada advises against trying to get a seat on the day.
- **Season and cost.** In 2026, the Lake Louise Lakeshore shuttle ran from 15 May to 12 October and Moraine Lake from 1 June to 12 October, weather permitting. Banff lists shuttle fares at CAD 12.75 per adult, CAD 6 per senior and CAD 4 per youth, plus a non-refundable reservation fee of CAD 3.50 online or CAD 5.50 by phone.
- **How it works.** Check in at the Lake Louise Park and Ride during your one-hour departure window. Parking there is free with a reservation, and the ticket includes the Lake Connector shuttle between the two lakes. Expect to wait at least 30 minutes for a shuttle at busy times.

You can drive to Lake Louise itself, but Parks Canada says you are unlikely to find a space and that parking must be paid for from May to October.

## Seasons on the Parkway

The Parkway changes completely through the year, and Parks Canada sets out what to expect.

- **Summer (roughly May to mid-October).** Services on the Parkway are open from early May to mid-October. The only petrol station is at Saskatchewan River Crossing, and it opens in summer only. Lakes usually thaw by late June, and alpine trails are generally free of snow by mid-July. Even in summer, overnight temperatures near freezing are common.
- **Winter (early November to mid-June).** There are no services between Lake Louise and Jasper: no fuel, hotels, restaurants or phone signal. From 1 November to 31 March, or whenever the road is covered in snow and ice, you must have snow tyres (marked with a snowflake or "M+S") or carry chains. The road can close for extreme weather or avalanche control, and you must not stop in the 38 marked avalanche paths.
- **Shoulder seasons.** Snow can fall at any time of year, and conditions can vary from one stretch of the road to the next. Ploughing and road maintenance take place from 7am to 3.30pm, October to May.

Before setting out, check road conditions on 511 Alberta and the weather forecast for Banff, Jasper and Saskatchewan River Crossing. From May to early October there is limited mobile coverage at the Columbia Icefield, and none anywhere else on the Parkway.

## Rules worth knowing

- Recreational drone use is prohibited in all national parks. Parks Canada says flying without a permit can bring fines of up to CAD 25,000.
- Area closures and wildlife restrictions are legally binding. Check each park's bulletins before you travel.
- Commercial trucks are not allowed on the Parkway except by permit.

## Before you go

- Buy a daily pass or Discovery Pass before you reach the Icefields Parkway. Check current fees on the Parks Canada website, because they change.
- If you want to see Moraine Lake, book a shuttle seat on the day reservations open or on the two-days-ahead release.
- Fill up with fuel and carry water and food before you leave Lake Louise or Jasper.
- Download maps and trip information in advance. There is almost no mobile signal on the Parkway.
- Between November and March, confirm that your hire car has snow tyres marked with a snowflake or M+S.
- Check 511 Alberta and the Parks Canada bulletins on the morning you drive.
