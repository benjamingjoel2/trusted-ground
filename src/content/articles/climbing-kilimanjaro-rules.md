You cannot climb Kilimanjaro independently. Tanzania National Parks (TANAPA) requires every climb to be guided by a licensed operator, and solo trekking is not permitted. Your choice of operator, route and number of days therefore shapes almost everything about the climb, from how you acclimatise to what you pay in park fees.

## The mountain and the park

Kilimanjaro National Park is managed by TANAPA. According to TANAPA, the mountain is made up of two dormant volcanoes, Kibo (5,895 m) and Mawenzi (5,149 m), and one extinct volcano, Shira (3,962 m). Uhuru Peak on Kibo is the summit. The park covers about 1,712 km². It was declared a national park in 1973, opened to tourism in 1977 and listed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1987.

The climb passes through very different zones, from montane forest through moorland to alpine desert and the glaciers near the summit.

## Rules set by the park

TANAPA's visitor guide sets out the main rules:

- **A licensed operator is required.** All climbs must be guided by a licensed operator, and guide and porter services are arranged through licensed tour operators.
- **No solo trekking.** You must not hike alone.
- **Stay on the routes.** Keep to marked routes, follow camp rules and the instructions of rangers and guides, and practise Leave No Trace.
- **Restrictions.** Off-trail travel and open fires are restricted, and drones need prior authorisation.
- **Special permits.** TANAPA lists camping in Kibo's crater (Crater Camp) as an activity that needs a special permit.

Because the licence sits with the operator, check that the company you pay is licensed, not just an agent reselling someone else's climb. Our guide to licences explains what to look for.

## Routes and itinerary length

According to TANAPA, six trails lead to the summit, each with its own scenery and challenges, and the climb to Kibo takes five to eight days depending on the route. TANAPA notes that more days give you a better chance of reaching the summit.

The visitor guide names the main gates as Marangu, Machame, Lemosho (via Londorossi) and Rongai in the north. Mweka Gate is used for the descent. Accommodation also depends on the route:

- The **Marangu route** uses alpine huts at Mandara, Horombo and Kibo.
- **Other routes** use designated tented camps run by licensed operators.

Most climbers fly into Kilimanjaro International Airport or Arusha Airport, and operators provide transfers to the gates. Moshi and Marangu have hotels and lodges for the nights before and after the climb.

## Fees: what you pay the park

TANAPA says park fees depend on your visitor category (citizen, resident or non-resident), your route and the number of days. Conservation, camping or hut, and rescue fees may apply. These are normally built into an operator's package price.

TANAPA publishes its fees on its tariff page and advises climbers to confirm the current schedule before travelling. At the time of writing (November 2026), that page still showed the 2023/2024 tariff, so ask your operator for an itemised breakdown of park fees in writing. Then you can see how much of your price goes to the park and how much covers the operator's services, crew and food.

## Altitude and safety

Altitude is the main risk. TANAPA advises choosing an itinerary with acclimatisation days, climbing slowly, drinking plenty and reporting any symptoms to your guide promptly. It also recommends proper cold-weather and rain gear, a head torch and any personal medication. The UK Foreign, Commonwealth & Development Office (FCDO) warns that altitude sickness is a risk in parts of Tanzania and that medical facilities are limited outside Dar es Salaam.

The FCDO also notes that wildfires can occur in hot, dry periods, including in Kilimanjaro National Park. If one affects you, contact your tour operator and follow the instructions of local authorities. Make sure your travel insurance covers trekking at the altitude you will reach. ## Questions to ask your operator

- Are you licensed to run Kilimanjaro climbs yourselves, or will another company operate my climb?
- Which route and how many days do you recommend for me, and where are the acclimatisation days?
- Can you give me an itemised list of the park fees in my price, by category, route and number of days?
- How many guides and porters will there be, and how are they paid and equipped?
- What is your procedure if I develop altitude sickness, and how would I be brought down or evacuated?
