Most visitors to Bhutan pay a Sustainable Development Fee (SDF) for every night they spend in the country, on top of a one-off visa fee. You no longer have to book through a tour operator, but you must be accompanied by a certified guide at all times, and treks must be run by an accredited operator or guide. In practice, that is why most trips are still arranged through a licensed Bhutanese operator.

## What the Sustainable Development Fee is

Bhutan's Department of Tourism describes the SDF as "a daily levy paid by visitors to support Bhutan's development". According to the department, it helps fund free healthcare and education, tourism training, infrastructure, environmental and cultural conservation and local businesses, and supports the country's forest cover and carbon neutrality.

At the time of writing (November 2026), the Department of Tourism gives these rates:

- **Adults:** USD 100 per night, for visitors from all countries except India.
- **Children aged 6 to 11** (that is, from their sixth birthday until they turn 12): USD 50 per night.
- **Children under 6:** no SDF.

Visitors from India pay under separate arrangements; check the official site if that applies to you. The rate has changed before, so confirm it on [bhutan.travel](https://www.bhutan.travel/visa) before you budget. The SDF is a government levy. It is separate from what you pay for hotels, transport, meals and guiding.

## How the visa works

Every visitor needs a visa to enter and leave Bhutan, according to the UK Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office (FCDO). The key points from the Department of Tourism and the FCDO:

- **Fee:** a one-off, non-refundable processing fee of USD 40, paid in addition to the SDF.
- **Who applies:** you can apply online through the official immigration portal, or a Bhutanese tour operator or hotel can apply for you.
- **Paying the SDF:** the SDF for your stay is paid during the online visa application, together with the USD 40 fee. The department warns that some international banks block card payments to Bhutan, so tell your bank before you pay.
- **Timing:** the department says applications are reviewed within five working days, so leave time before you fly.
- **Passport:** the FCDO says UK passport holders need a passport with an expiry date at least six months after the date they plan to leave Bhutan.
- **Extensions and refunds:** extensions can be paid through the online portal or in person at the Department of Immigration. SDF refund requests are made online through the visa portal.

Travel insurance is no longer mandatory. According to the Department of Tourism, the requirement was lifted on 23 April 2024, but the department says it is "strongly recommended" that all guests have it. See our guide to [travel insurance](/guide/insurance/) for what to check.

## Guides, operators and permits

This is where Bhutan differs most from other destinations. The Department of Tourism's FAQs say:

- "It is mandatory to be accompanied by a guide at all times in Bhutan." Guides must be certified by the Department of Tourism.
- Booking through a tour operator is no longer required. Before the September 2022 reopening, all visitors had to book through an accredited operator. Now you can arrange flights and hotels yourself.
- "All treks must be undertaken with an accredited tour operator or guide."
- You may only stay in accommodation or campsites approved by the Department of Tourism.
- Route permits are no longer needed for tourists to move around the country, but entering national parks requires a separate permit, which can be applied for online.

Put together, an independent trip still means hiring a certified guide, using approved accommodation and arranging your own transport between valleys. A licensed operator usually bundles all of this, applies for the visa and collects the SDF for you. If you go that way, check that the company is accredited by the Department of Tourism. Our page on [operator licences](/guide/licences/) explains what to look for, and our [Bhutan travel guide](/countries/bhutan/) covers the wider trip.

## Reading a quote from an operator

Because the SDF is charged per night, it can make up a large share of the cost of a trip. When you compare quotes:

- Check that the SDF is shown as a separate line, at the correct rate for each traveller's age.
- Check that the USD 40 visa fee is included or clearly listed.
- Confirm that the guide, approved hotels, internal transport and any national park permits are included, and say so in writing.
- Ask how payments are handled and what happens to the SDF if your dates change. Our guide to [deposits and payment protection](/guide/deposits-and-payment-protection/) covers what to ask before you send money.

## Questions to ask your operator

- Is your company accredited by Bhutan's Department of Tourism, and is our guide certified by the department?
- Does your quote show the SDF separately for each traveller and each night, at the current official rate?
- Will you apply for our visas, and when will we receive the approval?
- Are all our hotels and campsites on the Department of Tourism's approved list?
- Does our itinerary enter any national parks, and will you arrange those permits?
- If we shorten or cancel the trip, how will SDF refunds be requested and returned to us?
