As the light fades on a late-November afternoon, German town squares fill with wooden stalls, strings of lamps and the smell of mulled wine, roasted almonds and gingerbread. The short answer: most markets open in late November, around the start of Advent, and run until just before Christmas. There is no single national opening date, so check each city's own dates before you book.

## When the markets open

The German National Tourist Board describes the markets as starting to open in late November and running through Advent, a season Germany marks for more than four weeks. In 2026 the first Sunday of Advent falls on 29 November, so many markets open in the days around it.

Two of the most famous markets have published their 2026 dates:

- **Nuremberg Christkindlesmarkt:** 27 November to 24 December 2026, open Monday to Sunday from 10am to 9pm, according to the City of Nuremberg. The opening ceremony is on 27 November at 5.30pm.
- **Dresden Striezelmarkt:** 25 November to 24 December 2026, according to the City of Dresden. It opens from 4pm to 9pm on the first evening, then daily from 10am to 9pm until 23 December, and from 10am to 2pm on Christmas Eve.

Markets usually close on or before Christmas Eve. Dates and hours differ from city to city and from year to year, so treat the official market or city website as the final word.

## Nuremberg and Dresden: two classics

Nuremberg turns its opening into theatre. Germany Travel describes how, on the Friday of the first Advent weekend, the main square goes dark and a spotlight picks out the Christkind, the Christ Child, on the balcony of the Frauenkirche, who invites everyone to the market.

Dresden claims the longest pedigree. The tourist board calls the Striezelmarkt the oldest German Christmas market, dating to 1434, and names it after the Striezel pastry linked to Dresden Christstollen. The city says the market fills the Altmarkt square with more than 200 traders, show workshops for Erzgebirge crafts and a daily cultural programme. Its centrepiece is a stepped Erzgebirge Christmas pyramid that the city describes as the world's largest, at 14.61 metres tall.

## Beyond the big names

Big cities often run many markets at once rather than one. Germany Travel says Berlin has more than 50 themed markets, including a children's market, a market in an old railway locomotive shed and an organic market at Kollwitzplatz. Hamburg has 17, among them "Santa Pauli" on the Reeperbahn, a waterfront market in HafenCity, markets around the Binnenalster lake and Christmas events on boats on the Alster and Elbe.

This makes a city break flexible: if one market is packed, another is usually a short walk or tram ride away.

## What to eat, drink and buy

According to Germany Travel, typical market treats include gingerbread, candy apples, Christmas stollen, hot chestnuts and roasted almonds, washed down with mulled wine. In Hamburg, grog takes the place of mulled wine and fish sandwiches stand in for bratwurst.

Several cities have their own seasonal speciality, which the tourist board highlights:

- Dresdner Christstollen, the fruit loaf from Dresden
- Nuremberg Lebkuchen, a spiced gingerbread
- Aachener Printen, a hard spiced biscuit from Aachen
- Lübeck marzipan

For gifts, look for wooden carvings from the Erzgebirge mountains, artistic glass baubles and handmade regional decorations, all of which Germany Travel names as market staples.

## Crowds and safety

Markets are busiest in the evenings and at weekends, especially close to Christmas. A weekday morning visit is calmer if your schedule allows it.

The UK Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office (FCDO) says terrorists are very likely to try to carry out attacks in Germany and lists crowded places and public gatherings, including markets, among possible targets. It advises taking sensible precautions against mugging, bag-snatching and pickpocketing, especially in crowded public places and at stations, and not leaving bags unattended. Read the current advice before you travel and follow any instructions from local authorities.

## Before you go

- Check each market's official dates and hours for 2026; they differ by city and some close early on Christmas Eve.
- Book accommodation early for Advent weekends in popular market cities.
- Plan a weekday or morning visit if you want to avoid the thickest crowds.
- Carry valuables securely and keep bags with you at all times.
- Dress for cold evenings: you will spend hours standing outdoors.
- Read the FCDO's Germany advice shortly before you leave.
