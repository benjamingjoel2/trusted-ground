Honey-coloured bastions drop straight into the Grand Harbour, and a short bus ride away stand limestone temples that were already ancient when the Knights of St John arrived. Malta and Gozo pack thousands of years of building into a small area, which makes them easy to explore in a few days. The one thing that needs real planning is the Ħal Saflieni Hypogeum: tickets are limited, so book it before anything else.

## Valletta: a city built by the Knights

Valletta is named after Grand Master Jean Parisot de la Valette, and construction began in 1566, according to Visit Malta. The Knights of St John built it as a "modern" planned city on the Mount Sceberras peninsula, between Marsamxett and Grand Harbour, and it was completed in about 15 years. Visit Malta describes it as a World Heritage City and as the country's capital and administrative and commercial centre.

What makes Valletta rewarding on foot is the density of detail. Beyond the bastions, forts, cathedral, churches and palaces, look out for the smaller things Visit Malta highlights: votive statues set into street corners, fountains and carved coats of arms on façades.

- Walk the line of the bastions for views across both harbours.
- Give yourself time in the street grid itself rather than rushing between headline sights.
- Churches are places of worship; covering shoulders and knees is a sensible courtesy even where no rule is posted.

## The megalithic temples

Malta's prehistoric temples are the islands' oldest monuments. Heritage Malta says Ħaġar Qim and Mnajdra date to the late Neolithic, are over 5,000 years old, and were designated UNESCO World Heritage Sites in 1992 as part of the "Megalithic Temples of Malta" serial inscription.

**Ħaġar Qim and Mnajdra (Qrendi, Malta)** share an archaeological park. Ħaġar Qim is built of globigerina limestone on a ridge; Mnajdra, about 500 metres away along a footpath, has three structures of globigerina and coralline limestone. Protective shelters were added over both in 2008 to slow weathering while other preservation methods are tested. According to Heritage Malta, the doorway of Mnajdra's South structure is aligned with sunrise at the spring and autumn equinoxes, and at the solstices sunlight lights a different decorated slab inside. The visitor centre, which displays one of the original low-relief carvings, is wheelchair accessible, but Mnajdra itself is not.

**Ġgantija (Xagħra, Gozo)** consists of two structures with separate entrances inside a shared outer wall, built roughly 3,800 to 2,000 BC according to Heritage Malta. Fragments of red ochre plaster suggest the interiors were once painted. Visits begin at an interpretation centre with Neolithic finds from Gozo, then follow an outdoor path to the temples; Heritage Malta suggests allowing about an hour. There is no café on site, but Xagħra's village square is a short walk away.

## The Ħal Saflieni Hypogeum

The Hypogeum in Paola is an underground burial complex cut into globigerina limestone across three levels, with the deepest room 10.6 metres below road level. Heritage Malta says it was in use between 4000 BC and 1500 BC, was discovered by a stonemason in 1902, and was inscribed on the World Heritage List in 1981.

Access is tightly controlled because visitors affect the carbon dioxide, temperature and humidity in its small chambers. Key rules, according to Heritage Malta at the time of writing (November 2026):

- Only 10 people are allowed inside at any one time.
- Heritage Malta "strongly recommend[s] booking early"; regular tickets are sold online and at Heritage Malta sites.
- A limited number of last-minute tickets go on sale one day before the visit, first come, first served, at a higher price, with a limit of four per buyer.
- Photography is not allowed, for conservation reasons.
- Infants aged 1 to 5 are not admitted.

Check the Heritage Malta page for current prices, opening hours and any planned closures before you build an itinerary around it.

## Getting to Gozo

Gozo is easily done as a day trip, though an overnight stay gives you a quieter evening. Visit Gozo lists two main options:

- **Regular ferry** from Ċirkewwa, at Malta's northern tip, to Mġarr Harbour. The crossing takes about 25 minutes, with sailings up to every 30 minutes during the day; the timetable varies by season. This is the route for cars.
- **Fast ferry** from Valletta's Grand Harbour to Mġarr, for foot passengers only. Visit Gozo advises checking in 15 to 20 minutes before departure.

Beyond Ġgantija, Visit Gozo highlights the Cittadella, Ta' Pinu Basilica, Dwejra and the coastal saltpans.

## Staying safe by the sea

Many visitors combine heritage with swimming, and the UK Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office (FCDO) is clear that drownings happen every year. It notes that some beaches have strong undercurrents and hidden rocks, that lifeguards and a flag system operate on most beaches in summer, and that red flags mean dangerous conditions. The FCDO also warns that bird hunting takes place in spring and autumn, often in unmarked areas that can overlap with walking routes, which is worth knowing if you plan countryside walks around the temples.

## Before you go

- Book the Hypogeum first, as early as you can, and fit the rest of your days around your slot.
- Check Heritage Malta's site for opening hours and one-off closure days at each temple.
- Check Gozo Channel or the fast ferry operator's timetable for the season you are travelling.
- Wear sturdy shoes: the path to Mnajdra and the outdoor route at Ġgantija are uneven in places.
- Pack modest clothing for church visits in Valletta and on Gozo.
- Read the latest FCDO advice, including entry requirements, before you travel.
